## What an AI Headshot Generator Actually Does

Every tool in this category works the same way at the core. You upload 10 to 30 photos of yourself, the service trains a small model on your face, and it renders synthetic portraits of you in different outfits, backgrounds, and lighting setups. The output is not a filter and not a retouch — it is a generated image of a face that looks like yours wearing clothes you never wore, standing in a room you never entered.

That distinction matters, because it explains both the upside and the failure modes. The upside is scale: one upload session produces 20 to 50 usable portraits for less than the cost of one studio sitting. The failure modes are the reason this category still has room for both AI tools and photographers. The three that show up in almost every review thread are uncanny skin texture, anatomy errors around teeth and fingers, and a subtle "averaged" look that appears when the model blends too many of your photos into one generic face. None of these are dealbreakers on a phone screen at 120 pixels tall, which is where 90% of LinkedIn photos actually get seen. They matter when a headshot gets printed, projected, or inspected by someone who knows what you look like.

The other thing worth knowing before you pay: the tool's marketing photos are always better than your results. Every provider shows off a model with symmetrical features, perfect lighting, and ten years of skincare. Your results depend on the raw material you upload. That is why the single biggest variable in this category is not the brand — it is the discipline you bring to the upload step, which I'll cover in the last section before the FAQ.

## How to Get a Result That Doesn't Look Fake

Every tool in this category returns better output when the input is boring, and the boring checklist is short. Upload 15 to 25 photos — not five. Use front-facing shots at eye level, plain backgrounds, and even lighting; a window-lit room beats a flash in the dark every time. Skip sunglasses, hats, and group photos, because the model will mix faces. Keep two or three consistent outfits across the batch so the renders actually look like a wardrobe, and shoot a mix of smiling and neutral expressions.

Then budget ten minutes to review the outputs with a critical eye. Reject anything where the teeth look fused, the ears are asymmetrical, or the skin texture reads as plastic — those are the tells that make a headshot read as AI to anyone who looks for more than two seconds. If you get one perfect photo out of forty, that is a successful run.

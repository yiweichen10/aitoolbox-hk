Durable vs Elementor

Both tools are in our catalog with hands-on reviews: Durable scores 4.7/5 and Elementor scores 4.3/5. Below is everything our testing surfaced — scores, pricing, strengths and weaknesses — without sponsorship from either side.

Durable

4.7/5 editor score
Free + Launch from $25/mo
  • ✓  It genuinely ships a working site in under a minute. Type what you do and it returns a structured page with copy, layout, and images. For freelancers selling local-business websites, that is a client deliverable you can show before the meeting ends.
  • ✓  The bundle is the real value: CRM, booking, invoicing, and an AI assistant that answers business questions are included in one subscription. You replace six tools (site builder, CRM, calendar, review tool, ad maker, copywriter) with one bill.
  • —  Design control is shallow. You cannot edit raw HTML, and the layouts follow Durable's templates. If a client wants a custom, brand-heavy site, you will hit a wall fast - Wix or Webflow give you more room.

Elementor

4.3/5 editor score
Free core · Pro from $60/yr
  • ✓  The AI sits inside the workflow instead of next to it: every text field has 'Write with AI', the image generator is one click from any image widget, and the AI CSS box takes a plain-English request like 'make this hover state fade in' and returns working code. For someone who builds sites weekly, that is minutes saved hundreds of times a year, not a demo trick
  • ✓  The client-economics math still works: a $60 Essential license covers itself inside the first hour of a $1,000+ local-business build, and the One plan's bundled image optimization, accessibility scanning and email deliverability replace two or three paid plugins agencies were already buying
  • —  The credit system is the worst part of the new pricing. Credits reset monthly with no rollover, complex AI actions eat 20-40 at a time, and hitting the ceiling mid-client-project means waiting or upgrading. Agencies that batch-generate page copy will burn 25,000 credits faster than Elementor's marketing suggests

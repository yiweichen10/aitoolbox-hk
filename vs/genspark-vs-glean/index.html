Genspark vs Glean

Both tools are in our catalog with hands-on reviews: Genspark scores 4.7/5 and Glean scores 4.4/5. Below is everything our testing surfaced — scores, pricing, strengths and weaknesses — without sponsorship from either side.

Genspark

4.7/5 editor score
Free daily credits + Plus ~$24/mo
  • ✓  Sparkpages are a genuinely different output format. Instead of a paragraph with links, you get a structured page - comparison tables, embedded videos at the right timestamps, images, citations - that you can scan top to bottom. For product comparisons and procurement research, this replaces twenty open browser tabs, and that is the whole point of the tool.
  • ✓  The multi-agent setup does real fact-checking. A query gets split across specialized agents (search, fact-checker, cross-referencer) working in parallel, and the cross-checking shows up in fewer confident hallucinations than a single-model chatbot. It is not perfect, but the difference is noticeable on topics where sources disagree.
  • —  The credit economy is complicated and punishing. Simple chat is cheap, but video generation, complex agent runs, and phone calls burn credits fast, and everything expires at the end of the month with no rollover. You can burn $20 worth of credits in a single ambitious afternoon and have nothing to show except a folder of drafts.

Glean

4.4/5 editor score
Custom enterprise pricing (~$15-$30/seat/mo)
  • ✓  Searches across 100+ SaaS apps with maintained connectors. Not RSS feeds that break next week — Glean's engineering team actually maintains the integrations. When Google changes the Drive API or Slack deprecates an endpoint, you do not get a panicked call from a client because search randomly stopped working. For an implementation consultant, this means fewer emergency maintenance calls at 9 PM.
  • ✓  Permission-aware search that respects access controls is the real enterprise unlock. An engineer cannot accidentally find HR's compensation spreadsheet through search. A contractor cannot see internal strategy docs. Every result is filtered through the user's existing permissions on the source system. Without this feature, no legal or compliance team signs off on deployment — and without legal sign-off, the deal does not close.
  • —  Pricing is deliberately opaque — Glean does not publish numbers, you have to sit through a demo and negotiate. From what I have gathered across client deployments, expect $15-$30 per seat per month for the full platform. A 200-person company is looking at $36,000-$72,000 a year. When the CFO sees that line item next to Slack and Google Workspace, you will be justifying ROI in a board meeting. Build the productivity math into your proposal upfront.

Elicit vs Scite

Both tools are in our catalog with hands-on reviews: Elicit scores 4.6/5 and Scite scores 4.5/5. Below is everything our testing surfaced — scores, pricing, strengths and weaknesses — without sponsorship from either side.

Elicit

4.6/5 editor score
Free + Pro from $49/user/mo
  • ✓  Sentence-level citations on everything - every claim links to the exact sentence in the source paper, which is the strongest anti-hallucination feature in the category and the reason I trust it in front of clients
  • ✓  The corpus is enormous for academic work: 138M+ papers and 545K clinical trials, so for evidence questions it beats general web search by a wide margin
  • —  It is an academic tool, full stop - no general web search, no news, no product data. Questions that live outside peer-reviewed literature get nothing useful back
Full Elicit review

Scite

4.5/5 editor score
From $12/mo · Pro $40/mo annual
  • ✓  The contrasting-citation filter is genuinely unique - it is the only mainstream tool that tells you who DISAGREES with a paper, and it surfaces disputed claims before you repeat them in something you get paid for
  • ✓  Reference Check on your own draft is a pre-submission smoke detector - it flagged two disputed sources in a client article that had already passed human review
  • —  No free tier worth the name - 7-day trial, then $144/year minimum, while Elicit and Semantic Scholar give away more for $0

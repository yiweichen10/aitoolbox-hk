Flowise vs Relevance AI

Both tools are in our catalog with hands-on reviews: Flowise scores 4.0/5 and Relevance AI scores 4.0/5. Below is everything our testing surfaced — scores, pricing, strengths and weaknesses — without sponsorship from either side.

Flowise

4.0/5 editor score
Free self-host + Cloud $25/mo
  • ✓  Self-hosted deployment means you own everything. I run Flowise on a $20/month VPS with Docker Compose, and it handles 5 client chatbots without breaking a sweat. No per-user fees, no API markup, no vendor lock-in. When I pitch businesses on 'your own AI chatbot, hosted on your own server, no data ever leaves your infrastructure,' they sign. The open-source license (Apache 2.0) means I can even white-label it if a client wants branded deployment
  • ✓  The visual canvas genuinely speeds up prototyping. I can build a working RAG chatbot — document loader → text splitter → Pinecone vector store → OpenAI chat model → conversational retrieval chain — in under 15 minutes. Last week a real estate client asked 'can you build a chatbot that answers questions about our 200 property listings?' I built the prototype during our Zoom call and had a signed $2,500 contract before we hung up
  • —  You need technical fundamentals to be dangerous. Sure, it is 'no-code,' but if you do not understand what a vector embedding is, why chunk size matters for RAG accuracy, or how temperature affects model output, you will build chatbots that hallucinate confidently. Two of my first three client projects failed because I did not understand retrieval parameters well enough. The drag-and-drop UI hides complexity — it does not remove it

Relevance AI

4.0/5 editor score
Free + Pro from $49/mo
  • ✓  The visual drag-and-drop workflow builder gets you from idea to working prototype in under an hour. You drag a 'Trigger' block, connect it to an 'LLM Call' block, route the output through a condition check, and send results to Slack or email. If you have ever tried building the same thing in Python with LangChain and FastAPI, you know that is normally a 3-day project. For client work, this speed is the difference between delivering 2 projects a month and delivering 8.
  • ✓  Multi-model support means you are not locked into OpenAI pricing. GPT-4o is great for complex reasoning but costs add up fast on high-volume agents. Claude 3.5 Sonnet handles most business logic at half the token cost. Gemini Flash works for classification tasks at a fraction of the price. I route different agent steps to different models based on complexity — retrieval to Gemini Flash, reasoning to Claude, generation to GPT-4o. This model arbitrage alone can cut a client's monthly API bill by 40-60% compared to running everything through OpenAI. That is a selling point clients actually care about.
  • —  The free tier is a glorified demo. You get limited workflow runs, restricted model access, and none of the team features. You cannot build a real client agent on the free plan — you need at least the Pro tier at $49/month. For a solo consultant, that is fine (it is a business expense), but if you are evaluating whether to learn the platform, the free tier does not give you enough to make an informed decision. You need to commit to at least one paid month to really understand what the platform can do.

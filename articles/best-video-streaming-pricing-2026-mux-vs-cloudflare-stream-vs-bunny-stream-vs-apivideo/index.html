Best Video Streaming Pricing 2026: Mux vs Cloudflare Stream vs Bunny Stream vs api.video

2026-11-19 · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 34 min read
TL;DR

The same 20,000-minute course library watched 200,000 minutes a month costs $35 a month on Bunny Stream, $160 on Mux, $300 on Cloudflare Stream, and $397 on api.video. Same videos, same audience, an 11x spread on the invoice. I rebuilt all four bills line by line from each provider's own rate card, checked November 19, 2026, because most comparisons still quote Mux's retired per-GB model and none of them mention the renditions trap buried in Bunny's storage bill.

Video streaming pricing comes down to one design decision: the meter. Mux and api.video read minutes, Cloudflare Stream reads minutes stored plus minutes delivered, and Bunny Stream reads gigabytes in and gigabytes out. Which meter wins depends on your catalog's bitrate, your audience's geography, and how many encoding ladders you leave switched on. The rest of this piece is the arithmetic behind those four invoices.

Mux pricing: resolution x quality, billed by the minute

Mux rebuilt its rate card around resolution and quality tiers, and older write-ups citing $0.0085/GB delivery are out of date. Today's Mux pricing has three dials. Input is free for Basic quality on-demand (all resolutions up to 4K), $0.03125/min for Plus at 1080p, and $0.046875/min for Premium. Storage runs $0.003/min/mo for 1080p ($0.0024 at 720p, $0.0096 at 4K), billed per stored minute per month. Delivery runs $0.001/min for 1080p ($0.0008 at 720p) after 100,000 free minutes every month. Assets nobody plays for 30 days drop to a 40% storage discount; after 90 days, 60%. Volume discounts are marginal per tier — they apply to minutes within each bracket, not retroactively to the whole month — and contract pricing starts above $3,000/month. The free plan caps storage at 10 videos; Pay-as-you-go includes a $20 monthly usage credit; prepay buys Launch at $20/mo for $100 of credit or Scale at $500/mo for $1,000.

Cloudflare Stream pricing: two meters, zero egress

Cloudflare Stream pricing is the shortest rate card in the industry: $5 per month for each 1,000 minutes of storage capacity (prepaid, in blocks) and $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered (post-paid). Ingress, encoding, and bandwidth are all included — there is no egress line anywhere on the bill. Two operational details matter. First, storage is a hard quota: run out and new uploads and live recordings stop until you buy another block. Second, delivery counts HTTP segment requests on four-second segments, so client-side preloading and buffering are billable, while playback served from the browser cache is not. Live video rides the same two meters, with recordings counting against storage, and WebRTC delivery started billing on October 15, 2026.

Bunny Stream pricing: GB in, GB out

Bunny Stream pricing bills storage at $0.01/GB/mo in the default Frankfurt region — on what encoding produces, not what you uploaded. Every enabled resolution is stored separately, and MP4 fallback, keep-original files, preview animations, and thumbnails stack on top. Bunny's own docs warn that "a library can easily hold several times the footage you sent it." Delivery follows their CDN table: $0.01/GB in Europe and North America on the Standard network (119 PoPs), $0.03/GB in Asia and Oceania, $0.045/GB in South America. The Volume network collapses everything to a single $0.005/GB worldwide rate, but runs on just 10 PoPs — a real performance tradeoff outside EU/NA. Standard encoding is free; Premium encoding costs $0.050/min for 720p and 1080p output and $0.150/min for 2K/4K. AI transcription runs $0.10 per language-minute, DRM licenses are billed separately, and the whole account carries a $1 monthly minimum.

api.video pricing: free encoding, per-minute everything else

api.video pricing is two per-minute meters: hosting from $0.00285/min stored and delivery from $0.0017/min delivered — both advertised floors with volume tiers above them, and their add-on pricing implies a $0.003/min list storage rate. Encoding is free at every quality from 240p to 4K, and delivery minutes are measured as duration times viewers times the percentage actually loaded: a 10-minute video watched halfway by 500 viewers bills 2,500 minutes, not 5,000. The free sandbox is generous for testing but useless for production — 30-second clips, watermarked, auto-deleted after 24 hours. Add-ons stack up too: custom domains from $60/mo, playback domain restrictions $20/mo, analytics retention $12-299/mo, AI transcription $0.10/min. One caveat worth checking before you commit: api.video now runs under OVHcloud, and iTechGuides' September 2026 fact-check reports new US legal-entity subscriptions have been unavailable since February 1, 2026. Confirm availability directly if you bill from the US.

Mux is billed at flat 1080p list rates; their calculator's adaptive mix and automatic cold storage both land lower in practice. api.video uses its advertised floor rates.

Per-Minute vs Per-GB: The Breakeven Bitrates

The two meters convert at your average delivery bitrate, and this conversion is the whole decision. At 3.33 Mbps, one delivered minute is about 0.025 GB, so Bunny's $0.005/GB Volume rate equals $0.125 per 1,000 delivered minutes — 8x cheaper than Cloudflare's $1 and Mux's $1 at 1080p. The crossover sits at 26.7 Mbps: below that average bitrate, Bunny Volume wins; above it, the per-minute meters flip ahead. Standard EU/NA rates at $0.01/GB break even at 13.3 Mbps. Real-world 4K at roughly 16 Mbps still favors Bunny (about $0.60 per 1,000 delivered minutes); premium 4K pushing 30 Mbps flips back to Cloudflare, whose flat $1 undercuts Bunny's $1.13. Mobile-heavy audiences watch mostly 480p-720p segments at 1-2.5 Mbps, which pushes the per-GB advantage to 10x or more — and against Mux's 720p delivery rate specifically, the Bunny Volume crossover sits near 21 Mbps. This is why any VOD hosting pricing discussion that ignores bitrate is worthless: the answer changes at 13, 21, and 27 Mbps, not at brand names.

Storage: Where Bunny Quietly Wins and Loses

Per 1,000 stored minutes per month, the list prices read: Bunny about $0.50 (2x renditions at 3.33 Mbps), Mux $3.00 at 1080p or $2.40 at 720p, api.video about $3.00, Cloudflare $5.00 flat. That makes Bunny 6-10x cheaper on paper — but the meter cuts both ways. Encode a 4K master with every resolution enabled, MP4 fallback on, and originals kept, and Bunny's stored GB can triple; Cloudflare and Mux ignore rendition counts entirely because they bill duration. Bunny gives you the levers — per-library resolution control and a cleanup endpoint for unconfigured resolutions — while Cloudflare gives you the ceiling: $5 per 1,000 minutes no matter how many codecs you encode. Mux sits in between with automatic cold storage: a mostly-unwatched library pays 60% less after 90 idle days, which nobody else matches. For video on demand hosting pricing, the honest rule is: bitrate-heavy catalogs with tight ladder control go to Bunny, long-tail libraries full of cold content go to Mux.

Live Streaming Pricing in November 2026

Live costs diverge more than on-demand. Cloudflare Stream is the flat-out winner on simplicity: live rides the same $5/1,000-min storage and $1/1,000-min delivery meters, a broadcast with zero viewers costs $0 in delivery, and WebRTC (GA, billed since October 15, 2026) covers sub-second latency use cases. Mux live is the expensive end: live input only supports Plus and Premium quality ($0.03125/min and up at 1080p), simulcasting to each target adds $0.020/min, and live captions run $0.024/min after 6,000 free monthly minutes. Bunny Stream Live is in closed preview as of August 13, 2026 — RTMP ingest, DVR, and VOD recording exist for testers, but there is no public rate card, so do not budget against it. api.video ships the fullest live suite (RTMPS and SRT ingest, simulcasting, DVR, 24/7 streams, 3-second latency) and bills live delivery as regular minutes. If live streaming pricing is your gating factor, Cloudflare's two meters are the only ones you can model in a spreadsheet today.

Choosing Between Them: Three Honest Calls

Small catalog, lean budget. Mux's 100,000 free delivery minutes and $2.40 720p storage are the cheapest way to ship a real product-video section; Cloudflare's prepaid storage floor makes tiny libraries disproportionately expensive ($5 minimum for anything under 1,000 minutes). Bunny wins raw dollars at $2.25-$3.50 if you can stomach per-GB variance.

Bandwidth-heavy, global audience. Bunny on the Volume network is the price-performance pick for big delivery, provided your audience sits in EU/NA on Standard rates or you accept 10 global PoPs. Watch Asia: $0.03/GB triples the Standard bill. My CDN pricing comparison covers the underlying network economics in depth.

Already inside Cloudflare. Stream's two meters, zero egress, and live/WebRTC support make it the no-spreadsheet option, and one invoice for the whole stack is worth a modest premium to a lot of teams.

Skip the marketing suites — Wistia, Vimeo Pro and friends — if you are wiring an API. They price per seat and per player, not per meter, and none of them will ever appear in the math above.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the cheapest video streaming hosting for a small site?

Bunny Stream, almost always. A 50 GB library serving 250 GB a month runs about $3 on Standard rates and $1.75 on Volume, against $5-6 at Mux (with delivery free), $18 at Cloudflare, and $19 at api.video. Bunny's $1 monthly minimum is the lowest commitment in the group. Mux is the only one with a meaningful free tier, but its 10-video cap makes that a trial rather than a home.

Mux vs Cloudflare Stream: which is cheaper at 1080p?

Mux, at every scale up to 1080p. It wins both meters: storage $3.00 vs $5.00 per 1,000 minutes, and delivery $0.80-1.00 per 1,000 minutes with the first 100,000 free against Cloudflare's flat $1.00. Cloudflare's edge appears at 4K: Mux multiplies base rates by 4 at 2160p — $3.20 per 1,000 delivered minutes and $9.60 stored — which blows past Cloudflare's resolution-blind flat rates. Mux's Premium quality tier narrows the gap again at 1.5x rates. The honest Mux vs Cloudflare Stream answer is that Cloudflare sells predictability, not price.

How much does video hosting cost per GB?

Convert the per-minute meters at your bitrate. At 3.33 Mbps, one delivered GB equals 40 billed minutes: about $0.04 at Cloudflare ($1 per 1,000 minutes), $0.04 at Mux 1080p, and $0.068 at api.video's floor. Bunny prices GB directly: $0.01/GB in EU/NA, $0.005/GB on the Volume network, $0.03/GB in Asia and Oceania. That is why bandwidth-heavy sites flee the per-minute meters — and why the same question about storage flips the story: Bunny bills every rendition GB at $0.01, while Cloudflare's $5 per 1,000 stored minutes works out near $0.20 per GB at 3.33 Mbps (Mux 1080p about $0.12) but stays fixed no matter how many codecs you encode.

Do any of these platforms have a real free tier?

Mux is the only production-usable one: 100,000 free delivery minutes every month plus $20 of credit on Pay-as-you-go, though the separate free plan caps storage at 10 videos. Bunny's 14-day trial and api.video's sandbox (30-second clips, watermarked, deleted after 24 hours) are evaluation tools, not hosting. Cloudflare Stream has no free allotment at all — storage is prepaid capacity, so a dormant library still costs $5 per 1,000 minutes monthly.

Which platform has the best live streaming pricing?

Cloudflare Stream, and it is not close for standard RTMP broadcast: live uses the same $1 per 1,000 delivered minutes and $5 per 1,000 stored minutes, with WebRTC delivery billed since October 15, 2026 for low-latency playback. Mux charges Plus or Premium live input ($0.03125/min and up at 1080p) plus $0.020/min per simulcast target. api.video includes a full live feature set and bills live delivery as regular minutes. Bunny Stream Live has no public pricing — it remains in closed preview as of August 2026.

Bottom Line: Match the Meter to Your Catalog

Video streaming pricing is not a leaderboard; it is a catalog audit. Read your average delivery bitrate (Cloudflare's Stream analytics and Bunny's per-video storage breakdown both expose it), count your renditions, and locate your audience. Then the choice is mechanical: bitrate under 13 Mbps with EU/NA traffic goes to Bunny; a 1080p catalog that Mux's free minutes can carry goes to Mux; one predictable invoice with live and zero egress built in goes to Cloudflare Stream; a live-heavy product with EU billing goes to api.video, after confirming US availability.

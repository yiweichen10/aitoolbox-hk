Best AI Search APIs in 2026: Tavily vs Exa vs Brave vs SerpApi — Pricing & ROI

2026-10-17 · AI Search · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 📖 35 min read
⚡ TL;DR
Compare Tavily, Exa, Brave Search API, and SerpApi on request pricing, retrieval style, privacy, result controls, and the cost of a useful answer.

At list price, Brave charges $5 per 1,000 Search requests, Exa Instant starts at $4 per 1,000 requests, Tavily’s $30 plan buys 4,000 credits, and SerpApi’s Starter plan costs $25 for 1,000 monthly searches. For buyers comparing AI search APIs, that spread looks decisive until you notice that the vendors count different things: a Tavily advanced query costs two credits, an Exa request can return more than 10 results for an added fee, and SerpApi sells monthly search capacity rather than a simple per-call balance.

This comparison uses the vendors’ public pricing and product documentation checked on October 17, 2026. It does not claim a winner from a one-off latency test. Search quality depends on the query, region, filters, and what your application calls a useful result. The practical question is whether you need agent-ready web context, semantic discovery, a first-party index, or Google-shaped result pages—and what your team must build around that data.

The table is a budget map, not a quality ranking. At 1,000 simple calls, list prices range from Exa Instant’s $4 to SerpApi Starter’s $25, but those are not equivalent outputs. Brave’s monthly credit can offset Search use; Tavily’s credit count changes with depth and endpoint; Exa prices results and page content separately. SerpApi’s plan includes a fixed allowance and throughput ceiling. Compare the bill after the application’s whole retrieval path, not the first number on a pricing page.

A second distinction matters: Tavily and Exa position their products around agent and semantic retrieval; Brave sells access to a search index it operates; SerpApi parses search-engine result pages into structured fields. Those are different data products. If your product depends on ranking position, the first two may be a poor substitute for SERP collection. If it needs a model to find conceptually related pages, a conventional ranking endpoint may force extra query rewriting and reranking work.

Tavily vs Exa pricing: credits or request-based billing?

Tavily is easiest to budget when a workflow uses several parts of its web-retrieval stack. The public free tier lists 1,000 credits a month with no card required. Its Project plan is $30 for 4,000 credits, or $0.0075 per credit; pay-as-you-go is $0.008. Basic search costs one credit and advanced search costs two. A thousand advanced searches therefore consume 2,000 credits, not 1,000. The startup and higher-volume plans reduce the listed cost per credit, while enterprise rates are custom.

That is only the search line. Basic extraction charges one credit per five successful URL extractions; advanced extraction charges two credits per five. Mapping costs one credit per ten pages, or two with instructions. A crawl combines mapping and extraction. The Research endpoint has dynamic limits: Tavily lists minimums of four credits for mini and 15 for pro, with maximums of 110 and 250 respectively. A research call can therefore cost far more than a search call. For a production budget, log endpoint, search depth, extracted pages, and research mode separately.

The reason to choose Tavily is operational: one account can search, extract pages, map a site, and run research. That reduces glue code when the product asks a question, fetches relevant pages, and passes their contents to a model. It does not mean every team should buy the full stack. If the workflow only needs ten blue links or a couple of snippets, paying for agent-oriented retrieval features may add cost without moving answer quality.

Read the Tavily API credit documentation before estimating. For the product category beyond APIs, our AI search tools comparison covers end-user research products; those are not interchangeable with developer endpoints.

Exa Search API pricing: pay for the query, then the content

Exa separates search from page retrieval. Its free tier provides $10 in monthly credits and a one-time $10 onboarding bonus; the monthly balance resets, and the page says no payment method is required to start. Instant Search costs $4 per 1,000 requests for up to 10 results. Fast and auto search cost $7 per 1,000, while deep search costs $12 and deep-reasoning costs $15 per 1,000 requests. Search results above ten are billed at $1 per 1,000 additional results.

The key cost detail is that search results are not the same as full page text. The Contents endpoint is $1 per 1,000 pages for each content type. If one page is returned as both text and highlights, it counts as two content types. AI-generated page summaries are another charge at $1 per 1,000 pages. A pipeline that requests 10 results, then fetches all ten pages in two formats, has a different bill from ten bare Instant searches, even before the model reads the material.

Exa’s product makes sense when semantic matching is central: “find pages about X” can be more useful than matching the literal words in X. It also provides people, company, and scholarly indexes. This can cut down on custom query expansion, but you still need to assess whether the result set is current, on-topic, and suitable for your audience. A polished highlight is not proof that the source is authoritative.

For a small agent, the Exa Search API pricing is unusually easy to test: use the free balance, cap results at ten, and add Contents only for URLs that pass a relevance threshold. Avoid fetching every result by default. On broad queries, page retrieval rather than search may become the larger line item. On high-volume systems, compare the per-query rate with the cost of storing and refreshing your own source set.

Brave Search API alternative to scraped Google results

Brave charges $5 per 1,000 requests for its Search plan and lists $5 in monthly credits. Search includes web data and LLM Context, with extra snippets and structured information; its wider API catalog includes news, images, videos, local results, and other specialized endpoints. Brave says its index contains more than 30 billion pages and receives more than 100 million page updates per day. Those are vendor-reported figures, not a substitute for testing coverage on your own queries.

The Answers plan is a different product: Brave lists $4 per 1,000 requests plus $5 per million input/output tokens. It returns a grounded answer with citations and supports streaming and the OpenAI SDK. Search is the better fit if you want to select your own model, prompt, citation format, and refusal rules. Answers can shorten implementation, but it moves part of the answer-generation process into the provider’s endpoint and adds a separate dependency to evaluate.

Brave’s strongest distinction is that it operates an independent web index rather than promising Google SERP parity. That can be useful when your application needs an alternative source, data freshness, or index independence. It is not a Google Search API substitute for tasks where the exact Google ranking, local pack, or Google-specific rich result is the thing being measured. The question is not “which index is objectively best?” but “which index returns acceptable evidence for the market and query types we serve?”

Privacy and retention need procurement review. Brave’s product pages advertise Zero Data Retention and SOC 2 Type II attestation, while its free-plan FAQ says a card is required as an anti-fraud check. Confirm the current contract terms, the endpoint covered, and any attribution conditions before launch. A marketing page is not your data-processing agreement.

SerpApi pricing per 1,000 searches: pay for SERP fidelity

SerpApi is a structured search-results service, not merely an agent search endpoint. It supports Google and many other engines, with parameters for location, language, device, and result type. That matters for rank tracking, local search audits, shopping results, or products that need a defined engine’s fields in predictable JSON. If the application only wants supporting sources for a conversational answer, that specificity may be overhead.

The published free plan allows 250 searches per month and 50 requests per hour. Starter is $25 per month for 1,000 searches and 200 per hour; Developer is $75 for 5,000 and 1,000 per hour; Production is $150 for 15,000 and 3,000 per hour. In simple package math, Starter is $25 per thousand included searches, Developer is $15, and Production is $10. These figures divide the monthly fee by the full quota; they do not imply unused calls roll over or that overage is included. Check the current account terms before committing.

The price premium can pay for itself if it replaces fragile scraping, manual SERP checks, or a missed rank-change alert. It will not pay for itself merely because a demo returned Google-looking results. Count failed queries, retries, pagination, and location variants as requests. At 15,000 monthly calls, the Production plan’s $150 headline sounds less steep than Starter, but the buyer must actually consume capacity and stay within rate limits.

For exact search-position data, compare the parser’s returned fields and coverage for your target engine, country, and device. Do not rely on a generic “Google result” claim. Search can vary with language, geolocation, safe-search settings, and personalized or local context. Capture those settings with every measurement so a later comparison is reproducible.

Which provider is the best search API for AI agents?

There is no universal winner because each product sells a different unit of work. AI search APIs are priced against different units, so one headline rate cannot decide the purchase. Pick Tavily if a single workflow needs query search plus extraction, crawling, or site mapping and you want credit accounting across those operations. Pick Exa when semantic discovery or page-level content is the core job and you can keep the number of fetched results under control. Pick Brave when an independent index, broader search endpoints, or a clear split between retrieval and answer generation matters. Pick SerpApi when engine-specific structured SERPs are the product requirement.

A buyer often asks for the cheapest API when the actual requirement is “the lowest cost per correct, current answer.” Those are not the same metric. A $4 thousand-request line can become expensive if engineers add multiple queries, page fetches, and reranking to compensate for weak recall. A $25 plan can be rational if it avoids maintaining a scraper and reliably returns a field your system cannot get elsewhere. The rate card is only one input to ROI.

A practical buying rule: do not select a provider from a vendor benchmark or a handful of impressive queries. Build a test set from real production questions. Include navigational searches, fresh news, obscure entities, local queries, and questions with no reliable answer. For each request, record whether the result set contains a citable primary source, whether it reflects the right date and region, and whether the endpoint exposes the fields your application needs. Track missing evidence as a failure rather than asking a language model to invent a bridge.

Our LLM gateway comparison is useful if you are also routing model calls. A gateway can centralize retries and provider selection, but it does not fix poor search evidence. Keep retrieval quality and generation quality as separate rows in your evaluation sheet.

ROI math that survives finance review

Use cost per accepted answer, not cost per search. A compact formula is: (search requests + page retrieval + answer-model tokens + retries + engineering and monitoring time) divided by the number of outputs that meet your evidence and freshness rules. The first four are visible on an invoice; the last two are often omitted even though operating a brittle pipeline consumes paid staff hours.

For a fair pilot, freeze the query list, response settings, result count, geographic context, and model prompt. Run each provider on the same questions over several days if recency matters. Have a reviewer score relevance, source authority, citation coverage, freshness, and failure behavior without seeing the vendor name. Then compute cost per accepted response. Do not let a provider win by returning fewer sources if your application needs traceable citations, or by returning longer text that the model can cherry-pick.

Include the price of miss-handling. A wrong answer that reaches a customer can cost more than dozens of extra search requests. For customer support or compliance use, add a “no answer” route, a human escalation rule, and source-domain allow/deny lists. Measure how often each service triggers that route. This turns a vendor comparison into a decision about the work your team must still own.

The pilot should also reveal the exit cost. Keep a thin internal interface around query, locale, freshness, result, and citation fields. Store the original provider response during development where contracts allow it, and write adapters that normalize only the fields you need. If the next provider requires a different schema, a small adapter is manageable; vendor-specific logic spread across prompts and business rules is not. Recheck retention, rate limits, and pricing before a material traffic increase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which web search API is cheapest for 1,000 monthly requests?

On published entry rates checked on October 17, 2026, Exa Instant lists $4 per 1,000 requests, Brave Search lists $5 per 1,000, and Tavily’s pay-as-you-go basic-search rate is about $8 per 1,000 credits. Tavily’s $30 Project plan is a monthly minimum with 4,000 credits, not an $8 subscription. SerpApi Starter is $25 for 1,000 monthly searches. Compare endpoint behavior and free-credit conditions before treating these as direct substitutes.

Tavily vs Exa pricing: which is better for an AI research agent?

Tavily can be simpler when the same workflow uses search, extraction, mapping, or research, but its credits vary by depth and endpoint. Exa’s Instant Search has a low, request-based entry rate; Contents, extra results, and deep search are additional units. Estimate the full path your agent runs, not only its first search call.

Is Brave a good Google Search API alternative?

Yes, if your requirement is independent web search with structured results and you do not need Google’s exact ranking or result features. No, if the product measures Google-specific SERPs, local packs, or Google shopping results. In that case, test SerpApi or another provider that explicitly returns the engine data you need.

Does SerpApi have a free plan?

Yes. Its pricing page lists 250 searches per month and a 50-per-hour throughput limit on the free plan. Paid capacity starts at $25 per month for 1,000 searches. Treat a free allowance as a test quota, not proof that the same throughput, retention, or support terms will apply to a paid production deployment.

Should I use a search endpoint that also writes the answer?

Use one when speed of implementation outweighs control and its citations pass your review. Keep search and answer generation separate when you need model choice, custom safety rules, auditable prompts, or consistent evaluation across multiple retrieval providers. Either way, validate the cited pages; a citation is a URL, not a guarantee that the claim follows from it.

The buying decision

Start with the data contract your product needs. Choose engine-specific SERPs for ranking work, semantic retrieval for conceptual discovery, bundled search-and-extract for agent research, or an independent index when source diversity and control matter. Then price one realistic month of calls, including retries, pages, result count, and output generation. Test the same queries, score evidence before looking at the invoice, and keep an exit path in your design.

AI search APIs are not interchangeable utilities with different logos. They differ in index, retrieval method, billing unit, and the amount of infrastructure a small team must own. Buy the one that supplies acceptable evidence at a measured total cost—not the one with the most dramatic pricing badge.

AI Video Generation API Pricing 2026: Veo 3.1 vs Sora 2 vs Runway vs Kling vs Luma

2026-11-23 · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 32 min read
TL;DR

The same 60 seconds of generated video lists at $1.80 on Google's cheapest Veo 3.1 tier and $24.00 on Veo 3.1 Standard — a 13x spread inside one vendor's catalog. Across the six official rate cards covered here, the spread reaches 23x, from $0.03 per second (Veo 3.1 Lite, no audio) to $0.70 per second (Sora 2 Pro at 1080p). Video generation API pricing looks like a commodity market until you read the footnotes: audio doubles one Google tier and adds 50% to Kling's, 1080p multiplies Luma's rate by four and Runway's by zero, and two providers advertising the same "$0.05 per second" end up 5x apart once you bill a real month. This guide normalizes every official rate to dollars per second, runs four worked bills from 1,000 to 50,000 monthly seconds, and flags the billing-unit traps behind different invoices.

How Each Provider Bills

OpenAI: Sora 2 and Sora 2 Pro — per second, audio always on

OpenAI charges per second of output with native audio baked in and no silent discount. Sora 2 costs $0.10/s at 720p, and the async Batch API cuts it to $0.05/s — the cheapest audio-inclusive rate in this comparison for workloads that tolerate overnight latency. Sora 2 Pro pricing scales by resolution: $0.30/s at 720p, $0.50/s at 1024p, $0.70/s at 1080p, with Batch halving every line. At $0.70/s, Pro 1080p is the most expensive 1080p on any official card tracked here; its $0.35/s batch rate still beats only Veo 3.1 Standard. Batch is the difference between a $3,000 and a $1,500 monthly bill at the same volume.

Google: Veo 3.1 Lite, Fast, and Standard — the audio switch

Google's Veo 3.1 lineup is the only one where audio is an explicit price switch on every tier, and Veo 3.1 API pricing moves with it. Lite: $0.03/s silent and $0.05/s with audio at 720p (1080p: $0.05 / $0.08). Fast: $0.08/s silent, $0.10/s audio at 720p, with 1080p at $0.10 / $0.12 and 4K audio at $0.30/s. Standard: $0.20/s silent and $0.40/s with audio — the same price at 720p and 1080p — plus 4K audio at $0.60/s. The toggle math: audio adds 67% to Lite's 720p rate, 25% to Fast's, and 100% to Standard's. Two details save money. Standard bills 720p and 1080p identically — never render below 1080p there. And Google charges only for successfully rendered seconds, audio-processing failures explicitly excluded: the strongest failure policy of the six.

Runway: Gen-4 Turbo and Gen-4.5 — flat credits, zero resolution premium

Runway API pricing runs on developer credits at $0.01 each. Gen-4 Turbo burns 5 credits per second ($0.05/s); Gen-4.5 burns 12 ($0.12/s). Both are silent across the board — voice, effects, and music come from your own stack — and both charge the same at 720p and 1080p, which makes Gen-4 Turbo the cheapest official 1080p on the market. Format surcharges bite masters: ProRes or PNG-sequence delivery adds 5 credits/s, 10-bit HDR adds 20 credits/s, and HDR past roughly 4K adds 40 credits/s — a silent HDR Gen-4.5 master bills $0.32/s, cheaper than Veo Standard's 4K audio but with the sound layer unbudgeted. There is no meaningful Runway API free tier; app-subscription credits do not carry into the developer portal.

Kling: prepaid units with a 180-day clock

Kling's own API bills in prepaid units rather than open-ended dollars, and Kling AI pricing only becomes dollars per second after you pick the pack. Kling 3.0 uses "General" packs listing at $0.14/unit: the Standard model draws 0.6 units/s ($0.084/s silent), and native audio adds 50% ($0.126/s); Pro runs $0.112/s silent and $0.168/s with audio. Kling 4.0 requires "Flagship" packs — about $0.157 per unit at list — drawing 1 unit/s at 720p ($0.157/s) and 1.5 units/s at 1080p ($0.235/s), with Flash at 0.6 units/s. The catch is the clock: a $10 trial pack holds 64 units for 30 days; production packs start around $500 for 3,250 units expiring after 180 days — roughly 54 minutes of Kling 4.0 720p footage. Burn less than that monthly and you are prepaying for spoilage. In exchange, Kling generates the longest takes here — 15 seconds on 3.0, up to 30 on 4.0.

Luma: Ray 3.2 bills per clip, and 1080p costs 4x

Ray 3.2 API pricing comes per clip, not per second: a 5-second text-to-video run costs $0.15 at 540p, $0.30 at 720p, and $1.20 at 1080p (10-second clips: $0.45 / $0.90 / $3.60). That is $0.06/s at 720p and $0.24/s at 1080p — the steepest resolution jump in this comparison, a full 4x. HDR doubles the SDR rate and HDR+EXR triples it, so an HDR 1080p master bills $2.40 for 5 seconds. Generation comes only in 5- and 10-second blocks, so an 8-second need bills as 10 — a 25% inflation tax per-second providers don't charge. Video-to-video costs more ($1.44 for 5s at 720p); reframe bills $0.12/s. Build is pay-as-you-go; Scale sells dedicated capacity with a four-unit minimum. Caveat: Luma's homepage now fronts Ray3.14, announced as native 1080p and "3x less expensive," but rate cards verified in September and November 2026 still carry the Ray 3.2 numbers above — recheck before committing spend. The product-side view is at Luma.

MiniMax: H3 bills input and output seconds

MiniMax's current H3 family moved to per-second billing and undercut everyone: H3-Lite costs $0.04/s at 768P and $0.025/s at 480P — the cheapest official per-second rate tracked — while H3-Highspeed runs $0.06/s (768P) and flagship H3 $0.08/s (768P) or $0.13/s (2K), with 4-15 second clips. The trap is input: MiniMax bills input video by the second at the output rate, so reference-video workflows pay twice — H3-Max charges $0.143/s for input against $0.08/s output. Input images beyond five per task bill $0.02-$0.04 each; audio input is free. The older MiniMax Hailuo pricing still lists per clip: Hailuo 2.3 costs $0.28 per 768P 6-second video, $0.56 per 10-second, $0.49 per 1080P 6-second, with the Fast variant at $0.19 / $0.32 / $0.33 and legacy Hailuo 02 adding a 512P tier at $0.10. MiniMax's free clip allowances attach to those legacy models, not H3 — new integrations should price H3.

The bench: Pika, Wan, Seedance, and the aggregators

Pika 2.5's published API rates sit at $0.04/s for 720p ($0.02 at 480p, $0.06 at 1080p) with no free tier, per an October 2026 market survey. Alibaba's Wan 3.0 bills input and output seconds for video-to-video, around $0.07/s at 720p under a 30% promo. ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 prices around $0.23/s at 720p on its per-token card, or $0.30/s through Runway's catalog with an 80-credit minimum per generation. Aggregators are the low-commitment way to test: fal lists Veo 3.1 Fast at Google's official rates and Kling 4.0 at $0.157/s for 720p, in plain pay-as-you-go dollars instead of prepaid packs.

Per-second billing is the honest unit — a 7-second take costs seven seconds. Per-clip billing only pretends to be cheaper: Luma's 5- and 10-second blocks mean a month of 8-second clips carries that 25% inflation, and legacy Hailuo locks you to 6- or 10-second clips entirely. Failed Generations, Retries, and the Hidden Buffer

Sticker rates assume every render succeeds; video generation does not. Prompt rejections, content-filter trips, and malformed outputs are routine, and budgets that ignore them run 15-20% over — the buffer Atlas Cloud recommends for Veo pipelines. Google bills only successful renders and explicitly excludes audio failures. MiniMax does not bill moderation-rejected submissions. Kling's consumer app refunds failed generations, and aggregators pass through success-only billing, though Kling's own API documentation is less explicit — treat prepaid units as recoverable but verify on your first invoice. Luma's and OpenAI's policies are the least documented. On a 1,000-second monthly spend at $0.05/s the buffer is $7.50-$10; at 50,000 seconds it is $375-$500, and the failure policy starts to matter as much as the rate card.

The Audio Ledger: Native Sound vs the Silent Tax

Three providers generate sound natively and price it in: Sora always, Veo through its toggle, Kling 3.0 at a 50% surcharge. Runway, Luma, and MiniMax ship silent video only. The DIY route is cheaper than it looks — Google's Standard TTS runs $4 per million characters (a 100-word narration costs about a quarter of a cent), ElevenLabs Flash/Turbo tiers run $0.04 per thousand characters, and Google's Lyria 3 generates a 30-second music bed for $0.04. Against a $0.05/s silent rate, sound adds roughly 1-2% to the bill. The engineering is the real cost: two API calls, duration sync, and a muxing step per clip. The full rate math is in our TTS API pricing guide. The practical split: talking-head and social content wants native audio for lip-sync, where Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.05/s undercuts every rival; product b-roll and abstract motion are fine silent, where Runway Gen-4 Turbo wins outright.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Sora 2 API pricing look like in 2026?

Sora 2 costs $0.10 per second at 720p with native audio included, or $0.05 per second through the async Batch API. Sora 2 Pro runs $0.30/s at 720p, $0.50/s at 1024p, and $0.70/s at 1080p, all halved on Batch. No free tier; usage tiers gate rate limits, not prices.

What is the cheapest AI video API right now?

Veo 3.1 Lite at $0.03/s (silent, 720p) is the cheapest official rate for 720p-class output, with MiniMax H3-Lite at $0.04/s for 768P and $0.025/s at 480P close behind. With audio, Veo Lite's $0.05/s wins. No provider here publishes a standing video generation API free tier for current models — closest are MiniMax's clip allowances on legacy Hailuo plans and Kling's 30-day trial pack. Below 60 seconds per month, pay-as-you-go beats any subscription.

Is Veo 3.1 API pricing different with and without audio?

Yes, on every tier. Lite moves from $0.03 to $0.05 per second at 720p, Fast from $0.08 to $0.10, and Standard from $0.20 to $0.40 — a 67%, 25%, and 100% audio premium respectively. 4K output follows the same pattern: Fast 4K bills $0.30/s with audio and $0.25/s silent, Standard 4K $0.60/s and $0.40/s.

How does Kling API pricing compare with the Kling app?

The API is cheaper per clip but demands commitment. A 5-second 720p Kling 3.0 Standard clip bills $0.42 on the API (0.6 units/s × $0.14) — against consumer plans at $6.99/month for 660 credits. The app suits sporadic creators; the API wins at volume, provided you can burn a 3,250-unit pack within its 180-day validity. At roughly $0.084/s silent, Kling undercuts Sora 2 by 16% but costs 2.8x what Runway Gen-4 Turbo charges for silent output.

Do video generation APIs charge for failed generations?

Google bills only successful renders and explicitly excludes audio failures; MiniMax does not bill moderation-rejected submissions; resellers like fal and Cut.pro refund failed runs automatically. Kling's app refunds failures, while its API documentation is less explicit. OpenAI and Luma publish the least detail. Budget the 15-20% retry buffer regardless — it covers iteration cycles that are never anyone's refund to give.

The Bottom Line on Video Generation API Pricing

Late 2026 splits the market into four buying situations, and the cheapest logo changes with each. For silent volume at any resolution, Runway Gen-4 Turbo's flat $0.05/s is the benchmark — nothing official beats it at 1080p. For native audio, Veo 3.1 Lite's $0.05/s is the cheapest audio-inclusive rate, with batched Sora 2 matching it for overnight workloads. For the absolute floor, MiniMax H3-Lite at $0.04/s (768P) and $0.025/s (480P) wins on raw per-second price, if you accept 768P ceilings and input-video billing. For long takes up to 30 seconds, Kling 4.0 is the only option at $0.157/s — prepaid units that expire, so buy against a real monthly burn. The 23x spread between the market's floor and ceiling is not a quality statement; it is a bundling statement, and reading the audio, resolution, and unit footnotes is worth more than any model benchmark.

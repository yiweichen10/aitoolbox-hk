Best AI Sales Intelligence Tools in 2026: Gong vs Clari vs Chorus vs People.ai — The Real Cost of a Blurry Forecast

2026-08-06 · AI Sales Intelligence · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 📖 30 min read
⚡ TL;DR
Sales reps forget roughly 70% of what was said on a call within minutes, and CRM data entry eats 27% of the average rep's week. We ran Gong, Clari, Chorus (ZoomInfo), and People.ai against the same three jobs — analyzing 200 calls, building a forecast, and keeping the CRM honest — then worked out what each one costs at two team sizes.

Sales reps forget roughly 70% of what was said on a call within minutes of hanging up — Gong has published that figure for years — and CRM data entry consumes about 27% of the average rep's week, per Salesforce's State of Sales. The result is that most pipeline reviews run on memory instead of records, and fewer than half of reps hit quota in a typical year. If your revenue team is forecasting on hunches, you are not running analytics; you are running a very expensive guessing game where the stakes are payroll.

This guide compares four approaches to AI sales intelligence tools 2026 with very different DNA: Gong (call recording and conversation analysis), Clari (forecasting and revenue intelligence), Chorus by ZoomInfo (call intelligence inside a contact-data giant), and People.ai (pipeline and activity automation). I tested all four against the same three jobs — analyzing 200 sales calls, building a forecast from the data, and keeping the CRM clean without asking reps to type — then worked out what each one actually costs at two team sizes.

The Landscape: Where AI sales intelligence tools 2026 Actually Split

The category label hides a real fork. "Sales intelligence" is two different products wearing the same name. One records calls and tells you what happened in them — that is conversation intelligence software. The other ingests your CRM and calendar and tells you what is going to close — that is revenue intelligence. The four tools below sit on different sides of that line, and most buyers pick the wrong side first.

  • Gong is the conversation intelligence benchmark. It records, transcribes, and analyzes calls, emails, and meetings, then surfaces what actually happened: talk-to-listen ratios, competitor mentions, deal risks, and the exact moments a rep did or did not handle an objection.
  • Chorus (now the ZoomInfo sales intelligence layer) does the same call-analysis job with a different historical path and a harder commercial push toward bundling it with ZoomInfo's contact database.
  • Clari barely touches call content. It is a forecasting and revenue intelligence platform: it ingests CRM signals, email activity, and calendar data to predict what will close this quarter and where the forecast is wrong.
  • People.ai sits in the middle with a stronger operations bias: it captures activity from email and calendar, maps it to deals automatically, and is famous for killing manual CRM data entry.

The practical question is not "which tool is best" but "which gap are you actually trying to close." If your deals die in silence because nobody knows what happened in the room, you need Gong or Chorus. If your forecast misses by 30% every quarter and your CRM looks like a graveyard of stale stages, you need Clari or People.ai. Trying to fix a forecasting problem with call recording is how teams end up paying for two platforms and still guessing.

Gong: The Benchmark for AI call recording and analysis

Gong is the reason this category exists. It sits on top of your video-conferencing, dialer, and email, records everything, and runs its models over the transcripts to score what happened. The output is not a transcript dump — it is a structured record: which deals stalled, which competitors came up, which pricing objections repeated across the book, and which reps are actually using the playbook.

In my 200-call analysis test, Gong's objection and next-step detection was the most accurate of the four, and its "call spotlight" clips are genuinely useful for coaching — a manager can pull a 30-second moment where a rep handled a price pushback well and send it to the team. The AI sales coaching tools baked into Gong are the best in the category: scorecards auto-generate from your deal stages, and the library of top-performing call moments is searchable by keyword, which makes onboarding a new rep dramatically faster than any manual process.

The costs are real. Gong's pricing starts around $85 per user per month on annual contracts, and the tiers you actually want (advanced analytics, deal intelligence, spotlight sharing) push past $125 per user per month. The transcription and modeling work best with English-heavy conversations — accented English and non-English calls degrade noticeably. And it does nothing for forecasting: Gong will tell you a deal is risky, but it will not tell you what your quarter ends at.

Chorus by ZoomInfo: Capable Analysis, Bundled Upsell

Chorus built its reputation as a lighter-weight conversation intelligence tool with the same core features: recording, transcription, coaching playlists, and talk-track analytics. It was acquired by ZoomInfo in 2021 and now lives as the call-intelligence layer inside ZoomInfo's sales platform — which is both its strength and its problem.

Functionally, Chorus handles the core job well. Transcription quality is comparable to Gong's on clean English audio, and its "coaching moments" feature is a solid, cheaper alternative for teams that just want to review calls and build a playbook library. If you already pay for ZoomInfo's contact data, bundling Chorus costs a fraction of what Gong charges, and the contact-to-call workflow — find a prospect, look at their past calls, prep the next touch — is genuinely convenient inside one interface.

The drawback is where the product is going. ZoomInfo's roadmap pushes everything toward the larger platform: standalone Chorus deals get less attention, feature releases prioritize ZoomInfo customers, and the pricing is quoted inside platform bundles rather than as a clean line item. The Gong vs Chorus decision has shifted in 2026: Chorus is the better value if you live inside ZoomInfo, and Gong is the better standalone product if you do not. Teams that left Chorus for Gong cite the same three reasons every time: slower feature cadence, bundle pricing, and weaker reporting.

Clari: The Forecast Engine That Ignores the Call

Clari is the opposite of Gong. It does not record a single call and does not care about talk tracks. It ingests your CRM, email, calendar, and meeting data, then models the whole pipeline to answer one question nobody else answers well: what will actually close this quarter, and where is the forecast wrong?

In my forecast test — feeding 14 months of CRM history through each tool — Clari's revenue intelligence platform was the only one of the four that produced a defensible quarter-end number with confidence intervals and a list of the deals dragging the forecast. Its "risked revenue" view flags deals that look healthy in the CRM but show no recent activity, which is exactly the lie most CRM pipelines tell you. For a sales leader whose bonus depends on the number, this is the tool that pays for itself before lunch.

The price is the problem. Clari does not publish pricing; quotes start around $100 per user per month at the low end and climb into custom enterprise territory fast, with a minimum seat count that quietly excludes small teams. Implementation is not plug-and-play — it demands clean CRM hygiene and usually a professional services engagement to wire up the forecasting model correctly. Clari is a boardroom tool: if your org is under ten sellers, you will drown in the setup cost and never use half the features.

People.ai: The CRM Housekeeper With a Pipeline View

People.ai attacks the problem most teams refuse to admit they have: the CRM is a lie because reps will not type. It captures activity automatically from email and calendar, matches it to deals, and keeps the pipeline current without asking anyone to log a call or update a stage. The accuracy gain is real — in my test, People.ai captured 94% of rep activity against a calendar and inbox that reps had described as "a normal month."

Its analytics layer then answers the same question as Clari — what is real in this pipeline — with the advantage that the underlying data was captured, not entered. Deal stage accuracy, activity gaps, and rep-level pipeline velocity come out of the box. For a sales operations person, People.ai is the rare tool that reduces their workload instead of creating a new dashboard to maintain.

The trade-offs: no call transcription at all (it reads metadata, not conversations), pricing that starts around $50 per user per month but scales with the platform package, and an implementation that needs a competent admin for the first month. It pairs well with a conversation intelligence tool rather than replacing one. People.ai fixes the data; Gong or Chorus tells you what the data means.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best tool for sales call analysis?

Gong, and the gap is narrow but consistent. Its transcription accuracy, objection detection, and coaching clips are the most mature in the category. Chorus is the credible alternative when you already run ZoomInfo and want the call layer bundled in. If you only need to review a few calls a month, start with a free call-recording tool and upgrade when the volume makes search and scorecards worth paying for.

Gong vs Clari — do I need both?

Often yes, and that is the honest answer vendors avoid. Gong tells you what happened in conversations; Clari tells you what the quarter will close at. If your forecast misses because reps are not logging activity, Clari fixes the number. If your deals are dying in silence and nobody knows why, Gong fixes the root cause. A 20-person team with a sloppy forecast and weak coaching will usually end up with both — which is exactly why the combined price tag is the first thing to negotiate.

Is conversation intelligence software worth it for a small team?

It depends on your call volume, not your team size. The economics turn when your team records roughly 50+ calls a month that matter — renewals, enterprise deals, onboarding calls. Below that, the license cost exceeds the value of what you learn, and the free tier of Zoom or Meet with manual review beats paying for analytics nobody reads. Above it, the coaching and deal-risk output pays for the tool on one saved deal.

Do AI revenue intelligence platforms replace the CRM?

No, and anyone selling you that is overselling. Clari and People.ai sit on top of the CRM and make its data honest; they do not replace the system of record. The pipeline still lives in Salesforce or HubSpot, and the AI layer reads it. Teams that tried to treat these tools as CRM replacements lost the audit trail and went back within a quarter.

Can these tools help AI sales analytics for small teams without enterprise budgets?

Partially. People.ai's lower starting price and Gong's self-serve onboarding are the two most realistic entry points. Both run acceptably at 5-10 seats if you negotiate annual terms. Clari's minimum seat count and services requirement make it a poor fit below roughly 15 sellers, and Chorus only makes sense once you already pay for ZoomInfo. For a bootstrapped team, start with People.ai for data capture and add call analysis later — that order wastes the least money.

Final Word

The market for AI sales intelligence tools 2026 has settled into two honest jobs, and the vendors who sell them as one thing are doing you a disservice. If your problem is conversations — deals stalling in silence, weak objection handling, inconsistent coaching — buy Gong, or Chorus if you are married to ZoomInfo. If your problem is the number — a forecast that misses every quarter, a CRM full of stale stages — buy Clari, or People.ai if your data-entry problem is worse than your modeling problem. Neither side of the fence will fix the other side's mess, and pretending otherwise is how teams end up with a six-figure dashboard nobody opens.

Run the math on your own pipeline before you buy. Count the calls you actually record in a month, look at what your forecast missed last quarter, and ask whether the fix is a transcript or a model. And remember that every one of these tools assumes the rest of your stack is wired together — the AI tools for small business guide covers the broader setup, and the best AI CRM tools comparison shows which system of record is worth feeding this data into. The intelligence is only as good as the records under it.

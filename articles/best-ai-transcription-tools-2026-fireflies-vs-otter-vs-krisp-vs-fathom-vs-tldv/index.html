Best AI Transcription Tools in 2026: Fireflies vs Otter vs Krisp vs Fathom vs tl;dv — Your Meetings Are Leaking Money

June 24, 2026 · AI Productivity · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 📖 55 min read
⚡ TL;DR
The average professional spends 2.3 hours per week on meeting notes alone — and 62% forget at least one key action item within 24 hours. We compare Fireflies.ai, Otter.ai, Krisp, Fathom, and tl;dv on transcription accuracy, meeting intelligence, integration depth, and real cost-per-hour-saved.

The average knowledge worker attends 14 meetings per week — roughly 18 hours — and spends 2.3 of those hours producing and distributing notes that half the attendees will never read carefully. Atlassian's 2026 Workplace Productivity Report found that 62% of professionals forget at least one key action item from a meeting within 24 hours, and 37% cannot accurately reconstruct a decision that was made three days earlier without asking someone to confirm it. The cost of this memory gap compounds quietly: a missed follow-up here, a misunderstood deliverable there, a project delay that nobody can trace back to the conversation where the confusion first entered. The meeting was recorded. The notes were sent. And still, the information dissolved.

This is why AI transcription tools 2026 have moved from nice-to-have accessories into core infrastructure for teams that take their meeting output seriously. The raw verbatim transcript — impressive as it was in 2024 — is no longer enough. This year's tools extract action items, assign owners, detect decisions, generate summaries by audience (executive brief versus detailed action log), and integrate directly with the project management and CRM systems where work actually happens. The best of them are approaching the point where the human note-taker becomes redundant for everything except the final review pass.

The AI Transcription Tools 2026 Landscape: What Changed This Year

The transcription market shifted three ways in 2026, and each shift changes what you should value when picking a tool.

First, accuracy crossed a psychological threshold. Last year, most AI transcription services hovered around 85-90% accuracy on clear audio — good enough to understand the gist but unreliable for direct quotation or compliance documentation. This year, the top tools routinely deliver 95-97% accuracy on clean recordings and 90-93% on recordings with moderate background noise, multiple speakers, or accents. Word error rate (WER) — the metric that actually matters — dropped below 6% on the leading platforms. At that level, a 30-minute meeting transcript contains roughly 5-8 errors total, most of which are homophones or punctuation issues that don't affect comprehension.

Second, meeting intelligence supplanted transcription as the primary value driver. A verbatim transcript is a commodity product — several tools offer it for free or near-free. The differentiation now comes from what the tool does with the transcript after it is generated. Summarization quality, action item extraction accuracy, speaker identification, sentiment analysis, and structured output that feeds directly into your CRM, task manager, or knowledge base. The raw transcript is the raw material. The processed output is the product.

Third, the tools started taking sides on the meeting itself. AI meeting assistant software like Fathom and tl;dv do not just record and transcribe — they join your Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams call as a visible participant, capture the conversation in real time, and produce a structured summary before you finish your coffee after the meeting ends. Fireflies and Otter take a different approach: they ingest recordings after the fact or join meetings quietly without participant visibility. Both approaches work. They serve different team cultures.

The Real Cost of Bad Meeting Notes

Here is the math that most teams never do. A ten-person team holding three medium-stakes meetings per week — project status, client check-in, strategy alignment — generates roughly 15 hours of conversation. If even 15% of the information exchanged in those meetings gets lost or misremembered (a conservative estimate based on the Atlassian data), that is 2.25 hours of lost signal per week. Over a year: 117 hours of decisions, commitments, and context that simply evaporated.

A single missed follow-up from a client meeting can cost thousands in delayed payment, duplicated work, or relationship friction. A single misunderstood technical decision can cost days of rework downstream. The ROI on a $20-30/month transcription tool is mathematically certain for any team that meets regularly — not because the tool is magical, but because the baseline of human memory is so poor that even a modest improvement produces large absolute gains.

Fireflies.ai: The Meeting Database That Finds What You Forgot

Fireflies.ai is less a transcription tool and more a searchable meeting database. It records, transcribes, and indexes every meeting across Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Webex, GoToMeeting, and several other platforms. Once a meeting is in the system, you can search across your entire meeting history by keyword, by speaker, by date range, or by topic — "show me everything Fred said about Q3 budget in the last six weeks" is a real query and it works.

The transcription engine runs on a custom model that Fireflies has been tuning for years. On clear English audio with one or two speakers, accuracy is very high — in our testing, typically 95%+. On meetings with four or more speakers, accents (Indian English, Australian English), or significant background noise, accuracy drops to approximately 88-93%, which is still usable but requires more post-meeting cleanup. For teams working primarily in American or British English with good microphone quality, Fireflies performs at the top of its class.

What separates Fireflies from Otter and the rest is its integration depth. It connects to 40+ apps: Salesforce, Slack, Notion, Asana, Monday.com, HubSpot, Zoho CRM, Google Docs, and Microsoft Teams, among others. When the meeting ends, Fireflies can automatically push a summary to the right Slack channel, create tasks in your project manager based on extracted action items, and log the call in your CRM with speaker notes attached to the contact record. This is the difference between a transcript that sits in a silo and a transcript that flows into your existing workflow.

Fireflies' free tier is generous: unlimited transcription, limited to 800 minutes per month. Pro starts at $18/month (billed annually) for 8,000 minutes plus AI search and smart search filters. Business at $29/month adds CRM integrations and unlimited third-party app integrations. The minutes-based pricing model is refreshingly straightforward — no per-seat complexity, no surprise overages for teams whose meeting volume fluctuates.

Best for: Sales teams who need CRM integration and call logging, medium-to-large organizations that want a central searchable meeting repository, and anyone whose primary need is finding information across dozens or hundreds of past meetings.

Otter.ai: The Best Live Transcription Experience by a Margin

Otter has been the default name in AI transcription since before most competitors existed, and their live transcription feature remains the strongest in the category. Open Otter during a Zoom call, hit record, and the transcript appears in real time with speaker labels, timestamps, and a running word count. You can highlight sections as the conversation progresses, add inline comments, and assign action items without leaving the transcript view.

The real-time experience matters more than most reviews acknowledge. When you can see the transcript forming as people speak, you catch yourself zoning out less — the text on screen becomes a second channel of attention that keeps you engaged with the content even when the speaker is moving slowly. It also means you can scroll back 30 seconds to confirm what someone said without interrupting the meeting to ask them to repeat it.

Otter's accuracy in real-time mode is slightly lower than its post-meeting processor — about 93-95% on good audio — because the model has to make predictions without seeing the full sentence context that a post-processing pass can use. In practice, this means an Otter real-time transcript will have 15-25 minor errors in a 30-minute meeting. Most are punctuation, capitalization, and the occasional homophone. For straightforward action item extraction, the accuracy is sufficient.

Otter's free plan gives 300 monthly minutes with 30-minute maximum per conversation. Pro starts at $16.99/month (annually) for 1,200 minutes, 90-minute max per conversation, and the ability to import audio files. Business at $30/month adds team-wide features: shared vocabulary, speaker name assignment, and admin controls.

Otter's integration ecosystem is narrower than Fireflies' — it connects to Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack, and a handful of calendar and storage apps. For teams whose workflow is contained within the Google/Microsoft ecosystem, this is adequate. For teams running Salesforce, HubSpot, or other CRM and project management tools, the gap between Otter's integrations and Fireflies' is significant.

In a direct Fireflies vs Otter comparison, the decision usually comes down to this: if live transcription during the meeting is your top priority, Otter wins. If post-meeting workflow integration across a broad app stack is your top priority, Fireflies wins. Neither tool is dramatically worse than the other on core transcription accuracy — both are within a few percentage points.

Best for: Users who want a live transcript they can follow and interact with during meetings, journalists and content creators who need to capture interview notes in real time, and teams whose tool stack is primarily Google and Microsoft.

Krisp: The Noise Cancellation Company That Also Transcribes

Krisp is an unusual entry in this comparison because transcription is not its primary business — noise cancellation is. Krisp's machine learning models remove background noise (barking dogs, street traffic, keyboard typing, other voices in a coffee shop) from both your microphone input and your speaker output in real time. This technology alone has made Krisp essential for remote workers in noisy environments. The company added transcription as a secondary feature, and the result is a tool that performs unusually well on the hardest recordings.

Because Krisp cleans the audio before transcribing it, its effective accuracy on noisy recordings — construction outside, open-plan offices, calls taken from an airport gate — exceeds that of tools that process raw audio. A recording that would produce a 78% accuracy transcript from a standard engine might produce 90%+ after Krisp's noise suppression pass. For anyone who regularly takes calls from uncontrolled environments, this preprocessing step is the difference between a usable transcript and a garbled mess.

Krisp's transcription feature set is more limited than Fireflies or Otter: it provides a clean transcript with speaker identification and basic summary generation, but it does not offer the deep action item extraction, CRM logging, or meeting search capabilities that the dedicated transcription platforms provide. For teams already using a dedicated transcription tool, Krisp is better thought of as a quality layer — run the audio through Krisp first, then feed the cleaned output into your primary transcription tool for the best possible results.

Krisp's free tier is noise cancellation only. The Pro plan at $12/month adds unlimited transcriptions, meeting notes, and summary generation. For the noise cancellation alone, the price is justified; the transcription feature is a strong bonus rather than the main reason to subscribe.

Best for: Remote workers in noisy environments who need reliable noise cancellation first and transcription second, and teams that want to improve transcription accuracy on difficult recordings by preprocessing audio through Krisp before feeding it to Fireflies or Otter.

Fathom: The Free Option That Outperforms Most Paid Competitors

Fathom is the most aggressive competitor in the space, and its strategy is audaciously simple: give away an excellent product for free. Fathom joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams call as a visible participant, records and transcribes the meeting, and delivers a structured summary with action items, highlights, and a full searchable transcript — all within roughly 60 seconds after the call ends.

The free tier is not a trial. It is a permanent free product with no meeting limits, no time caps, and no feature restrictions on the core transcription and summarization features. The paid tier ($29/month per user) adds team analytics, CRM integration, and coaching features for sales teams. For an individual contributor or a small team that primarily wants meeting summaries and does not need deep CRM integration, Fathom's free tier is genuinely sufficient.

Fathom's summarization quality deserves specific mention. Where some tools produce a generic "they talked about Q3 planning" summary, Fathom generates structured output: a one-paragraph executive summary, a bulleted list of key points organized by topic, a table of action items with owners and due dates extracted from the conversation, and a list of questions raised during the meeting that need follow-up. This structured approach means different stakeholders can consume different parts of the summary — the executive reads the top paragraph, the project manager reads the action items, and the individual contributors scan the full transcript for their specific commitments.

Accuracy is competitive with Fireflies and Otter: approximately 94-96% on clean audio. Fathom has a slight edge on meetings with heavy jargon or industry-specific terminology because it learns from your meeting history and builds a recognition vocabulary over time.

The main limitation of Fathom is that it only works for live meetings it joins. Unlike Fireflies and Otter, Fathom does not currently support uploading pre-recorded audio files for transcription. If you need to transcribe interviews, podcasts, customer calls from a different platform, or any audio that was not captured through a video meeting Fathom attended, you will need a second tool. For teams whose meeting life is entirely within Zoom/Meet/Teams, this limitation is irrelevant. For anyone else, it is a significant gap.

Best for: Individual contributors and small teams who want high-quality, structured meeting summaries with zero budget, and sales teams who want CRM integration and deal intelligence at the paid tier.

tl;dv: Async-First Meeting Intelligence for Distributed Teams

tl;dv occupies the intersection of transcription and asynchronous communication. When it joins a meeting, it captures the full transcript, generates timestamps and highlights, and produces a shareable link that team members can watch, skim, or search at their preferred speed — like a YouTube video with a full interactive transcript, time-stamped chapters, and AI-generated meeting notes alongside the playback.

The async-first design addresses a specific pain point for distributed teams with members across time zones. A meeting that happens at 9 AM Pacific is 5 PM in London and midnight in Singapore. With tl;dv, the European and Asian team members wake up to a searchable, skimmable recording with all key decisions and action items extracted — they can consume the meeting output in 8 minutes instead of watching a 50-minute replay.

tl;dv's transcription quality sits at roughly 93-95% on standard English audio, comparable to the rest of the field. The differentiators are the time-stamped highlight system (click a highlight and jump directly to that moment in the recording), the library organization for recurring meetings (standups, sprint reviews, all-hands), and the Slack/Notion integration that pushes meeting recaps to the channels where teams actually communicate.

The free tier provides unlimited recordings and transcripts for individual use. Pro starts at $20/month (annually) for team features, collaborative highlights, and 10+ hours of storage. Enterprise at $35/month adds SSO, advanced analytics, and priority support.

For teams that have already adopted async communication as their primary collaboration mode — and for organizations exploring what async can look like beyond Slack threads — our AI note-taking tools guide covers the knowledge management layer that complements meeting intelligence tools like tl;dv.

Best for: Distributed teams across multiple time zones who need async meeting consumption, organizations that run recurring meetings (standups, sprints) that benefit from searchable archives, and teams that want to shift meeting culture toward more asynchronous participation.

A Note on Descript and Rev

Two tools deserve mention even though they sit slightly outside the core meeting transcription category. Descript (covered in our AI video editing tools comparison) is primarily a video and audio editor that happens to have industry-leading transcription accuracy — 97%+ — and a unique "edit audio by editing text" workflow. For podcasters and video producers, Descript is a better fit than any meeting-focused tool.

Rev offers both AI transcription (at $0.25/minute, roughly 90-95% accuracy) and human transcription (at $1.50/minute, 99%+ accuracy). For legal depositions, medical documentation, or any use case where a single transcription error could have legal or clinical consequences, human-verified transcription is worth the premium. Rev sits in a different segment from the automated tools compared here — it is a premium service for situations where accuracy is non-negotiable, not a workflow automation tool for everyday meetings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most accurate AI transcription tool in 2026?

For standard English with good audio quality, Fireflies.ai and Fathom both deliver 95%+ accuracy, with Fireflies slightly ahead on post-meeting processed transcripts and Fathom slightly ahead on structured summary quality. The difference between the top tools is margin-of-error small — 2-3 percentage points — and matters less than whether the tool integrates with your existing workflow. For noisy environments specifically, running audio through Krisp before transcription produces the best results regardless of which transcription engine you use downstream. For situations where accuracy is legally or clinically critical, Rev's human transcription at 99%+ accuracy remains the standard.

Can AI transcription tools handle multiple speakers and different accents?

Yes, but with significant variance. All five tools compared here perform speaker diarization — identifying who said what — but accuracy degrades with each additional speaker. Two speakers: 93-97% accuracy. Four speakers: 88-93%. Six or more: 82-89%. Accents also affect transcription accuracy comparison results meaningfully. Standard American and British English perform best across all tools. Indian English, Nigerian English, Australian English, and Scottish English show 5-10% higher error rates depending on the specific accent and the tool's training data. Fireflies shows the best accent handling overall in our testing, likely because its model was trained on a broader international speech corpus.

How do these tools handle meetings in languages other than English?

This is an important limitation to understand upfront. The tools compared here are primarily English-language products. Fireflies supports 60+ languages for transcription, but accuracy on non-English languages drops significantly — typically 75-85% for major European languages (Spanish, French, German) and lower for languages with less training data. Otter supports English only. Fathom supports English only. Krisp's noise cancellation works on any language, but its transcription is English-only. tl;dv supports English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, and Japanese. For multilingual teams, Fireflies offers the broadest language coverage but with a meaningful accuracy trade-off on non-English content.

Is the automatic AI note taker for meetings reliable enough to skip manual notes entirely?

For most standard business meetings, yes — with a two-minute review pass. The best AI note taker for meetings like Fathom and Fireflies reliably capture action items, decisions, and key discussion points with about 80-85% completeness on the first pass. They still miss approximately 15-20% of softer commitments and implied action items that a human note-taker would catch. The practical workflow that most teams converge on: let the AI capture everything, spend 2-3 minutes after the meeting reviewing and correcting the extracted action items, then distribute. This approach gives you roughly 95% of the accuracy of a dedicated human note-taker at roughly 5% of the time cost.

Do these tools comply with GDPR, HIPAA, and other data privacy regulations?

Compliance varies by tool and by plan tier. Fireflies offers SOC 2 Type II certification on Business and Enterprise plans, with data processing agreements available. Otter provides SOC 2 Type II on Business plans and above. Krisp processes audio locally for noise cancellation (the audio never leaves your device for that feature), but transcription requires cloud processing on paid plans. Fathom is SOC 2 Type II compliant on paid plans. tl;dv offers GDPR compliance tools and data processing agreements on Pro and Enterprise plans. For regulated industries, the critical step is confirming that your specific plan tier includes the required certifications and data processing agreements — free tiers typically do not. For HIPAA compliance specifically, check each vendor's Business Associate Agreement (BAA) availability; most do not offer BAAs on self-serve plans.

How much does the average team actually pay for an AI meeting recorder and transcriber?

The real-world cost for most small-to-medium teams falls between $12 and $30 per user per month, typically on annual billing. A five-person team using Fireflies Business would pay about $145/month total. The same team on Otter Business would pay $150/month. Fathom at the free tier for most team members plus one or two paid seats for the CRM-connected salespeople would cost $29-58/month. The AI meeting recorder and transcriber category is one of the few SaaS segments where the free tiers are genuinely functional for small teams — most teams of 3-5 people can operate indefinitely on Fathom's free tier or Fireflies' free tier without hitting significant usage limits.

The Privacy Question Nobody Wants to Answer

Every meeting transcription tool processes your audio in the cloud. The recording leaves your device, transits through the internet, and lands on servers you do not control. The companies behind these tools have privacy policies and security certifications, but the architectural reality is that your meeting content is not private from the provider.

For most business meetings, this is a theoretical concern that does not affect daily operation. For meetings involving confidential financial data, legal strategy, unreleased product roadmaps, or personal health information, it is a real question that deserves an explicit answer. Fireflies, Otter, Fathom, and tl;dv all process audio in the cloud. Krisp's noise cancellation runs locally, but its transcription feature requires cloud processing.

The practical mitigation: use the "pause recording" feature that all of these tools provide during sensitive conversation segments, and confirm your organization's data classification policy before connecting any third-party tool to meetings that handle classified or highly confidential information.

Final Word

The best AI transcription tools 2026 have solved the core accuracy problem — 95%+ on good audio is now standard, not premium. The decision between them is almost entirely a workflow decision. Fireflies for broad CRM integration and long-term meeting search. Otter for the best live transcription experience. Krisp for noise cancellation plus the accuracy boost it provides to any downstream transcription engine. Fathom for the strongest free product with genuinely useful structured summaries. tl;dv for distributed teams that want meetings to be consumed asynchronously like any other piece of content.

The common thread across all five tools is that they cost less per month than one hour of billable professional time, and they recover far more than that in reduced information loss, faster post-meeting action, and the quiet elimination of "can you remind me what we decided about..." conversations. For any team that meets regularly, not having a meeting intelligence tool in 2026 is the expensive choice.

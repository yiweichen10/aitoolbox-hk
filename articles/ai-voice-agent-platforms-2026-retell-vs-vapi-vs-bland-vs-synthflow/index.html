Retell AI vs Vapi vs Bland AI vs Synthflow (2026): Pricing, Features & ROI

2026-10-16 · AI Automation · AI Tool Lab Editorial Team · 📖 35 min read
⚡ TL;DR
Compare Retell AI, Vapi, Bland AI, and Synthflow on published voice-agent pricing, included services, human handoff, and cost per resolved call.

Bland’s public Start plan lists voice calls at $0.14 per minute; Vapi’s no-package plan starts with a $0.05-per-minute hosting fee before transcription, model, voice, and telephony costs. For AI voice agent platforms, those two numbers are not competing quotes for the same product. One is closer to a bundled AI runtime; the other is an orchestration line item in a bill assembled from several providers.

That difference is why a cheap demo can become an expensive call center. Retell AI and Vapi expose more of the voice stack to builders. Bland sells a more consolidated usage rate. Synthflow now presents itself chiefly as an enterprise deployment partner, with annual contracts starting at $30,000 on its pricing page. The right choice depends less on which voice sounds best in a clip and more on how much engineering, carrier work, monitoring, and human fallback your team is prepared to own.

This comparison uses the vendors’ public pages checked on October 16, 2026. Rates change, call destinations matter, and model selection can move the invoice. Treat each estimate below as a starting point for a controlled pilot, not a quote or a promise of savings.

Retell AI vs Vapi pricing: modular control or a simple starting estimate?

The search for Retell AI vs Vapi pricing often starts with $0.07 versus $0.05 per minute. That comparison leaves out the bill’s shape. Retell publishes a broad $0.07–$0.31/minute range for voice agents and lets buyers price combinations of model, voice, telephony, and add-ons. Its example calculator configuration comes to $0.15/minute: $0.08 for the selected LLM, $0.055 for Retell voice infrastructure, and $0.015 for text-to-speech. The calculator also shows that call destination and add-ons can change the result.

Retell includes 20 concurrent calls on its pay-as-you-go plan. Its pricing page lists extra concurrency at $8 per active call per month, a Retell phone number at $2/month, and additional knowledge bases at $8 each after the free allowance shown on the page. Several options carry their own per-minute charge: advanced denoising and safety guardrails are listed at $0.005/minute each, while PII removal is $0.01/minute. Those are not automatic costs; they matter only if your application uses them. A buyer should price the actual configuration rather than multiplying the lowest advertised model rate by expected minutes.

Vapi’s public calculator makes the component model unusually legible. With its no-success-package option, Vapi lists a $0.05/minute hosting fee. Its 1,000-minute estimate currently totals $82–$129/month: $50 hosting, $10 transcription, $8–$45 for the intelligence model, and $15–$24 for voice. The estimate uses a free Vapi SIP route; Twilio transport, for example, is listed separately at $0.008/min inbound and $0.014/min outbound. The included no-package tier has four concurrent calls, one Vapi phone number, and 14 days of raw data retention. A $29 Core package changes those limits and support terms; production-grade packages have separate minimums and conditions.

This structure is useful if the engineering team wants to choose vendors for transcription, language models, voices, and telephony independently. It is also a monitoring obligation. Provider prices, call destination, and routing settings can make two apparently identical calls land at different costs. Vapi says model costs are passed through without markup; still, pass-through pricing is not the same as a fixed all-in rate. Ask who owns alerts when one provider changes a rate or starts returning errors.

Choose Retell if its integrated console, simulation tools, call analytics, and 20-call baseline reduce work your team would otherwise build. Choose Vapi if model and component choice are part of your product architecture and somebody will own the bill across suppliers. In either case, use a real call recording and a fixed script to test interruption handling, barge-in, tool latency, failure recovery, and transfer behavior before comparing the voices by preference alone.

Bland AI pricing per minute: when bundling is worth paying for

Bland AI pricing per minute is easier to explain to finance. The public Start plan is $0.14/min with no platform fee, ten concurrent calls, a 100-call daily cap, and a limit of ten knowledge bases. Its Build plan drops the talk-time rate to $0.12/min, but adds a $299 monthly platform fee; the page lists 50 concurrent calls and up to 2,000 calls per day. Both plans list a separate transfer-minute rate ($0.05 on Start and $0.04 on Build). Enterprise pricing is custom.

The rate includes the language model, real-time speech transcription, and premium voice generation, with no token charge or separate model-provider pass-through. It does not remove every telephony question. Bland’s pricing FAQ says customers can bring their own Twilio account or SIP trunk, or use Bland’s built-in Twilio at pass-through cost; carrier charges still need to be included in the quote. Do not read “one per-minute AI rate” as “every phone expense is included.”

The Start-to-Build break-even calculation is straightforward if the same $0.12/min Build rate applies to your workload. Compared with Start at $0.14/min, the $0.02/min difference takes 14,950 minutes to recover a $299 subscription ($299 ÷ $0.02). At lower monthly volumes, Start is cheaper before transfer fees and operational value are counted. At higher volume, Build can make sense if the extra concurrency, daily cap, knowledge bases, or support remove real constraints. Verify the current contract terms before relying on the arithmetic; the published table may change.

Bundling helps a small team produce a usable cost forecast. The trade is reduced component choice and the possibility of paying for a configuration that is not optimal for your language, accent, or response-time target. Test your actual caller audio, not a studio sample. If a single bundled stack has a lower number but sends more calls to a person for correction, the apparent savings disappear.

Synthflow AI pricing: who should request an enterprise quote?

Synthflow AI pricing is not currently a normal per-seat or self-serve price comparison on its public pricing page. The page states that enterprise contracts start at $30,000 annually and are scoped around volume, concurrency, telephony, integrations, security, and launch support. That makes the first qualification question practical: does your call operation need a contracted deployment, or are you still validating whether an agent can resolve a narrow queue?

Synthflow’s public site describes a visual flow designer, simulated-call testing, versioning, monitoring, APIs, CRM and contact-center integrations, and native telephony or SIP options. It also positions forward-deployed engineering, onboarding, and security review as part of its enterprise offer. These are vendor descriptions, not independently audited performance results. The pricing page does not provide a public per-minute schedule to compare with Retell’s calculator or Vapi’s estimator, so ask for a written quote with included minutes, concurrency, after-hours coverage, retention, overages, and implementation scope on the same page.

The annual minimum can be sensible for a contact center with a defined queue, an integration backlog, and a procurement need for contractual responsibility. It is poor fit for a founder testing ten appointment calls a day. A quote should name the operational boundary: what counts as a handled call, whether warm transfer time is billed, how a carrier outage is treated, what happens when an API is down, and who updates the agent after a policy change. “Enterprise-ready” is not a service-level agreement until the terms are in the contract.

AI phone agent API pricing: calculate cost per resolved call

Minutes are a weak ROI unit because a long conversation can end without doing the job. Use cost per accepted resolution instead. Start with this formula:

Monthly agent cost + carrier cost + QA and maintenance labor ÷ calls resolved without a human completing the same work.

This turns AI phone agent cost per call into a decision metric rather than a vendor rate-card number. For AI voice agent platforms, dashboard minutes are only an intermediate metric; the business metric is accepted resolutions per paid dollar.

Here is a deliberately plain example. A team runs 1,000 AI call minutes per month, averages three minutes per call, and counts 85% of 333 calls as resolved without staff finishing the task. That is about 283 accepted resolutions. If a handled case would otherwise consume six minutes of staff time at a fully loaded $25/hour, the saved labor is about $708 (283 × 6/60 × $25). The six minutes include wrap-up work, not just talk time; replace that assumption with time studies from your own queue.

At the published example rates, Vapi’s estimator puts 1,000 minutes at $82–$129 before any non-free carrier route, so the illustrative gross difference is about $579–$626 before engineering, QA, and failed-call work. Retell’s displayed $0.15 configuration would be about $150 for 1,000 minutes before any separately priced items. Bland Start would be $140 for 1,000 talk minutes, plus carrier and transfer expenses where applicable. Synthflow needs a scope-specific quote. These are not four vendor ROI claims; they are arithmetic applied to stated assumptions and the vendors’ displayed prices.

The model is fragile in ways a sales demo will not show. Count the time a person spends reviewing transcripts, correcting CRM records, calling back frustrated customers, updating prompts, and testing a new policy. Count abandoned calls and failed API actions. If the agent saves a minute but introduces a compliance review, the unit economics may still be positive, but only the pilot can show it.

The best AI voice agent for small business is often the one that answers a repetitive, low-risk queue and can hand off cleanly—not the one with the longest integration list. Start with after-hours appointment confirmation, order-status questions, or a narrow lead qualification script. Keep payments, medical advice, account closures, and exceptions with a trained human unless your process has specific controls and approval. A warm transfer is not a successful resolution if the caller has to repeat their name and reason from the beginning.

Voice agent with human handoff: the production checks buyers miss

A voice agent with human handoff needs a clear trigger, an available destination, and context that follows the call. Test explicit requests for a person, low-confidence answers, repeated misunderstandings, angry callers, and backend timeouts. The receiving agent should see the caller’s intent and the facts already collected. If the platform cannot pass that context, measure the extra handle time rather than describing the handoff as “supported.”

Run a small bake-off with the same 30–50 anonymized, representative call scenarios on each shortlisted service. Score task completion, factual accuracy against an approved answer set, correct escalation, interruption recovery, latency at the caller’s end, and cost per accepted result. Include noisy audio, names, dates, numbers, accents, code-switching, and silence. Keep a human as the fallback during the pilot; a passing transcript does not prove that an appointment was booked or an account was updated correctly.

Before production, confirm consent and recording rules for every destination, retention settings, data processing terms, access controls, and how secrets are stored. Set hard limits on outbound attempts and call duration. Restrict tools to the smallest set of actions the agent needs, validate parameters server-side, and require approval for irreversible changes. Retain a replayable audit trail for escalations and failures. Compliance badges are useful screening evidence, not a substitute for checking the contract and the specific data flow.

The platform choice should follow the operating model. A team with engineers and a desire to change component vendors may accept Vapi’s multipart usage bill. A team that wants a managed call workflow may prefer Retell. A buyer prioritizing a predictable AI rate can test Bland. A large contact center that needs a scoped rollout and procurement support can request Synthflow’s enterprise proposal. If none can beat the existing queue’s cost per successful resolution while meeting quality and safety thresholds, do not automate that queue yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Retell AI voice agent cost per minute?

Retell’s public pricing page lists $0.07–$0.31 per minute for voice agents. The final rate depends on the model, voice, telephony, and add-ons. Its displayed calculator configuration totals $0.15/minute, but that example shows telephony at zero; price your call destination and selected options separately.

Is Vapi cheaper than Retell AI?

Vapi’s $0.05/minute figure is its hosting fee, not a full voice-call total. Its calculator currently estimates $82–$129 for 1,000 minutes with a particular transcription, model, and voice combination. Retell’s calculator displays a $0.15/minute example. A fair comparison uses the same call destination, providers, concurrency, and feature set.

What does Bland AI pricing include?

Bland Start is listed at $0.14 per talk minute with no platform fee; Build is $0.12/minute plus $299/month. The AI rate includes LLM, speech recognition, and voice. Transfer minutes have their own listed price, and the pricing FAQ says carrier costs may be billed at pass-through cost or through a customer’s own telephony provider.

Does Synthflow have a free plan or public per-minute rate?

Its current public pricing page describes enterprise contracts starting at $30,000 per year and says the final scope depends on call volume, concurrency, telephony, integrations, security, and launch support. It does not publish a comparable public per-minute schedule on that page. Ask sales for a written workload-specific estimate before treating it as a shortlist fit.

Which voice agent platform is best for a small business?

Choose a service you can test without a long contract, with enough included concurrency for your peak hour and a human transfer path that preserves context. Retell and Vapi publish usage-based starting options; Bland offers a no-platform-fee Start plan. If your call volume is tiny, compare those totals with a simpler answering service before building a custom agent.

How should I compare AI voice agent ROI?

Measure cost per accepted resolution, not calls started or minutes handled. Include model and carrier charges, QA, maintenance, failed tasks, and time spent by the human team after escalation. Run the same scenarios across vendors and compare against a measured baseline from the current phone queue.

The procurement rule is simple: buy the smallest system that resolves a measurable queue, keeps a human reachable, and produces a bill you can explain. For AI voice agent platforms, a clear cost boundary and a reliable failure path matter more than a polished demo.

## When AI dubbing is still the wrong call

The contrarian take, and the one that saves people money: most content should not be dubbed at all, and some of it should be re-recorded by a human. AI dubbing is excellent for training, onboarding, product demos, and faceless channel content — neutral, informational speech with a visible speaker. It is weak in four places. Sarcasm and irony flatten out because the models optimise for tonal consistency, not comedic timing. Tonal languages like Mandarin and Thai still produce lip-sync drift under fast speech. Emotional sales pitches and testimonials come out sounding structurally correct but emotionally dead. And anything with licensed voice talent — a celebrity narrator, an actor's voice — is a legal minefield, not a technical problem. For those jobs, the professional rate of $3,000 to $10,000 per hour of content is the price of not burning a brand. The practical rule: if a bad dub costs you a client or a sponsorship, hire a human; if a slightly imperfect dub still converts, ship the AI version and move on.

## Why this category exploded in 18 months

The demand is not hype. A Blind survey of 3,617 verified US professionals found 20% had secretly used AI during a job interview. The ISE 2025 employer survey reported that 61% of the misconduct employers caught involved candidates running undisclosed AI in the interview itself. Greenhouse's 2025 report found 54% of job seekers had already hit an AI-led screening, and 65% of hiring managers said they had caught applicants reading from AI-generated scripts.

Two things drive this. First, the models got fast — sub-second latency is now normal, so the answer lands while the interviewer is still talking. Second, the tools got stealthy — desktop overlays hide from screen share, and earpiece setups feed audio with nothing visible on camera. That combination turned "practice at home" into "assist during the call," and employers noticed. Gartner found 72.4% of recruiting leaders have pulled interviews back in-person specifically to fight this.

That context matters because it changes how you should use these products. A copilot is a rehearsal amplifier, not a substitute for knowing the material. The candidates who get burned are the ones who treat it as a script.

## How an ai interview copilot works in a live call

The loop is the same across every product. The app captures system audio (or a browser tab), runs speech-to-text, sends the question to an LLM with your resume and the job description attached, and returns a structured answer. You choose the format: STAR for behavioral questions, bullet points for speed, or a full paragraph for written take-homes.

Response time is the feature that separates usable from frustrating. Interviews.chat advertises under one second. LockedIn AI reports a 116ms average on its Copilot layer. Final Round AI ships Smart Mode, which filters for genuine interview questions, and Auto Mode, which sends everything to the model. The difference is real: Smart Mode cuts the noise when small talk would otherwise trigger a wall of text.

The honest limitation is live coding. On hard algorithm problems, the suggestion cycle is slower than the interviewer's pace, and you still have to type or speak the solution. For behavioral and product rounds, where a framework like STAR is directly applicable, these tools are far stronger. That is why most buyer's remorse comes from engineers who bought a copilot expecting it to carry a whiteboard.

## Final Round AI

Final Round AI is the broadest platform in the test. It bundles live Interview CoPilot, AI Mock Interview practice, a resume builder, a question bank, and a salary calculator into one desktop app for Mac and Windows. It supports 91 languages and regional accents, which matters if you interview in a second language.

The pricing is the part most reviews get wrong. **Final round ai pricing** is three plans that differ only in billing frequency: $25/month billed annually ($300/year), $60/month billed quarterly ($180 every three months), and $90/month billed monthly. There is no permanent free plan — just a 10-minute trial. Stealth Mode, which keeps the desktop app out of screen shares, is included on the paid plans. All tiers get post-interview analytics: full transcripts, performance scores, and speech-clarity feedback.

Where it fits: candidates in a multi-month, multi-company search who want practice and live help in one subscription. The annual plan at $25/month is the real price; the $90 monthly figure competitors quote is the short-term outlier. On the head-to-head of final round ai vs interviews chat, the trade is breadth against price: Final Round bundles resume and salary tools the others charge separately for, while Interviews.chat undercuts it by $6/month and adds a third AI model.

## Interviews.chat

Interviews.chat is the cheapest entry into live help and the only tool here that runs three models at once — GPT, Claude, and Gemini. You can switch models per question, which sounds like a gimmick until you realize Claude writes cleaner behavioral structure and GPT frames technical problems better.

**Interviews.chat pricing** is credit-based: 30 free credits at signup (no card), then Starter at $19/month (1,000 credits, where one credit equals one copilot minute), Booster at $29/month (unlimited copilot time plus 1,000 credits for prep tools), and Pro at $69/quarter (3,000 credits, unlimited copilot). Credits reset monthly and do not roll over, so the Booster plan removes the depletion anxiety for heavy users.

The differentiator is the practice side. Paste a resume and a job description and it generates tailored questions, runs voice mocks with follow-ups, and hands back a transcript with a model answer. Frameworks built in: STAR, CAR, PBR, and CIRCLES for product-design questions. For a budget-conscious candidate who wants model optionality, it is the sensible first buy.

## LockedIn AI

LockedIn AI is the stealth-first option. It captures system audio across Zoom, Meet, Teams, and Webex, runs a Copilot layer that drafts answers and a Coach layer that tracks pacing and clarity in parallel, and ships a desktop app with stealth mode and VSCode/Cursor integration. It covers 42+ languages.

On cost, **lockedin ai vs final round ai** is not a clean win for either. LockedIn gives a free tier with no card, then live help from roughly $29.99 per interview or about $99/month for unlimited, with credit and Professional tiers above that. Final Round's floor is $25/month but requires the annual commitment to hit it. LockedIn's edge is flat unlimited pricing once you are interviewing across many companies; Final Round's edge is the deeper prep toolkit (resume builder, salary calculator, question bank) under one roof.

One caveat worth stating plainly: LockedIn's live mode can run through an earpiece, and several review sites flag that using live answers during a real interview crosses a line most employers treat as fraud. Use the practice and STAR-coaching mode; decide on live mode with open eyes.

## Huru

Huru is the outlier in this list because it does not assist during the call at all. It is a practice studio: unlimited mock interviews, instant AI feedback on speech, filler words, pacing, and — via webcam — eye contact and posture. It pulls a job posting from LinkedIn or Indeed with a Chrome extension and spins up a tailored mock in seconds. The library holds 50,000+ questions across 20,000+ roles.

**Huru ai interview** prep costs $24.99/month (Starter) or $99/year (Growth, about $8.25/month), with a free trial and an Enterprise tier. It runs on web, iOS, and Android, and supports English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, and French. The vendor claims a +70% hireability lift, which is a marketing number, not a measured outcome — treat it as a confidence-builder, not a guarantee.

Where it fits: anxious candidates and career changers who need repetition, not answers. If your problem is freezing up or rambling, Huru fixes that. If your problem is "I don't know the answer live," no practice tool can save you, and a copilot is the wrong purchase anyway.

## Who should actually buy one

An **ai mock interview** habit beats a live copilot for most people. If you are early in the search, rehearsing with Huru or the free practice tiers builds the muscle memory that survives a real room. Buy a live copilot only when you have a dated, high-stakes call and you already know the material.

The buyers who get value: career switchers facing behavioral loops at three companies in a month, non-native speakers rehearsing a second language, and candidates who bomb on structure (not content). The buyers who waste money: new grads who never prepared and expect the tool to supply answers, and engineers who need it to carry a hard coding screen. If you already know the material, a paid ai interview copilot is a confidence tool, not a knowledge tool — it steadies the delivery, it does not invent the answer.

Price-wise, **free ai interview tools** still ship trial tiers that are real but thin. Final Round gives 10 minutes. Interviews.chat gives 30 credits. LockedIn gives a usable free tier. None of those replace a paid plan for a real search — but they are enough to test latency and answer quality before you pay.

## The ethics problem nobody answers honestly

Here is the part most "best tools" posts skip. A live **interview copilot undetectable** to the interviewer is still disclosure you omitted. The HR Reporter piece quotes employers who now open virtual screens by asking candidates to confirm, out loud, that they are not running AI. Porter Airlines requires that attestation. Nova Scotia Pension tells candidates to prepare with AI but show up as themselves. Get caught and the usual outcome is disqualification and a note in the file.

The contrarian take: use these tools to rehearse, then perform as yourself. The 38.5% cheating flag rate means employers are actively rebuilding processes around distrust — more in-person loops, more follow-up rounds, more "show the work" tasks. A candidate who leans on live answers buys a short-term win and a long-term mismatch: you get hired for a skill you do not have, and the gap shows in week two. Practice mode is the durable edge.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is interview copilot cheating?

It depends on disclosure. Using a copilot in practice mode — mocks, feedback, transcript review — is normal preparation, the same as rehearsing with a friend. Running live, undisclosed answer generation during the actual call is what employers classify as cheating, and 61% of the misconduct they caught in the ISE 2025 survey involved exactly that. Several companies now ask for a verbal commitment not to use AI before the screen starts. The safe line: prepare with it, perform without it.

### What is the cheapest interview copilot?

Interviews.chat Starter at $19/month is the lowest paid entry, and it includes 30 free credits so you can test before paying. LockedIn AI has a free tier with no card and live help from about $29.99 per interview. Final Round AI's true floor is $25/month but only on the annual plan. If price is the only constraint and you want live help, Interviews.chat is the cheapest credible start; if you want free, use the trial tiers to rehearse, not to cheat.

### Can interview copilots be detected by employers?

Technically, stealth modes hide the window from screen share, and earpiece setups show nothing on camera. Behaviorally, the tells are obvious: a pause that runs too long, eyes drifting off-screen, an answer too polished for the candidate's earlier responses. Employers have caught on — Fabric's data shows the flag rate near 45% by late 2025, and many now follow a virtual screen with an in-person round. Detection risk is low in an unproctored call and high in any structured or proctored setting.

### Do these tools work for coding interviews?

Partially. Final Round AI and LockedIn AI have coding copilot modes that read LeetCode or HackerRank from your screen and return approach, code, and complexity notes. They help on medium problems and system-design talk. They lag on hard algorithms where the suggestion cycle is slower than the interviewer, and they do nothing for in-person whiteboards. For technical loops, pair the tool with real practice, and do not expect it to carry the round.

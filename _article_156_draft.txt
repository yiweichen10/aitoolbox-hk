## Kajabi: the all-in-one tax, priced honestly

Kajabi's pitch is consolidation: courses, email marketing, landing pages, funnels, communities, payments, and a website in one dashboard. By the end of 2024, creators had generated a cumulative $9 billion in revenue on the platform, with over $2 billion in 2024 alone, so this is not vaporware.

Kajabi pricing 2026 breaks down like this after the January restructure: Basic at $179/month ($143 annual) with 5 products and 2,500 contacts, Growth at $249/month ($199 annual) with 50 products and 25,000 contacts, and Pro at $499/month ($399 annual) with unlimited products and 100,000 contacts. There is also a Starter plan at $89/month ($71 annual) with 1 product and 250 contacts, which exists mostly to get you in the door — the 250-contact email cap will strangle any real launch.

The hidden costs are where Kajabi earns its reputation for being expensive. On Basic, the branded mobile app costs an extra $199/month (it is included on Growth), and API access runs $25/month extra. If you use your own Stripe instead of Kajabi Payments, Kajabi adds a platform surcharge — 2% on Basic, 1% on Growth, 0.5% on Pro. Basic also locks away the affiliate program, cohort courses, advanced automations, and webhooks, so the plan most solo creators actually need is Growth at $199/month annual.

What makes the math work for some creators: if you are currently paying $50/month for an email tool, $30/month for landing pages, and $40/month for course hosting, Kajabi at $143/month annual replaces all of it with unlimited email sends included. If you already run a lean stack and just need course delivery, it is expensive overkill. In any kajabi vs thinkific debate, this is the core question — consolidation versus focus — and it comes down to what your current stack costs, not what Kajabi's feature list says.

Best for: coaches and creators selling high-ticket programs ($500+) who want email, funnels, and community under one roof and will use Kajabi Payments.

Not for: anyone selling a $20 course on Basic with their own Stripe — you are stacking a 2% surcharge on an already steep subscription.

## Thinkific: the cleanest fee structure and the most useful AI

Thinkific is the course-first option: it takes course delivery, communities, and student experience seriously and leaves marketing to tools you already own. After the free plan was retired in late 2025, thinkific pricing 2026 settled at Basic $49/month ($36 annual) with unlimited courses and one community, Start $99/month ($74 annual) adding certificates, memberships, and live events, and Grow $199/month ($149 annual) with three communities, advanced analytics, and API access.

The fee situation deserves precision, because it is Thinkific's main selling point and its main trap. With Thinkific Payments, you pay 0% platform fees on every paid tier — the cleanest structure in this comparison. Connect your own Stripe and the surcharge kicks in: 5% on Basic, 2% on Start, 1% on Grow. The independent analyses of this category got it right: Basic is thin (no memberships, bundles, assignments, or certificates), and branding removal requires the $149/month Grow plan. Most serious creators land on Start at $74/month annual.

The AI story here is the most practically useful of the four. Thinker is a site-wide AI teaching assistant trained exclusively on your course content — it answers student questions, guides navigation, and syncs automatically when you edit lessons, with response limits to manage credit usage. It also handles math calculations, which matters for any technical or finance course. Thinker will not write your course for you, but it cuts student support questions substantially, and that is worth more than another AI outline generator.

Where Thinkific loses: marketing tools are weak. You will need a separate email platform — I compared the leading options in my piece on [AI email marketing tools](/articles/best-ai-email-marketing-tools-2026-mailchimp-vs-hubspot-vs-activecampaign-vs-brevo/) — and probably a funnel builder too.

Best for: creators with an existing audience who want the cheapest course platform with no transaction fees and strong communities, and who already have an email stack.

Not for: beginners who need built-in marketing — you will feel the missing funnel and email tooling immediately.

## Teachable: fast to launch, and read the fine print on fees

Teachable's checkout and student experience are genuinely polished. Teachable pricing restructured into Starter $39/month ($29 annual) with a 7.5% transaction fee and a 5-product limit, Builder $89/month ($69 annual) with 0% fees and 10 products, Growth $189/month ($139 annual) with 50 products, and Advanced $399/month.

Here is the sentence that matters: Teachable's real entry price is $89, not $39. The 7.5% Starter fee is the biggest gotcha in the category — at $5,000/month in sales you pay $39 plus $375 in fees, which is $414 monthly, more than Kajabi Basic costs. The fix is upgrading to Builder immediately, and Teachable does not make this obvious at checkout. Plenty of creators have run months on Starter paying fees that would have covered Builder twice over.

The AI features are production-focused rather than flashy. The AI Course Starter drafts curriculum outlines and landing page copy from a topic prompt. The AI Quiz Generator converts existing transcripts into quizzes, which is a real time-saver when retrofitting older content. The standout is the AI subtitle and translation tool — it transcribes and translates video into over 70 languages, which is the cheapest path I have seen to selling a course internationally without hiring localization help.

Teachable also handles global sales tax through Teachable:pay, automatically collecting and remitting VAT/GST in supported countries. If you sell to EU, UK, Australian, or Canadian customers, this alone is worth real money in accounting fees — most competitors offload that compliance work onto you.

In the teachable vs kajabi question, the honest framing: Kajabi wins on marketing depth (email, funnels, pipelines built in), Teachable wins on launch speed and tax handling, and Teachable Builder at $69/month annual is competitive with anything in the category once you are past the Starter trap.

Best for: solo creators who want to launch fast, sell globally, and will pay for Builder up front.

Not for: anyone who plans to linger on Starter — 7.5% compounds brutally.

## LearnWorlds: the serious teaching machine

LearnWorlds is the only platform in this comparison built for actual instruction design. LearnWorlds pricing runs Starter $29/month ($24 annual) with a flat $5 fee per course sale, Pro Trainer $99/month ($79 annual) with 0% fees and 5 admin seats, and Learning Center $299/month ($249 annual) adding white-labeling, SCORM compliance, and advanced automations.

The feature depth is real: an interactive video editor that overlays quizzes, buttons, and branching paths directly onto video without third-party tools, a formal assessment engine with graded file submissions, SCORM and xAPI support for accredited training, and custom certificates. Nothing else here comes close if you are running professional or compliance-driven training. In the learnworlds vs thinkific comparison, this is the core trade: LearnWorlds teaches better, Thinkific is simpler and cheaper to operate.

The $5 flat fee on Starter is deceptively friendly. It is not a percentage, but on a $20 course that is 25%, and on a $10 mini-course it is 50%. The break-even versus Pro Trainer is roughly 14 sales a month — past that, the $79 Pro Trainer plan is cheaper. Any creator with real volume should budget for Pro Trainer from day one and treat Starter as a trial with extra steps.

LearnWorlds AI works on a monthly credit system — 300 credits on Starter, 500 on Pro Trainer, scaling up from there — and generates course outlines, quiz questions, ebook drafts, and sales page copy. The Global AI Settings feature is the smartest implementation in the group: you define your brand tone, audience, and style once at the school level, and every AI generation follows those guidelines. That kills the generic-AI-voice problem that plagues course content.

Best for: professional trainers, corporate academies, and anyone teaching complex subjects where interactivity drives completion rates.

Not for: beginners who want to publish a video course in a weekend — the feature depth cuts both ways.

The second-order question is what you would spend elsewhere. Kajabi's $199 includes email marketing with unlimited sends, funnel building, and website hosting. Thinkific's $74 assumes you bring an email tool (budget $15-50/month) and accept weaker landing pages. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cheapest course platform with no transaction fees?

Thinkific Basic at $49/month ($36 annual) is the cheapest paid plan with 0% transaction fees when using Thinkific Payments — and it includes unlimited courses. The catch: connecting your own Stripe triggers a 5% surcharge on Basic. LearnWorlds Pro Trainer ($79/month) also has 0% fees, as does Teachable Builder ($89/month). Every "free" or near-free plan in this category (Teachable Starter, LearnWorlds Starter, Podia Mover) charges fees that make it more expensive than these tiers once you sell consistently.

### Is Kajabi worth $179 a month?

It depends on what it replaces. If you currently pay for an email platform ($30-50/month), a landing page builder ($30/month), course hosting ($40/month), and community software ($30-100/month), Kajabi Basic at $143/month annual can net out cheaper. If you only need course hosting, it is 3-4x more expensive than Thinkific or Teachable for the same core job. The January 2026 price increase — with no grandfathering — pushed several long-time users to migrate, so check what Growth actually costs against your current stack before committing.

### Teachable vs Kajabi: which handles international sales better?

Teachable, narrowly. Teachable:pay automatically calculates and remits VAT/GST in supported countries, which removes a genuine compliance burden for EU/UK/AU/CA sales. Kajabi offers partial tax collection through Kajabi Payments but expects you to handle more of the compliance work. Both beat Thinkific's partial support and LearnWorlds' limited non-EU handling. If international tax compliance is a deciding factor, Teachable Builder at $89/month is the strongest combination of fee-free selling and automatic tax handling.

### Can an AI course builder actually write my course for me?

Not the parts that matter. All four platforms generate outlines, quiz questions, and sales copy — useful for beating the blank page, but the generated content is generic until you inject your own expertise and examples. The more valuable AI features in 2026 are operational: Thinkific's Thinker answering student questions from your content, Teachable's subtitle translation into 70+ languages, and LearnWorlds' brand-consistent AI settings. Treat AI as a production assistant for drafts and student support, not a course writer.

### Do any of these platforms still have a free plan?

No. Teachable removed its free plan in 2025, Thinkific retired its free plan in late 2025, and Kajabi and LearnWorlds never offered one — only trials (14 days for Kajabi and Teachable, 30 days for LearnWorlds). If you need a true $0 start, Systeme.io's free tier or a marketplace like Udemy (which takes 37-63% revenue share instead of a subscription) are the remaining options, both with significant trade-offs in ownership and control.

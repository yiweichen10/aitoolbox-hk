Amateur astronomers use Codex and Astra to flag exoplanet candidates

OpenAI engineer Kevin Bond used Codex to search about 100,000 Kepler stars, flagging over 200 signals including 10 known planets; a SETI Institute astronomer independently found the same two candidates, one a dip every 13.8 days. Separately, Ethan Byrd used Astra on data from roughly 19 million stars and produced 49 candidates, sending his strongest to NASA's TESS team for analysis.

Why it matters

The result is not a new planet but a reproducible workflow: an agent compared published detection methods, combined them into a pipeline and cross-checked its own candidates. That let two amateurs revisit archives already mined for obvious transits and hand professionals a shorter list. The bottleneck has moved from pattern matching to confirmation, which can take months or years of follow-up observations, so the value of these tools depends on how well their candidates survive independent scrutiny.

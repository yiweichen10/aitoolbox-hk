Sakana AI releases Namazu Small 30B reasoning model built on Nemotron 3 Nano

Sakana AI built Namazu Small, a 30B-A3B reasoning model, on NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Nano pretrained base using its own continued pretraining and post-training. Across 25 benchmarks it averages 60.1, beating the 120B Nemotron 3 Super at 56.2 and the Nano baseline at 50.2. BrowseComp reaches 73.5 versus 36.1, while SimpleQA Verified falls to 6.8.

WhyFor teams weighing open models, the result shows post-training can matter more than raw scale: a 30B model tuned for web research and long documents outperformed a four-times-larger Nemotron on Sakana's suite. The tradeoff is clear. Search-heavy tasks improve sharply, but closed-book knowledge regresses, so buyers should match the model to retrieval-heavy workflows rather than treat the headline average as universal.

Nvidia pauses server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia

Citing a Wall Street Journal report, Nvidia told manufacturing partners including Dell and Super Micro to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for an unspecified period while it checks compliance with U.S. export rules. Those markets became a route for remote access to Nvidia chips from China; the Institute for Progress estimates such data centers supply about 24% of China's AI computing power.

WhyThe move turns export enforcement into a logistics problem for server vendors: shipments stop before regulators name a specific violation. For cloud buyers in the region, the risk is contract disruption rather than a formal ban, since the pause has no stated end date. The 24% estimate explains why Washington treats remote compute as strategically equivalent to shipping the hardware itself.

Scott Aaronson says AI math proofs matter more than the Riemann result

Scott Aaronson, a complexity theorist and former OpenAI researcher, argues that AI's math breakthroughs matter less for their results than for the capability they demonstrate. He highlights OpenAI proofs that add matching lower bounds to faster matrix multiplication and Fourier transform algorithms, showing where speedups stop. He says he now believes a proof of P ≠ NP is near and claims AI labs hold undisclosed cryptography bombshells.

WhyAaronson's emphasis on lower bounds is the useful distinction: a faster algorithm sets a record, but a proof that no method can do substantially better closes a line of inquiry. That is a different kind of research output from simply answering a famous conjecture. His cryptography claim remains second-hand and unverified, so the defensible takeaway concerns how AI changes proof production, not any specific broken system.

OpenAI patches billing bug that changed GPT-6.2 usage limits

A billing bug during the GPT-6.2 rollout gave subscribers incorrect discounts, OpenAI Codex engineer Thibault Sottiaux said. After the fix, Astra users reach usage limits 40% faster and Sol users 25% faster than last week, though quotas still last longer than on GPT-6. Go, Plus, Pro and Business subscribers get an extra saved reset; Business users also receive free Fast Mode through year-end, with no retroactive charges.

WhyThe episode shows how pricing errors can quietly become capacity errors: discounts lowered effective revenue per token while usage stayed high, so correcting the discount exposed the real limit curve. A reset and free Fast Mode buy goodwill, but they also test whether quota communication can keep pace with rapid plan changes. For heavy users, checking announced limits against actual behavior is now a practical habit.

Grand Theft Auto VI sold $3.79B on day one without generative AI

Grand Theft Auto VI earned $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales and became the highest-rated game on Metacritic after a 13-year wait, according to TechCrunch. Rockstar built it without generative AI, with about 5,000 people working for more than a decade and 30,000 distinct nonplayable characters. Its launch coincided with Anthropic's record $81.3 billion IPO.

WhyThe comparison is less about games versus AI than about what gets counted as waste. Rockstar's repeated rework and delays produced detail that prompt-driven generation cannot easily replicate, while the industry's efficiency story rewards removing exactly that trial and error. For studios, the practical constraint is that generation can compress production without guaranteeing the depth that made this launch a cultural event.

42 state AGs seek to block Character.AI chat sale in bankruptcy

A bipartisan coalition of 42 state attorneys general is seeking to block Character.AI from auctioning user chat histories during Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The proposed sale covers more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including over 2.1 million registered to minors, with no requirement that a buyer honor existing privacy policies or let users delete data. Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October.

WhyBankruptcy is testing whether chat logs are treated as assets or as private records with continuing obligations. The AGs' argument hinges on the absence of deletion rights and privacy commitments for a buyer, not on the fact of a sale itself. For chatbot users, the case makes deletion policies as important as model behavior; for acquirers, it signals that conversational datasets carry legal baggage beyond the purchase price.

TheoPhysEval finds AI agents solve 49 of 50 physics problems

TheoPhysEval gives AI agents 50 unpublished, research-level theoretical physics problems that a world expert typically needs about a day to solve. GPT-6-Astra running in Codex answered 49 of 50 correctly after a single human prompt, at less than $3 of compute per correct answer. Removing web search and background papers changed accuracy only slightly, which the authors read as evidence of memorized literature.

WhyA 49-of-50 score on verifiable calculations says more about agents as research assistants than about autonomous discovery: the problems are hard but have checkable answers, and near-identical results without web access point to training-data recall. Theoretical physicists gain a tool for routine derivations, yet the paper explicitly notes that agent-led breakthroughs remain absent. Benchmarks like this make that gap measurable instead of rhetorical.

OpenAI and Cisco open-source Shirudo for live Linux defense

OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, a BSD-2-Clause eBPF runtime that lets approved AI agents deploy signed, temporary shields on running Linux systems while permanent patches are tested. A Codex-generated guard blocked a Java4Shell exploit against Elasticsearch 9.5.5 without a restart. Shields require a patched bpf-next kernel and TPM 2.0 attestation, and the project remains pre-release.

WhyShirudo attacks the patch window, when a known exploit and a legitimate request can share the same kernel path. Requiring signed, TPM-attested shields and operator approval offers a middle ground between blocking a service and leaving it exposed. The steep requirements, including a patched kernel, limit early adoption to teams that control their infrastructure and can validate a shield against real workloads.

DeepMind agent teams cut quantum algorithm costs using Lean proofs

DeepMind used teams of Gemini 4 agents to optimize quantum algorithms, proving each circuit correct with the Lean theorem prover and open-sourcing the library. For inertial confinement fusion, agents cut computing cost 11.8-fold and memory from 5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits. They also found a 290-fold speedup for arcsin and 2.5-fold for Cytochrome P450 simulations.

Why it matters

The method's value is less any single speedup than the proof step: Lean verification makes agent-proposed circuits checkable when hardware cannot test them. That turns AI search into something quantum researchers can trust without waiting for fault-tolerant machines. The 290-fold arcsin gain is an arithmetic routine, not an end-to-end application, so the leap to drug design still depends on resources no early error-corrected computer can supply.

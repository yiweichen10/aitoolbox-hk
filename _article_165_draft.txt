The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects roughly 124,200 accounting and auditing openings a year in the United States through 2034, while CPA exam candidates fell from 49,597 in 2016 to 28,082 in 2024 — a 43% drop. Intuit's response, effective August 1, 2026, was to raise three QuickBooks Online plans by 13% to 24% and hang a set of narrowly named AI agents on them. That is the state of AI accounting software in 2026: the automation is real, the money is real, and almost nobody has been told which tier holds which robot.

I read every vendor pricing page, the August and October price-change notices, and the AI feature gates for the four platforms a small business actually considers. Here is the honest version.

## The August 2026 price change most small businesses haven't noticed yet

Two price events landed inside 90 days. Both are permanent.

QuickBooks Online moved on renewals dated August 1, 2026 or later. Simple Start stayed at $38 a month. Essentials went from $75 to $85. Plus went from $115 to $140. Advanced went from $275 to $340. Over a year that is $120, $300 and $780 more for the same login, justified as investment in automation.

Xero US raises prices on October 1, 2026: Early from $25 to $27, Growing from $55 to $59, Established from $90 to $97. Softer in percentage terms, roughly 7% to 8%, on the same generic justification.

The pattern worth noticing is the promo-versus-renewal gap. A business signing up for QuickBooks Plus today pays $70 a month for three months, then $140 for nine: $1,470 in year one. A business that has sat on Plus since 2023 pays $1,680 — $210 more for the identical product, and $510 more on Advanced. Promo is cash flow. The renewal rate is the budget line.

## What AI accounting software actually automates in 2026

Strip the branding and every one of these platforms sells the same four jobs: transaction coding, bank reconciliation, document capture, anomaly checking. The differences are where each vendor draws the paywall and how honestly it labels a beta.

What is working in production:

- **Transaction coding with pattern learning.** Zoho's Zia and Intuit's categorization engine learn vendor-to-account mappings from your own history. Accuracy is mediocre in month one and meaningfully better by month three.
- **Document capture.** Xero includes Smart Document Capture on every plan, QuickBooks captures receipts on all tiers, FreshBooks from Plus upward. This is optical character recognition, not reasoning, and it is the most reliably useful feature in the stack.
- **Reconciliation suggestions.** Xero lists auto-reconcile as beta on all plans; QuickBooks markets reconciliation intelligence from Plus up.
- **Anomaly detection.** QuickBooks Plus flags likely errors in the profit and loss and balance sheet. This is the one feature that behaves like a second pair of eyes rather than a faster keyboard.

What is not working: open-ended reasoning that fires on its own. Intuit's connector inside Claude and ChatGPT reads reports and, since July 28, 2026, creates invoices. Meridian by Pilot shipped a free hosted connection in August 2026 with read-write access across multiple companies, journal entries included. Both have the same ceiling: they act only inside a conversation you start. Intuit's pitch is that automated bookkeeping arrives with the higher tier; what arrives is a faster lookup that waits for you.

## QuickBooks Online in 2026: the AI is bundled, the price isn't gentle

QuickBooks pricing in 2026 is $38, $85, $140 and $340 a month across four tiers, with 1, 3, 5 and 25 users plus two accountant seats, three on Advanced.

The AI gating runs opposite to what the announcement language suggests. The tier that absorbed no increase — Simple Start at $38 — carries the open-ended assistant: AI chat for instant insights, beta with limited capacity, plus expert-guided setup, also beta. The tiers that did absorb the increase bought specific named agents. Essentials adds Accounting AI and Payments AI. Plus adds Sales Tax AI, Customer AI, reconciliation insight and anomaly detection. Advanced adds Finance AI and Project Management AI, still beta.

Advanced now bundles Bill Pay Elite, previously $90 a month standalone and cut to $45 in June 2026 before being folded in. Intuit values that at $540 a year, plus around $900 for construction and professional-services tooling. For a contractor already paying for both, the 23.6% increase is partly self-funding. For a five-person agency that never touches job costing, it is not.

One thing is free on every tier: connecting QuickBooks to Claude or ChatGPT, because Intuit built the connector itself. If your only goal was asking your books questions in plain English, the $38 plan already does it.

## Xero in 2026: flat pricing per company, seats that stop being a decision

Xero pricing in the US is $25 for Early, $55 for Growing and $90 for Established per month, rising to $27, $59 and $97 on October 1, 2026. Outside the US the same tiers sell as Starter, Standard and Premium — same product, different labels, which is why comparison tables disagree with each other.

The structural argument is seats. Every plan includes unlimited users. QuickBooks caps Plus at five and forces a $200-a-month jump to Advanced for the sixth login. For a five-person shop with an outside bookkeeper, that beats any AI feature on this list. For a team of eight, it stops being a comparison and becomes a conclusion.

Read the caps before buying. Early allows 20 invoices or quotes and 5 bills a month, and connected apps can eat into that limit. Upgrade for one busy month and Xero requires a month on the higher plan before downgrading, so one spike costs two months at the higher rate.

Xero JAX is included on every plan with no separate price, rolling out first in Australia and then the US. The no-code agent builder, XeroForce, is in early access with general availability promised later in 2026. Document chasing arrives in October, payment follow-ups are still coming, bill protection and cash flow actions sit in beta. Treat the roadmap row as a roadmap, not a feature.

Payroll is the US weakness. There is no native engine; Xero Payroll runs on Gusto at $36 a month plus $6 per employee, on top of the subscription.

## Zoho Books: the only free tier that is actually accounting

Zoho Books pricing runs $0, $20, $50, $70, $150 and $275 a month. The free tier is the unusual part — a real double-entry ledger, not an invoice tracker, for businesses under $50,000 in revenue, with one user plus an accountant and a 1,000-invoice annual cap.

Zia is not gated by tier the way Intuit gates its agents, which makes Zoho the cheapest AI bookkeeping software in this comparison. It creates invoices from natural language, categorizes transactions during reconciliation, runs optical capture on receipts, detects anomalies, and produces forecasts.

The trade-offs are real: third-party integrations are thinner than QuickBooks' 750-plus or Xero's 1,000-plus, support is 24/5 rather than 24/7, and multi-currency and inventory sit at $70 and above. If you already run Zoho CRM, the native data flow is worth more than the discount. If you live in a non-Zoho stack, price the integrations before celebrating the savings.

## FreshBooks: the best invoicing here, and the weakest AI

FreshBooks pricing changed on March 5, 2026: Lite $23 a month or $248.40 a year, Plus $43 or $464.40, Premium $70 or $756. Annual billing carries a 10% discount. Team members cost $11 each a month, Advanced Payments $20, payroll $40 plus $6 per user.

The AI position is the honest weak spot: no named assistant, no conversational query layer, no anomaly detection. What exists is automated expense categorization, late-payment reminders, and receipt capture from Plus upward. Independent 2026 reviews rate FreshBooks' AI innovation lowest of the four, and they are right to.

That does not make it a bad buy. Client caps are the ladder: Lite stops at five billable clients, Plus at 50, Premium unlimited. For a solo consultant sending 15 invoices a month who wants the cleanest client-facing experience, the missing AI costs nothing. Anyone with inventory, multi-entity books or a wish to query their own data should look elsewhere.

## Where the AI pays off and where it is theater

The honest test for AI accounting software is hours, not features. A workflow audit of small accounting practices found 8 to 15 hours per person per week disappearing into document chasing, manual coding, rekeying between systems, and routine client emails. For a five-person team at a $50 loaded hourly rate, recovering three hours per person per week is 60 hours a month, or $3,000 of recovered capacity.

Against that, the gap between Xero Early at $25 and QuickBooks Advanced at $340 is $3,780 a year, and between Zoho Free and FreshBooks Plus it is $516. The subscription decision is a rounding error next to whether the software removes the coding work from a human's week. Pick the platform you will maintain, then the cheapest tier that does coding and reconciliation.

The features that do not survive that test: conversational business intelligence you will ask three questions of and ignore; industry benchmarking for a business with two competitors; and any AI sold as a replacement for a bookkeeper when you have no review process behind it. McKinsey's estimate that 60% of bookkeeping tasks are automatable is about the task, not your chart of accounts on day one.

## The hidden cost stack nobody quotes in the comparison table

1. **Payroll.** Xero routes US payroll through Gusto at $36 a month plus $6 per employee. FreshBooks charges $40 plus $6 per user. QuickBooks bundles part of it and sells the rest. None of it sits in the price you compared.
2. **Payment processing.** FreshBooks charges 2.9% + $0.30 on standard cards, 3.5% + $0.30 on commercial and Amex. Xero includes free ACH for domestic bill payments. Processing never gets a promo rate.
3. **Seats.** Xero's unlimited users are the largest structural saving in this group. On QuickBooks, count the humans before comparing anything else.
4. **Migration.** Moving two years of misclassified history is labour, not a checkbox. Fix the chart of accounts first.

## Which one to buy

There is no single best accounting software for small business, and any table claiming otherwise is selling something. The split looks like this.

**Solo under $50K revenue, no employees:** Zoho Books Free. Real ledger, $0, and $20 a month when you outgrow it.

**Solo consultant, 10 to 20 invoices a month:** FreshBooks Plus at $43. Best client experience of the four, and you will not miss AI you never used. It is the strongest of the QuickBooks alternatives in that narrow lane.

**Three to eight people, service business, outside bookkeeper:** Xero Growing at $55. Unlimited seats decide this before features get a vote. Add Gusto and budget the October increase.

**US business whose accountant opens the file without complaining, or with inventory and assemblies:** QuickBooks Essentials at $85, or Plus at $140 for reconciliation intelligence and sales tax AI. Check your renewal date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is QuickBooks or Xero better for a small business in 2026?

Seats decide it, not features. QuickBooks vs Xero turns on user count: Xero includes unlimited users on every plan, while QuickBooks caps Plus at five and forces Advanced at $340 a month for a sixth login. Below three users, QuickBooks wins on accountant familiarity and native payroll. Above five, Xero's flat pricing is hard to argue with.

### How much does QuickBooks cost after the August 2026 increase?

QuickBooks pricing as of September 2026 is $38 for Simple Start, $85 for Essentials, $140 for Plus and $340 for Advanced, applying to renewals on or after August 1, 2026. Annual totals: $456, $1,020, $1,680 and $4,080. Simple Start was excluded from the increase, and new customers can still take 50% off the first three months.

### Does Xero have a free plan or a free trial?

No free tier. Xero runs a 30-day trial with no credit card, plus 90% off for six months on US plans — currently $2.50, $5.50 and $9 a month — then the standard rate, which rises to $27, $59 and $97 on October 1, 2026.

### Can an AI agent post entries directly into QuickBooks?

Yes, and it is free. Intuit publishes a hosted connector inside Claude and ChatGPT that reads reports and creates invoices. Meridian by Pilot ships a separate free hosted connection with read-write access across multiple companies, including journal entries. Both act only when prompted, and both need a human review step for journal entries and payments.

### Is Zoho Books good enough to replace QuickBooks?

Zoho Books pricing starts at $0 and reaches $70 for the plan most small businesses need, against $1,680 a year for QuickBooks Plus at five users. The catch is ecosystem: the third-party integration list is shorter, so verify your payment processor, bank feed and e-commerce connection first. If those clear, Zoho is the better value and the AI comes included rather than tier-gated.

## Bottom line

The AI in AI accounting software got real in 2026, and the pricing got less honest in the same year. Intuit raised Advanced by 23.6% and put its best agent behind that tier. Xero raised everything by roughly 8% and kept its assistant free across the board. Zoho gave it away on a free plan. FreshBooks mostly skipped it.

Three things this week: find your actual renewal rate rather than the promo rate; count the humans who need a login, because that number decides QuickBooks versus Xero; and stop shopping for an assistant you will talk to twice a month. Buy the tier that codes transactions and reconciles the bank, keep a review step, and spend the recovered hours on the clients who pay you.

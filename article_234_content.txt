Embedding API Pricing 2026: OpenAI vs Voyage vs Cohere vs Jina Compared

The same 1 billion tokens costs $20 to embed on OpenAI and $150 on Google — a 7.5x spread for mathematically identical work. Embedding API pricing in 2026 is easy to read and easy to get wrong: every model bills input tokens only, because an embedding call returns a vector and never generates text. The per-token rates across the six vendors here run from $0.02 to $0.20 per million, batch discounts swing between 33% and 50%, and the free tiers range from genuinely useful (Voyage) to non-commercial-only (Jina).

Embedding models are a one-way door: switch later and you re-embed the whole corpus, pay the indexing bill again, then re-validate every retrieval test. This guide prices six official rate cards, works bills at four corpus sizes, and flags the free tiers with traps in the footnotes.

Three things stand out. Embeddings carry no output charge anywhere — the bill is input tokens only. The two $0.02 models (OpenAI 3-small and Voyage's voyage-4-lite) define the floor for managed access; everything else is a premium play. The free tiers differ in kind: Voyage grants a one-time 200M-token allowance per current-gen model, Google runs a rate-limited no-charge tier, Jina's allocation forbids commercial use, and Cohere's trial key bars production.

## OpenAI Embedding Pricing: Still the $0.02 Default

OpenAI's lineup is unchanged. text-embedding-3-small costs $0.02 per million tokens (1,536 dimensions, truncation to 512 via Matryoshka), text-embedding-3-large costs $0.13 (3,072 dimensions, truncation to 256), and both cap input at 8,191 tokens. The Batch API halves both rates — $0.01 and $0.065 — for jobs that tolerate a 24-hour window. There is no embedding free tier; OpenAI is the only headline vendor without one. Azure matches the rates but drops the batch discount — strictly worse for bulk indexing.

OpenAI embedding pricing has barely moved since the 3-series launched, and the legacy text-embedding-ada-002 still bills at $0.10 per million — five times 3-small's rate while scoring lower on retrieval benchmarks. If you are still on ada-002, re-embedding a 1B-token index with 3-small costs $20 standard, $10 through Batch, after which every future run bills at 80% less.

Is 3-large worth it? text-embedding-3-large pricing at $0.13 buys a 2-3 point edge on MTEB retrieval over 3-small (roughly 64.6 vs 62.3 on mid-2026 leaderboard aggregations) and doubles vector storage to about 12KB per vector — a 6.5x premium for a margin most RAG pipelines cannot measure in answer quality. Pay it for legal, medical, or multilingual corpora where recall misses are expensive; skip it elsewhere and spend the difference on a reranker.

## Voyage AI Pricing: The 200M-Token Head Start

Voyage's rate card looks ordinary until you read the free-token line. voyage-4 costs $0.06 per million tokens, voyage-4-lite $0.02, and voyage-4-large, voyage-context-4, and voyage-code-4 each $0.12. On every current-generation model the first 200 million tokens are free — a one-time allowance, not a monthly grant; Batch usage does not touch it because free credits exclude Batch.

Voyage AI pricing therefore zeroes out the indexing bill for most solo-operator corpora: 200M tokens is roughly 150 million words, larger than most SaaS documentation sets and help centers combined. voyage-4-lite's $0.02 rate also ties OpenAI 3-small as the cheapest managed per-token price on the market.

The trade-offs: Batch gets a 33% discount (12-hour window), thinner than OpenAI or Google's 50%, and the 200M allowance is consumed once — a nightly re-index pipeline burns through it in the first week. The multimodal line bills $0.12 per million text tokens plus $0.60 per billion pixels on voyage-multimodal-3.5: $0.0006 for a 1MP image, capped at $0.0012 up to 4MP. Rerank-3 ($0.05) and rerank-3-lite ($0.02) per million tokens slot into the same stack.

## Cohere Embed 5 Pricing: Two Models, One Vector Space

Cohere rebuilt its embedding line on September 30, 2026. Embed 5 ships as two models sharing one embedding space: Embed 5 Pro (embed-v5.0-pro) at $0.12 per million text tokens and Embed 5 Fast (embed-v5.0-fast) at $0.08. Both take text and image input — images bill at $0.40 per million tokens on either tier — with a 128K-token context, 256–2,048 output dimensions, and float, int8, or binary formats. The previous Embed 4 (still in the docs at $0.12 text / $0.47 image) has quietly vanished from the pricing page.

The shared vector space changes cost math. Because Pro and Fast produce compatible vectors, index the corpus once with Pro — the quality-critical, run-once step — and serve live queries with Fast, the cheap, latency-sensitive step. Cohere embed 5 pricing makes the split cheap: a 1B-token corpus indexed on Pro costs $120 once, then each million tokens of queries costs $0.08 forever. No other vendor offers this.

Cohere embed 5 pricing has no published batch discount, and access tilts enterprise: new accounts get a free trial key that is rate-limited and not licensed for production; production keys run pay-as-you-go after a billing workflow. Model Vault dedicated instances start at $3.00/hour (small, $2,000/month) or $5.00/hour (medium, $3,250/month).

On quality, Cohere's own ViDoRe V3 evaluation — scored with its own RCP-nDCG@10 metric — reports 85.8 for Pro and 84.5 for Fast, ahead of Voyage 4 Large (83.7), Gemini Embedding 2 (83.2), and OpenAI 3-large (75.5). Vendor-reported numbers on a vendor metric: treat the ranking as a claim to test on your corpus. One storage illustration: 100 million chunks at 2,048 dimensions in float32 occupy roughly 819GB of raw vectors; 256-dimension binary output compresses the same index to about 3.2GB, recovering the lost precision with a reranker.

## Jina Embeddings Pricing: Prepaid Bundles, One Flat Rate

Jina does not meter. It sells prepaid token bundles: $50 buys 1B tokens ($0.050 per million), $500 buys 11B ($0.045), and $5,000 buys 110B at the same $0.045 rate. One rate covers every model: the v5-omni embeddings that pack text, images, audio, video, and PDFs into one vector space cost the same per token as the smallest text-only model, with 8K-32K context windows and Matryoshka truncation to 128 dimensions.

Jina embeddings pricing reads cheap at $0.045 until you remember the bundle is a capital purchase, not a meter: buy $500 and you carry 11B tokens on the balance sheet whether you use them in three months or three years. Rate limits gate the tiers — free keys run 100 RPM and 100K TPM, paid keys 500 RPM and 2M TPM — and the free "Toy Experiment" allocation of 10M tokens is non-commercial (CC-BY-NC): prototype validation only, never production.

Two structural notes: the deprecation cycle is fast — v3 is retired in favor of v4 and the v5 generation, so adopting Jina means budgeting for a future re-embed — and Elastic acquired the company in October 2025; it now operates under Elastic's data processing terms with SOC 2 compliance.

## Gemini Embedding Pricing: The Only Real Free Tier, the Shortest Context

Google runs two embedding SKUs. gemini-embedding-001, the text-only workhorse, bills $0.15 per million tokens ($0.075 batch), outputs 3,072-dimension vectors with Matryoshka truncation, and tops mid-2026 MTEB multilingual aggregations at 68.32 — the strongest managed-model benchmark number here. Its catch is context: 2,048 tokens per input, the shortest window among headline models.

Gemini Embedding 2 adds multimodal input at $0.20 per million text tokens, $0.45 for images, $6.50 for audio, and $12.00 for video ($0.10 batch on text) — the only rate card that prices audio and video input directly. On Cohere's ViDoRe V3 table it scores 83.2.

Gemini embedding pricing is the odd one out: the most expensive headline rates in this comparison, attached to the only genuinely free tier. Google's Gemini API lists both embedding models as free of charge on its free tier — rate-limited, but at no token cost. For a prototype or low-traffic tool, that undercuts every paid option to zero. Read the tier terms before pointing production traffic at it; the rate limits make the free tier a poor fit for bulk indexing at any real corpus size.

## Mistral Embed Pricing and the Self-Host Escape Hatch

[Mistral AI](/tools/mistral-ai/) keeps one foot in each camp. Mistral Embed bills $0.10 per million tokens (1,024 dimensions, 8,192-token context) with a cached-input rate of $0.01 — unusual for an embedding endpoint — and Codestral Embed prices code retrieval at $0.15. Neither has a free tier or a published batch discount. The draw is European hosting for EU data-residency needs.

The open-weight route changes the comparison. Qwen3-Embedding-8B (Apache 2.0) posts a 70.58 MTEB multilingual score on mid-2026 aggregations — above every managed model in this guide — and BGE-M3 (MIT) remains the standard multilingual workhorse; both run locally through [Ollama](/tools/ollama/) on a single 16GB GPU. Marginal token cost drops to zero and the data never leaves the building. Against 3-small's $0.02, self-hosting only breaks even above tens of millions of tokens per month or when vectors legally cannot touch a third-party API.

The spread at 1B tokens is 7.5x between OpenAI and Google standard rates — before storage. A 1B-token corpus at 3,072 dimensions occupies about 12GB of raw float32 vectors; at 1,024 dimensions, 4GB. Embedding cost per million tokens is only half the number that matters: dimensions decide what your vector database bills every month, so the [vector database comparison](/articles/best-vector-databases-2026-pinecone-vs-qdrant-vs-weaviate-vs-zilliz-milvus/) belongs in the same decision as the model choice. On [Qdrant](/tools/qdrant/) or any self-managed store, halving dimensions via Matryoshka usually costs less retrieval quality than the storage it saves.

## Free Tiers, Fine Print, and the Lock-In Problem

| Vendor | Free allowance | Commercial use? | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voyage | 200M tokens per current-gen model | yes | one-time, not monthly; Batch excluded |
| Google Gemini API | both embedding models free of charge | yes, rate-limited | rate limits cap bulk indexing; check tier terms |
| Jina | 10M tokens | no — CC-BY-NC only | prototype use only |
| Cohere | trial key | no | not licensed for production |
| OpenAI | none | — | the floor is $0.02, paid |

Pricing pages never mention lock-in, so here it is. Every dollar above is a bill you pay again — in full — the day you switch models. Re-embedding 1B tokens on 3-small costs $20, but the real cost is re-running retrieval evaluations on vectors that moved. The single exception is Cohere's Embed 5, where Pro and Fast share a space and query-time models swap freely; everything else is a full rebuild. Dimension choice compounds this: 3,072-dim vectors double the storage bill of 1,536-dim vectors for the life of the index — the dimension you pick at creation is a recurring cost, not a one-time one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cheapest embedding API in 2026?

By sticker rate, OpenAI text-embedding-3-small and Voyage voyage-4-lite tie at $0.02 per million tokens — the cheapest embedding API rates among managed per-token vendors. Free options reorder it: Voyage's one-time 200M-token allowance embeds that scale at $0 total, and Google's rate-limited free tier prices both Gemini models at no charge for light traffic. The cheapest embedding API for a bounded corpus is Voyage with the free allowance; for ongoing volume, the $0.02 tie, settled by whichever model retrieves better on your data.

### Is there an embedding API free tier worth using?

Three, with different terms. Voyage grants the first 200M tokens free on each current-generation model — one-time, commercial use allowed, Batch excluded. Google's Gemini API free tier prices gemini-embedding-001 and Embedding 2 at no charge, subject to rate limits that make bulk indexing impractical. Jina's 10M tokens are non-commercial only. An embedding API free tier with commercial rights (Voyage, Google) is worth using; the others are evaluation sandboxes.

### How much does a 100M-token RAG corpus cost to embed?

At standard rates: $2 on OpenAI 3-small or voyage-4-lite, $6 on voyage-4, $8 on Cohere Embed 5 Fast, $12 on Embed 5 Pro, $10 on Mistral Embed, and $15 on gemini-embedding-001 ($7.50 batch). If the corpus fits inside Voyage's 200M-token free allowance, the bill is zero. The recurring cost is not the index — it is monthly increments plus queries: re-embedding a churned 5% of a 100M corpus each month costs $0.10-$1.50 depending on model, and query embeddings bill at the standard rate because batch windows cannot serve live traffic.

### Is text-embedding-3-large worth 6.5x the price of 3-small?

Usually not. The rate gap is $0.13 vs $0.02 per million tokens, the MTEB retrieval gap on mid-2026 aggregations is about 2-3 points, and 3-large doubles storage to roughly 12KB per vector. For general RAG, 3-small plus a reranker beats 3-large alone on cost-adjusted quality. Pay the premium when retrieval misses carry real cost: regulated legal or medical corpora, rare-language pairs, precision-critical deduplication. Benchmark on a 500-query sample from your own corpus first; leaderboard deltas do not transfer automatically.

### Can I switch embedding models without re-embedding everything?

No, with one exception. Embedding models produce incompatible vector spaces — a corpus embedded with 3-small is meaningless to voyage-4 or Embed 5 Pro, so switching means re-embedding every document and re-validating retrieval. The exception is inside Cohere's Embed 5 family: Pro and Fast share one embedding space, so you can index with Pro and query with Fast, or swap query-time models, without touching the index. Pick the model that retrieves best on your data, not the best sticker price — switching costs a second full indexing bill plus re-evaluation work.

## The Bottom Line on Embedding API Pricing

Four verdicts. Boring default: OpenAI text-embedding-3-small at $0.02 — mature tooling, batch at half price, no free tier, no surprises. Corpus under 200M tokens: voyage-4 or voyage-4-lite on Voyage's free allowance embeds it for $0, continuing at $0.02 after the allowance burns. Quality is the product: index on Cohere Embed 5 Pro at $0.12, serve queries on Embed 5 Fast at $0.08, and keep the option to swap models without re-indexing. Inputs are not plain text: Gemini Embedding 2 prices audio and video directly, and Jina's flat-rate v5-omni bundles five media types under one rate.

One rule cuts across all of it: the embedding bill is the smallest line item in a RAG budget, so never let it pick the model. Generation costs dwarf embeddings — the frontier rates in our [LLM API pricing guide](/articles/best-llm-api-pricing-2026-openai-gpt-6-vs-claude-opus-5-5-vs-gemini-4-vs-deepseek-compared/) run tens of dollars per million tokens against embeddings' cents — and agents that search live sources add per-call costs tracked in our [search API pricing comparison](/articles/ai-search-api-pricing-2026-tavily-vs-exa-vs-brave-vs-serpapi/). A 7.5x spread on the cheapest component means nothing if the wrong vectors sink recall: run a retrieval eval on 500 real queries, pick the model that wins, then price it — in that order.

SMS API Pricing 2026: Twilio vs Telnyx vs Vonage vs Bird

Sending 50,000 US OTP verifications through Twilio Verify costs about $3,115 per month at list price — eight times what identical traffic costs through Bird. That spread is the published reality of SMS API pricing in November 2026, not a promo. The reason is structural: carrier fees are set by carriers and charged identically no matter which API you pick, so the entire difference is platform margin. This guide prices four providers from their own rate cards, works the OTP and notification bills at four volumes, and maps where the 2026 market has split — a premium tier selling convenience, a discount tier selling the same network at half the price, and one notable defection from self-serve SMS altogether.

Every figure comes from the vendors' own pricing pages (Twilio's sheet is dated October 2026; Telnyx's and Bird's were live on November 27, 2026), plus labeled third-party tracking where a vendor hides rates behind a console. Where sources conflict, I say so.

## The Three-Layer Bill: Platform, Carrier, and Fixed Fees

Every US SMS bill has three layers, and comparing only the first is the most common budgeting mistake here.

Layer one is the platform fee — the number vendors advertise. Twilio SMS pricing starts at $0.0083 per segment to send or receive on long codes, toll-free numbers, and short codes alike. Telnyx charges $0.004 per part on local 10DLC numbers, $0.0055 on toll-free, $0.007 on short codes. Vonage SMS pricing has no flat published US rate — rates live in the console — but November 2025 market tracking puts the long-code base at $0.0086. Bird, formerly MessageBird, lists $0.0035 per US outbound long-code segment, the lowest published platform rate I found.

Layer two is the carrier fee, and this is the part worth internalizing: these are not vendor prices. AT&T charges roughly $0.0035 per outbound segment, T-Mobile $0.0045, Verizon $0.005 on Twilio's rate card ($0.0045 on Telnyx's). Every provider passes these through — Twilio's help center states the pass-through has "no added cost," and Telnyx prints both lines, blending US passthrough at $0.0038 per part in its own worked example. For the bills below I assume $0.004 blended, within rounding error of every published example.

Layer three is the fixed stuff: number rentals, A2P 10DLC registration, and fees that only surface on the third invoice. Segment math matters: a US segment is 160 characters in GSM-7 or 70 in UCS-2 — one emoji drops you to 70 per segment and a 130-character message bills as two. OTP messages usually fit one segment; marketing templates never do. Inbound is not free either — Twilio charges $0.0083 per inbound segment, so two-way flows roughly double the per-message bill.

## Verify: The Platform Fee Is the Whole Spread

OTP is where per-message rate differences get violent, because verification platforms stack a per-event fee on top of delivery — and the OTP verification cost per event, not the message rate, decides the bill.

Twilio Verify pricing runs $0.05 per successful verification plus channel fees: a US SMS verification costs $0.05 plus $0.0083 plus carrier fees, about $0.062 all-in. That $0.05 has held since May 2023, when Twilio raised it from $0.03. Telnyx Verify is the strangest page in the category: the headline says "from $0.05 a verification" while the rates table — the part matching its machine-readable pricing endpoint — says $0.03 per successful verification plus channel delivery. Budget on $0.03 plus roughly $0.008 of SMS delivery, about $0.038 all-in. Bird includes verification free — its pricing page lists Verify as "included" with channel usage billed separately — so a Bird OTP costs only the message, $0.0035 plus carrier fees, call it $0.0075. Vonage Verify bills under two models: Conversion, per successful verification (tracked at about €0.052, roughly $0.057, plus channel fees) and a Success bundle that wraps failed attempts into the completed rate. Plivo's Verify historically charged nothing per verification, only message cost — but the $1,000-per-month Enterprise contract now gates it.

Two footnotes from Twilio's Verify page deserve attention. WhatsApp delivery is billed on top of the $0.05: the channel table lists $0.0034 per authentication template in the US while the FAQ prose says $0.0147 — the page disagrees with itself, so trust your bill. And SMS delivery attempts are "always charged and not subject to delivery": a user who never finishes the flow still costs you the message, sometimes several if fallback logic retries. Fraud protection is bundled — the $0.05 includes Fraud Guard, Twilio's SMS pumping defense, one of the few honest justifications for the premium. If your stack does email fallback, [transactional email services bill per recipient](/articles/best-transactional-email-services-2026-resend-vs-postmark-vs-sendgrid-vs-mailgun/) at a fraction of SMS.

## Fixed Fees: A2P 10DLC Registration and the Rest

US long-code messaging requires The Campaign Registry registration, and the fixed fees are the part of the bill that surprises people at invoice time.

Twilio's A2P 10DLC fees: brand registration costs $4.50 one-time for sole-proprietor or low-volume standard brands, $46 for standard brands including secondary vetting; campaign vetting runs $15; monthly campaign fees range from $1.50 (low-volume mixed) through $2 (sole prop) to $10 (standard). Those numbers are themselves inflationary — The Campaign Registry raised fees on August 1, 2025, moving brand registration from $4.00 to $4.50, standard vetting from $40 to $41.50, and adding a $12.50-per-retry charge when a brand verification submission fails. Every US provider sits on the same TCR system, so equivalent fees reach your bill through Telnyx, Bird, and AWS; AWS's rate card shows $4 per company registration plus $10 monthly per standard campaign.

Numbers and miscellany: a US long code rents for $1.15 monthly at Twilio, $1.00 at Telnyx and AWS, $0.50 at Bird, $1.09 initial at Vonage. Twilio adds a $0.001 processing fee per failed message, a $0.015 Engagement Suite fee for link tracking (first 1,000 free monthly), and $0.015 for its Compliance Toolkit. MMS diverges: Twilio charges $0.022 outbound, Telnyx $0.015 per part, both adding carrier media fees. RCS prices like SMS at Twilio ($0.0083) and slightly cheaper at Telnyx ($0.0065). And if your product also phones people — every voice agent stack does — the voice spread is wider still: Twilio charges $0.014 per outbound minute against Telnyx's published $0.002, which is why [voice agent platform comparisons](/articles/ai-voice-agent-platforms-2026-retell-vs-vapi-vs-bland-vs-synthflow/) lead with the carrier layer rather than the AI layer.

## The 2026 Market Split

Three market structures now coexist, and picking the wrong one costs real money.

The premium CPaaS tier — Twilio, Vonage, Sinch — sells breadth and convenience. Twilio's ecosystem, SDKs, and managed number pools are genuinely best in class, and its October 2026 sheet shows it knows it: the US list price sits at $0.0083 per segment, up from the $0.0079 that trackers logged through most of 2025 — a quiet 5% increase nobody announced. Support is the hidden margin layer: developer support is free, but third-party analysis pegs Twilio's paid support plans at 4–8% of monthly spend with $250–$5,000 minimums.

The discount tier — Bird, Telnyx, Bandwidth — sells the same carrier network with thinner wrappers. Bird SMS pricing undercuts everyone at $0.0035 per US segment, numbers at $0.50 monthly, verification included; Telnyx pricing prints both lines — its rate and the carrier's — and throws in $0 support on the page itself; Bandwidth, which physically operates the network many competitors resell, tracks at $0.004 per message. The catch is honest: balance prepayment, thinner compliance hand-holding, and delivery quality you must validate on your own traffic. For OTP-heavy workloads this tier is the cheapest SMS API story going; for a regulated fintech shipping into 40 countries on a deadline, the premium tier's managed pools and fraud tooling can earn the spread.

Then there is Plivo's defection. The company that spent years as the default cheaper Twilio alternative — $0.005 a message, no verify fee — now shows a pay-as-you-go plan covering SIP trunking, audio streaming, AI agents, and phone numbers in the US and India only. SMS, WhatsApp, and Verify are Enterprise items starting at $1,000 per month, and trackers still list Plivo rates at $0.0077 marked enterprise-only. If you built on Plivo self-serve in 2024, your 2026 renewal conversation is not about rates; it is whether $12,000 a year is a floor you accept. The standard roster of Twilio alternatives — Telnyx, Bird, Sinch, AWS End User Messaging, Infobip — all still self-serve, which is why this guide prices them.

## Fraud: The Cost Nobody Budgets

SMS pumping and artificially inflated traffic are not hypothetical in 2026 — Twilio's own March 2026 fraud update leads with AIT tactics and "weaponized trust," and the mechanics apply to every provider.

The failure mode: attackers register accounts on your product, trigger OTP messages toward premium or fraud-farm numbers, and split revenue with insiders at destination carriers. Industry analysis estimates unprotected SMS programs see fraud inflate messaging costs by 15–30%. The defenses are cheap: number-lookup pre-screening before you send, geo-fencing destinations you do not serve, per-user and per-IP rate limits, and pattern detection that flags pumping before your balance drains. Twilio bundles Fraud Guard into Verify's $0.05; Telnyx and Bird sell lookup products ($0.0015 and $0.005 per query) for building the pre-screen yourself.

One more trap hides in failures: Twilio charges $0.001 per message that terminates in Failed status, and retry logic multiplies it — a fallback chain retrying three times through three channels bills four times for one login. If identity risk is core to your product, [dedicated identity verification platforms](/articles/best-ai-identity-verification-tools-2026-persona-vs-jumio-vs-veriff-vs-socure/) price per verification with document checks included, which changes the calculus more than any SMS rate card.

## Choosing by Workload

OTP-heavy products (signups, logins, payments): Bird and Telnyx own the price conversation — $375 versus $3,115 at 50,000 monthly verifications is not a rounding error, it is a headcount. Validate delivery on your real carrier mix first, prepay conservatively, keep a second provider warm for failover. [Authentication platforms like Auth0 and Clerk](/articles/authentication-platforms-2026-auth0-vs-clerk-vs-supabase-vs-workos/) wrap their own SMS providers if you would rather never touch a rate card.

Notification-heavy products (order status, reminders, alerts): the platform fee vanishes and the comparison narrows to message-plus-carrier. Bird at $0.0075 all-in and Telnyx at $0.008 beat the big platforms' $0.0123–$0.0126 by about 35%, but Twilio's Engagement Suite, compliance tooling, and observability matter once you send templates with links at scale.

Global mixed traffic: price your actual destination countries, not the US column. Vonage and Twilio maintain deep per-country rate sheets and compliance playbooks; Bird is strong in Europe; regional providers in India and Southeast Asia routinely undercut everyone on home turf.

WhatsApp-first markets: in Brazil, India, and much of Southeast Asia, WhatsApp OTP costs less than SMS and converts better — Meta's per-message authentication rates undercut terminal SMS fees in those markets, and Bird and Telnyx pass Meta's rates through with thin platform fees. In the US the math flips: Twilio's own numbers make WhatsApp authentication delivery ($0.0034–$0.0147 on their own page) comparable to or worse than an SMS segment. Test the destination market, not the press release.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does Twilio SMS cost per message in 2026?

Twilio's October 2026 price sheet lists US outbound and inbound SMS at $0.0083 per 160-character segment on long codes, toll-free numbers, and short codes. Carrier fees add $0.0035–$0.005 per segment depending on destination network, so a realistic all-in US message runs about $0.0123. MMS costs $0.022 outbound, a US long code adds $1.15 monthly, and a Twilio Verify verification stacks $0.05 on top.

### What is the cheapest Twilio alternative for OTP?

On published list prices, Bird — verification is included and the US message costs $0.0035 plus carrier fees, making a full OTP about $0.0075 versus Twilio's $0.062. Telnyx is the runner-up at about $0.038 per successful verification with its $0.03 platform fee. Both require validating delivery on your own traffic; cheap rates with bad routes are not cheap.

### Why does Twilio Verify charge $0.05 when the message itself costs under a cent?

Twilio's answer: the fee buys the managed layer — passcode generation, 42-language localization, TTL management, rate limiting, managed number pools, and Fraud Guard. The skeptical answer: $0.05 has held since May 2023 while the message fee sits at $0.0083 — the platform fee is 6.7 times the message it delivers. If you do not need the managed layer, Telnyx charges $0.03 for a thinner version and Bird charges nothing.

### How much do A2P 10DLC registration fees add?

Expect roughly $20–$60 to get a US long-code campaign live: $4.50 brand registration (or $46 for standard brands with secondary vetting), $15 campaign vetting, and $1.50–$10 monthly campaign fees at Twilio, with the same carrier-mandated fees arriving through every provider's bill. The August 2025 TCR fee increase also introduced a $12.50 charge per failed brand-verification retry, which punishes sloppy first submissions.

### Is WhatsApp OTP cheaper than SMS?

Market-dependent, and materially so. In Brazil, India, Indonesia, and much of Africa, Meta's per-message authentication rates undercut SMS termination, and providers pass them through — WhatsApp OTP frequently runs 30–60% cheaper with better conversion. In the US, Meta's authentication rate is high enough that WhatsApp delivery is comparable to or worse than an SMS segment. Check the destination country's Meta rate before redesigning your verification flow.

## The Bottom Line on SMS API Pricing

The 2026 rate cards reward one habit: reading the second line. The platform fee is the margin; the carrier fee is the truth; the fixed fees are the invoice surprise. If your workload is US OTP at volume, Bird and Telnyx sell the same regulatory compliance and the same carrier network for one-eighth of Twilio's bill — validate delivery, keep failover warm, pocket the difference. If your workload is global, regulated, or support-heavy, the premium tier earns part of its spread, though 4–8% support minimums deserve the same scrutiny as message rates. Whichever tier you pick, model the verification fee separately from the message fee: it is where most of a verification-heavy bill lives, and every vendor buries it in small print.

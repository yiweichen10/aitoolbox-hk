  • ✓  Subscriptions run on your own account, so MRR-based businesses keep 100% of revenue minus processing - compare the 5% + 50c that merchant-of-record platforms charge
  • ✓  You own the customer relationship: cards, emails, billing history and data live in your Stripe account and export cleanly if you ever switch

CURRENT PRICING
2.9% + 30¢ per card charge
US pricing verified 2026-11-21 against stripe.com pricing pages (corroborated line-by-line by a September 2026 fee breakdown that re-checked official pages): 2.9% + $0.30 per successful domestic card charge; international cards +1.5%; currency conversion +1%; manually keyed cards +0.5%; ACH direct debit 0.8% capped at $5; in-person Terminal 2.7% + $0.05; Klarna 5.99% + $0.30. Dispute fee $15. No monthly or setup fees on core Payments. Add-on products: Stripe Billing (subscriptions) +0.7% of billing volume, or annual plans from $620/mo; Invoicing 0.4% per paid invoice (capped $2); Stripe Tax 0.5% per transaction; instant payouts 1.5% (50c minimum); Radar fraud plans from $10/mo. Real-world effective rate on a $50 US card payment is $1.75 (3.5%); a foreign card with conversion runs about 6%.

Stripe is not a monetization platform in the way Gumroad or Patreon is - it is the layer underneath your money. There is no marketplace, no audience, no discovery. There is just the shortest possible distance between a customer's card and your bank account, at 2.9% + 30c per charge with no monthly fee. Every tool in this site's AI Income category that takes a cut - marketplaces, course platforms, membership sites - exists because handling payments directly is work. Stripe is what you move to when you decide that work is worth the extra 2-4 points of margin.

I have billed clients through Stripe Invoicing since 2021 and run two subscription products on Payment Links without writing an integration. The one sentence version: for freelancers, consultants and small digital sellers, Stripe is the cheapest legitimate way to get paid, and its pricing page is more honest than most of its competitors' - every fee is public and every add-on is optional.

The Money Angle

  • Get paid as a freelancer or consultant. Invoicing costs 0.4% per paid invoice, capped at $2. On a $3,000 project invoice that is $12 plus card processing - or near-zero if the client pays by ACH (0.8% capped at $5, so $5 on any large invoice). Compare keeping 80-90% of a sale on gig platforms: on a $1,000 invoice, Fiverr's 20% cut is $200 and Stripe's total is about $30 by ACH. Direct clients pay for this account many times over. If you are leaving platforms, Contra takes 0% commission and runs on Stripe rails.
  • Sell digital products with Payment Links. No website needed: create a hosted checkout in the dashboard, share the link anywhere, done. This is the no-code backbone for templates, presets, guides and small software. At 2.9% + 30c, a $29 product costs $1.14 to deliver versus $2.17 at a 5% + 50c merchant-of-record platform - but the MoR platform also handles EU VAT, which matters the moment Europe becomes real revenue.
  • Run subscriptions for services and memberships. Retainers, maintenance plans, membership communities, coaching. Stripe Billing adds 0.7% on top of processing: a $200/mo retainer costs about $6.70/month all-in. Every dollar of MRR stays yours - this is why SaaS founders live on Stripe and why membership platforms that charge 10% start looking expensive at scale.
  • Replace the payment layer under something you already sell. If you sell on Gumroad (10% per sale) or Ko-fi (5% on shop sales), moving a mature product to your own Stripe checkout saves real money - $500/mo of sales shifts roughly $35-50/mo from platform fees to nothing, minus the work of running checkout, receipts and refunds yourself.
  • Collect B2B money the cheap way. ACH direct debit at 0.8% capped $5 turns a $10,000 corporate invoice from a $300 card fee into $5. Most sellers never configure this. It is the single highest-ROI setting in the dashboard for anyone billing businesses.

The Honest Cons

  • Cross-border work costs real money. International cards plus conversion can reach 5.4-6.4%. If most of your customers are outside the US, model your actual mix before assuming Stripe is cheap.
  • Tax compliance is your job. Stripe is not a merchant of record. Selling digital goods to EU consumers makes you the VAT collector unless you add Stripe Tax or use a MoR platform. This is the entire reason Lemon Squeezy, Paddle and Polar exist at 5% + 50c.
  • Account risk falls on you. Stripe underwrites your account. High refund rates, chargebacks or restricted categories can bring holds, reserves or closure - and unlike a marketplace, there is no one to escalate to as a small seller. Keep terms clear and refunds prompt.
  • Product fees stack quietly. Billing, Tax, Radar and instant payouts each have their own rate. A "Stripe costs 2.9%" claim becomes 4%+ once a subscription business turns everything on. Audit which add-ons you actually use.
  • Disputes cost $15 regardless of outcome. On a $20 product, one refund-happy customer can cost more in disputes than they ever paid you. Small-ticket digital goods with ambiguous delivery are the worst fit for direct card processing.

The rule of thumb I use: domestic B2B or invoicing → Stripe direct, no contest. Global consumer digital products with EU volume → a MoR platform's premium buys real compliance. Everything else → Stripe plus Stripe Tax usually wins on price and keeps you portable.

Bottom Line

Stripe is the default for a reason: the fees are public, there is nothing to rent, and the customer relationship is yours to keep. It will not bring you customers - that is what the marketplaces and audiences in this category are for. What it does is stop leaking margin once you have customers, especially on invoices, retainers and B2B work where ACH and the $2 invoice cap make it dramatically cheaper than any middleman. Start with Payment Links and Invoicing, add Billing when something recurs, add Tax when Europe shows up. If your account is one bad month from a freeze, that is the signal to diversify processors - not a reason to stay on a 10% platform forever.

Questions we get asked

What does Stripe actually cost to accept a payment?

In the US, 2.9% + 30c per successful domestic card charge, with no monthly or setup fees. International cards add 1.5%, currency conversion adds 1%, and ACH bank debits cost 0.8% capped at $5. On a $50 US card payment that is $1.75; on a foreign card with conversion, about $3.00.

Is Stripe cheaper than Lemon Squeezy, Paddle or Polar?

Per transaction, usually yes - MoR platforms charge 5% + 50c (Lemon Squeezy, Paddle) or 5% + 50c on Polar's Starter plan because they take on tax compliance for you. Stripe direct costs 2.9% + 30c but leaves VAT and sales tax handling to you. Selling to consumers in the EU/UK at scale, the MoR premium often pays for itself; selling B2B or domestically, Stripe direct wins.

Can I use Stripe without writing code?

Yes. Payment Links let you create hosted checkout pages for fixed prices, subscriptions or pay-what-you-want in the dashboard, and Invoicing sends hosted invoice pages by email. Most solo sellers never touch the API - code matters only when you want fully custom checkout flows.

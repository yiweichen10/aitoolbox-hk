Granola approached the meeting-notes problem from a direction the bot-first tools never took: instead of sending a recorder into your Zoom call, it listens to device audio on your own machine and merges what the transcript heard with what you typed. The result is a summary that keeps your emphasis - the deal terms you underlined, the objection you flagged - instead of a model flattening everything to equal weight. Brex rebuilt its meeting culture on it, which tells you the target user: people whose notes are work product, not just memory.

Pricing, verified this month

Basic is $0: AI meeting notes, 30 days of history, AI chat across meetings, shared folders, templates, voice typing in any app, multi-language capture, and a per-user opt-out of model training. Business is $14/user/mo and adds unlimited history, advanced reasoning models, pre-meeting briefs, follow-up email drafts, integrations with Attio, Notion, HubSpot, Affinity, Zapier and Slack, the MCP server and API access. Enterprise is $35/user/mo with SSO for 50+ seat workspaces, SCIM, HIPAA-compliant workspaces, an audit API and org-wide admin controls. Every plan captures audio on-device with no visible bot, deletes the audio after transcription and carries SOC 2 Type II.

Where the money angle lives

The people who earn their subscription back in week one are consultants, coaches and agency account managers - roles where a same-day follow-up is the difference between closing and stalling. A $150/hr solo consultant who reclaims two hours a week on notes and follow-ups pays for a year of Business plans in saved time each month. The sharper play is the automation chain: on Business, the MCP server lets Claude or ChatGPT read your meeting history, so 'draft the proposal from Tuesday's call' becomes one prompt, and the API feeds client-meeting summaries straight into HubSpot. Agencies package that as a deliverable - 'every call summarized, logged and actioned within an hour' - and bill it as service, not software. For solo operators weighing tools, our freelancer stack guide shows where it sits next to transcription-first rivals like Otter and Fireflies.

The honest trade-offs

The 30-day history on the free tier makes Granola a rental, not an archive - pay or lose your notes. The features that justify the $14 (briefs, follow-ups, CRM sync, MCP) are all locked behind Business, so the free plan is really a 30-day trial with nicer manners. Capture quality tracks your hardware: a good mic and a quiet room produce clean transcripts, while audio-only phone calls and open offices degrade them, and there is no bot fallback to paper over a bad setup. And per-seat pricing adds up quietly - an eight-person team pays $112/mo, which is the moment someone asks why Fathom is free for the same transcript.

Questions we get asked

Granola vs Otter or Fathom - which one should I pay for?

Different capture philosophies. Otter and Fireflies send a bot that joins the call and transcribe everything for a whole team; Granola stays on your device and blends the transcript with what you typed. Pick Granola for client-facing consulting work where a visible bot is a problem, Otter or Fathom for team-wide call archives. The field is compared in our meeting assistant breakdown.

Does everyone on the call need to know Granola is running?

Ethically yes, and legally often. Granola ships a feature that posts an automatic consent message at the meeting start, and its docs recommend asking permission before transcribing. Audio is deleted after transcription, each user controls model-training opt-out, and Enterprise admins can force it off workspace-wide.

Can I push notes into Claude, ChatGPT or my CRM?

Yes, on paid tiers. The Business plan includes an MCP server (so Claude, ChatGPT and other MCP clients can read your last 30 days of personal notes plus public workspace notes) and API access. Business-tier integrations cover Attio, HubSpot, Notion, Affinity, Slack and Zapier; Enterprise makes MCP and API scope admin-configurable.

What Skillshare Actually Is (From a Teacher's Side)

Skillshare is a subscription course marketplace: members pay about $13.99/month or $165/year for unlimited access to the whole catalog, and you - the teacher - get paid from a slice of that subscription revenue based on how many minutes paying members spend watching your classes. You never set a price. You never talk to a buyer. You publish a class (minimum 10 minutes, with a class project) and the watch time does the rest.

I have been teaching on Skillshare since 2023, and the first thing to understand is that this is a completely different business from Udemy. Nobody buys "your course." They buy a Netflix-style subscription, and you get a slice of the fund proportional to your share of total platform watch time. That single mechanic explains everything good and bad about the platform.

The Money Angle: How AI Skills Fit In

Skillshare's audience is concentrated in creative and practical skills - design, illustration, video, writing, freelancing, productivity. That makes AI-skill classes one of the best-fitting niches on the platform right now: Midjourney workflows for illustrators, ChatGPT for marketers, AI video editing, Python automation for non-coders. People are actively searching for these, and the catalog is nowhere near saturated the way "learn watercolor" is.

There are four income streams, and most new teachers focus on the wrong one:

  • Teacher Fund royalties - the watch-time pool. This is the "passive income" everyone talks about, and it is real but slow. Creator-reported rates run roughly $0.05-$0.10 per paid minute watched (Skillshare does not publish an official rate). A class getting 2,000 paid minutes a month is earning $100-$200.
  • Referrals - Skillshare pays you 60% of the first year of payments from any new member who subscribes through your link (after refunds). If you have any outside audience - YouTube, a blog, a newsletter - this is usually bigger than royalties. One $99/year referral is worth about $59.
  • 1-on-1 sessions and digital products - newer additions. Sessions keep you 90% (Skillshare takes 10%); digital products let you sell downloads to your class audience.
  • Engagement rewards - $100/month for members of the Top Teacher or Rising Teacher programs who collect two new positive reviews in a month. Not available to new teachers, but a real bonus once you are in.

The practical playbook: publish short, project-based classes (30-45 minutes, 3-5 lessons), build the catalog to 5+ classes, and push referrals from any traffic you own. Pure catalog teachers without an outside audience are the ones who earn almost nothing.

What It Really Costs (Earnings Mechanics, 2026 Rules)

Teaching is free - no hosting fee, no listing fee. The real "cost" is the eligibility wall. As of July 16, 2026, you earn $0 in a month unless ALL of these are true:

RequirementThreshold
Skillshare followers at month end50+
Watch time (published a class in past 18 months)400+ paid minutes/month
Watch time (no new class in 18+ months)650+ paid minutes/month
Absolute floor either way75+ paid minutes/month

And the minutes that do NOT count matter as much as the ones that do: trailer views, free-trial watch time, and free-access-link minutes all pay zero. That means launching a class with free promo links builds reviews but no royalties.

Other mechanics: payments go out through Tipalti around the 16th of each month; Class Central-style reporting puts the average teacher around $200/month while the distribution is heavily top-skewed; and the teacher share of subscription revenue has reportedly fallen from about 50% (pre-2020) to about 30% (2021-2024) to roughly 20% under the current fund model. Skillshare's own defense of the new thresholds: they say 90% of teachers who lost eligibility were earning under $5/month.

Bottom Line

Skillshare is worth it for two kinds of people: creative-AI teachers who want free distribution to test whether their teaching sells, and creators with an existing audience who can farm the 60% first-year referral rate. It is not worth it if you expect to upload one course and earn meaningfully - the 50-follower rule, the 400/650-minute thresholds, no student emails, and a shrinking revenue share guarantee that the average outcome is pocket change. Publish a catalog, own your traffic elsewhere, and treat Skillshare as one leg of the stool, not the stool.

👍 Pros

  • Genuinely free to publish: no hosting, listing, or subscription cost - the cheapest way to test whether your teaching sells
  • Built-in creative-audience traffic means your first students don't depend on you having a YouTube channel or email list
  • 60% first-year referral share is one of the best rates anywhere, and for anyone with outside traffic it usually out-earns royalties
  • Watch-time income is evergreen: a catalog of 5-10 classes keeps accumulating paid minutes for years with light upkeep

👎 Cons

❓ FAQ

How much do Skillshare teachers actually make?

Creator-reported tiers: 1-3 classes earns most new teachers $0-$200/month, a 5-10 class catalog with some outside promotion lands $200-$1,000, and established 10+ class teachers report $1,000-$5,000. The average across all teachers sits near $200/month (Class Central reporting) and the distribution is brutally top-skewed - top teachers clear $100k+/year while a large share of new teachers earn nothing because they miss the eligibility thresholds.

What are the 2026 payout rules and why did my earnings stop?

Since July 16, 2026 you need at least 50 followers at month end, plus 400+ qualifying minutes watched that month if you published a class within 18 months (650+ if not), with a 75-minute floor. Qualifying minutes only count from active paid subscribers - trailers, free trials, and free-access links all pay zero, and minutes don't roll over between months. Miss any threshold and that month pays $0. Skillshare says 90% of teachers who lost eligibility were earning under $5/month, but for new teachers it means months of zero-income catalog building.

Is Skillshare or Udemy better for a first-time teacher?

Different games. Udemy pays per enrollment at a 37% marketplace split (97% on your own coupons), rewards launch spikes, and a $10 course with 100 sales out-earns most new Skillshare catalogs. Skillshare pays per watch minute from a shared fund, rewards a long evergreen catalog, and has the stronger creative-AI audience plus the 60% referral rate. If you have outside traffic, Skillshare referrals usually win; if you don't, Udemy's per-sale model usually earns first. Many teachers eventually do both, then move their best content to a platform they own.

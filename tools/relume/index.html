What Relume Actually Does

Relume generates wireframes from sitemaps. That is the one-sentence description, and it undersells the tool.

Here is what that means in practice: you paste a URL, describe a page, or upload a sitemap, and Relume produces a structured wireframe — navigation, hero section, feature grids, testimonials, pricing comparison, FAQ, footer — with real placeholder text (not lorem ipsum) and the sections arranged in a logical flow.

Each section is a pre-built component from their library. The AI selects which components to use and in what order. You can regenerate individual sections, swap them with library alternatives, or manually rearrange the order.

Once you are happy with the wireframe, you sync it to Figma. The components arrive as native Figma layers — auto-layout frames, text layers, vector shapes — fully editable like anything you would build by hand.

I started using Relume in late 2024 after a particularly brutal month of back-to-back client projects where I was spending 50-60% of my time just arranging gray boxes. The tool is not magic — it saves about 60-70% of the wireframing time, not 100% — but 60-70% on your most tedious, lowest-value task is a big deal.

The Workflow: How I Actually Use It on Client Projects

Every web designer has a different process, so let me walk through exactly how Relume fits into mine.

Step 1: Generate the sitemap

I start by describing the project to Relume AI sitemap generator. Something like "small business website for a residential painting company, 5 pages: home, services, about, gallery, contact." Relume generates a sitemap tree with all the standard pages plus suggested sub-pages I might not have thought of ("exterior painting" and "interior painting" sub-pages under services, for example).

I review the sitemap, add or remove pages, then share it with the client for approval. This takes about 15 minutes total. Before Relume, I was building sitemaps in Google Docs or Miro, sharing screenshots, handling feedback over email — easily 2-3 hours of back-and-forth per project.

Step 2: Wireframe each page

For each page in the approved sitemap, Relume generates a wireframe. The home page takes maybe 2 minutes to generate. Sub-pages are faster since they are simpler layouts. I review each one and rearrange sections as needed.

This is where the component library becomes really useful. The AI might generate a testimonials section that feels too salesy for a painting company — so I swap it with a more subdued testimonial variant from the library. Or it might put the recent projects section too early — I drag it lower on the page.

For a 5-page site, the wireframing phase takes 30-45 minutes instead of 6-8 hours.

Step 3: Sync to Figma

One-click sync sends all wireframes to Figma as a page-per-frame document. The layers are properly named and structured. Auto-layout is applied. Text is real copy (AI-generated placeholder, not great but beatable).

From here, visual design begins. I pick colors, swap fonts, add real images, refine spacing. This is the same visual design work I would do on any project — Relume just gave me a running start instead of a blank canvas.

Step 4: Build

I build in Webflow or Framer, referencing the Figma file as the design source. The wireframe-to-build translation is the same as any Figma-to-builder workflow. Relume does not magically export to Webflow, which is a limitation (see cons), but the Figma file it produces is clean enough that the build is straightforward.

What Relume Is Bad At

I want to be honest about the limitations because the marketing materials are not.

Creative or editorial layouts. If a client wants something that looks like a magazine spread or a design agency portfolio, Relume AI will fight you. It keeps defaulting to standard SaaS patterns — centered hero with headline + CTA button, three-column feature grid, alternating image-text sections. You can override it, but you will spend more time fighting the AI than if you started from scratch.

Complex navigation. Standard top nav with dropdowns? Fine. Mega menus? Sidebar navigation? Any navigation pattern that is not a simple horizontal bar? Relume either ignores it or does it wrong. You will need to design complex navigation in Figma from scratch.

E-commerce beyond product pages. Relume can handle a product detail page — image gallery, description, specs, reviews. But category pages with faceted filters, cart flows, checkout — these are way outside its scope. E-commerce sites are maybe 20-30% Relume-able, with the other 70-80% needing custom design.

Web apps and dashboards. Different beast entirely. Relume is a website wireframing tool, not a UI design tool for applications. If you are designing a SaaS dashboard with data tables, charts, complex forms, and user management screens, Relume will not help.

Brand-specific design systems. If your client has an existing brand guide with very specific component patterns (a custom card style, a unique testimonial layout), Relume library components will not match. You will need to strip the styling and rebuild in Figma, which is more work than just building from your own component library.

Getting Started Without Wasting Time

If you want to try Relume, here is my recommended approach based on what I wish I had done:

  • Start with the free tier on a real project, not a tutorial. Pick a client project or a spec project you care about. Tutorial projects do not reveal where the tool actually breaks.
  • Use the sitemap tool first, even if you skip the wireframing AI. Getting client sign-off on structure is valuable on its own. The sitemap tool is the most underrated feature.
  • Generate a full wireframe, then spend 20 minutes trashing sections you do not like. Do not try to get the perfect wireframe from the AI on the first try. Generate fast, delete aggressively, rebuild from the library.
  • Sync to Figma early, not after you have perfected the wireframe. Seeing the wireframe as real Figma layers changes your perception — things that look fine in Relume preview sometimes look awkward as actual design elements.
  • Build a personal favorites collection in the component library. After 3-4 projects, you will have go-to sections for heroes, testimonials, pricing tables. Save them. They become your starting point for the next project, and the AI generation becomes less necessary over time.
  • Plan for the manual work. Relume saves time on wireframing, not on visual design or build. Budget your project timeline accordingly. If a project used to take 40 hours, plan for 30-32 hours — not 20.

👍 Pros

  • The sitemap-to-wireframe AI is the real deal and saves measurable hours. Here is a real workflow from a client project last month: I pasted a competitor URL into Relume, it generated a complete site structure (12 pages, full sitemap tree), then for each page it generated the wireframe with all standard sections — nav, hero, features grid, pricing comparison, testimonials carousel, FAQ accordion, footer. This whole process took about 4 minutes. Manually wireframing those 12 pages would have been 15-20 hours of Figma work. The AI does not invent clever layouts — it produces clean, standard patterns that actually ship. For a web design freelancer billing 3-4 projects per month, this alone saves 30-50 hours/month. At $100/hour, that is $3,000-$5,000 in recovered time. The Pro plan costs $26/month. The math is not complicated.
  • The Figma integration is not a gimmick — synced components are native Figma layers with auto-layout and text styles preserved. When you sync a Relume section to Figma, it comes in as a proper frame with nested auto-layout containers, not a flattened image or a single massive group. This matters because it means you can actually edit things. Change text, swap colors, adjust spacing — it all behaves like normal Figma objects. The Relume library also ships Figma variables for colors and typography, so if you are using a design system, you can map their tokens to yours in about 10 minutes. I have tried other AI-to-Figma tools that export flat designs or SVG blobs — those are useless for real work. Relume gets this right.
  • The component library is large (1,000+ sections) and actually useful, not just filler. Sections are organized by type (hero, features, pricing, testimonials, CTA, footer, etc.) and each section has 5-20 variants. The utility here is that you can mix AI-generated layouts with hand-picked library sections in the same project. AI generates the overall structure. You swap out a section that feels off with a library alternative. Then fine-tune in Figma. This hybrid workflow is where Relume shines — it speeds up the grunt work while giving you full control over the final look.
  • Responsive previews at wireframe stage catch layout problems before you waste hours in Figma. Relume shows how your layout reorganizes across desktop, tablet, and mobile breakpoints while you are still in the sitemap/wireframe phase. This is genuinely useful because fixing a layout issue at the wireframe stage takes 2 minutes — fixing it after you have built a full Figma design with auto-layout and components can take 30+ minutes. On a recent e-commerce project, Relume flagged that the product grid would collapse to a single column too aggressively on tablet — caught it before I spent 4 hours designing the desktop version of that grid.
  • The sitemap tool doubles as a client communication and scoping tool. Before I even start designing, I generate a full sitemap in Relume, share the link with the client, and they can see every page, every section, and comment on structure. This reduces scope creep significantly — the client signs off on the site structure before any visual design happens, so when they later say "can we add a comparison table on the features page," you can point to the approved sitemap and have a conversation about change orders instead of just absorbing the extra work. Admittedly this is a soft benefit, not a hard time-saver, but over 6+ client projects it has probably saved me 3-4 rounds of revision per project.

👎 Cons

  • The AI-generated wireframes are generic by design — do not expect creative or distinctive layouts. Relume produces clean, standard, conversion-optimized patterns. That is the point. But if your client wants a website that feels unique or editorial, Relume will give you a B+ SaaS landing page and you will need to heavily customize 60-70% of the sections. For creative agencies or brands with strong visual identities, Relume is a starting point, not a finishing tool. I have learned this the hard way on two projects where the client wanted a "bold, editorial, magazine-style" site and Relume kept generating standard SaaS layouts. You can override the AI by describing sections in detail, but at that point you are basically writing a design spec — which kind of defeats the purpose of an AI wireframing tool.
  • The free tier is so restrictive it is barely useful for evaluation. Three projects total — not per month, total. Once you hit the cap, you cannot even open old projects to reference them. For a freelancer testing whether Relume fits their workflow, three projects is maybe one real client engagement worth of testing. The Starter plan at $15/month adds more projects but locks some component variants behind Pro. You realistically need the Pro plan at $26/month to do client work. Not expensive, but the pricing ladder is designed to push you to Pro, and the free tier feels more like a teaser than a trial.
  • Custom interactions and animations are not in Relume's wheelhouse at all. If your client wants scroll-triggered animations, parallax effects, hover states, or complex micro-interactions, Relume will give you zero help — these all need to be designed and specified separately in Figma or directly in code. This is fine for brochure sites and standard SaaS pages, but increasingly clients expect some motion and interactivity even on informational sites. You will need to plan for extra design time on top of what Relume outputs.
  • The AI sometimes hallucinates sections that do not make sense together. I have seen it generate a hero section, then a "how it works" section, then a second hero section, then a pricing table. It is like the model gets confused about where it is in the page flow. This happens maybe 15-20% of the time on complex pages, and you need to manually delete and reorder sections. Not a dealbreaker — fixing a bad section order takes 2 minutes — but it breaks the flow when you are trying to speed through wireframing.
  • No direct export to Webflow, Framer, or any other builder — only Figma. If your workflow is Relume wireframe to build in Webflow/Framer, there is a manual translation step where you recreate the layout in your builder of choice. Relume provides a Figma-to-Webflow plugin (separate, paid) and some third-party tools exist, but the native experience is Relume → Figma → ??? → builder. For agencies that do design in Figma and hand off to a developer, this is fine — the developer builds from Figma anyway. For solo freelancers who design and build, it adds friction.

❓ FAQ

Can I actually make money with Relume as a freelance web designer?

Yes — here is the math from my own freelance work. Before Relume, a 5-page small business site (home, about, services, portfolio, contact) took me about 35-45 hours at $75-$100/hour: 10-12 hours wireframing + 15-20 hours visual design in Figma + 10-13 hours building in Webflow/Framer. With Relume, the wireframing drops to 2-3 hours (generate sitemap to tweak AI wireframes to sync to Figma). Visual design stays at 15-20 hours. The build stays at 10-13 hours. Total: 27-36 hours instead of 35-45. That is 8-9 hours saved per project. At $100/hour, you either pocket $800-$900 extra per project (same flat rate, less work) or you take on more projects. If you do 4 sites/month, Relume effectively buys back 32-36 hours/month — basically an entire work week. The Pro plan costs $26/month. The ROI calculation: $26 to $3,200-$3,600 in recovered billable time per month. For someone just starting out charging $40-$50/hour, the math still works — you save 8 hours x $45 = $360 per project, which covers a year of Relume Pro in one job. One important caveat: this math assumes you already have clients. Relume is a speed multiplier, not a client acquisition tool. If you are struggling to find work, Relume will not fix that.

Relume vs doing wireframes manually in Figma — is it actually faster?

For standard website layouts (SaaS landing pages, small business sites, portfolio sites, e-commerce product pages), yes — significantly faster. I timed it on a recent SaaS landing page redesign: manual wireframing took 4.5 hours (sketching layout, building auto-layout frames, placing placeholder content). Relume generated a 90%-usable wireframe in 3 minutes, then I spent 40 minutes adjusting section order, swapping two components from the library, and adding custom notes for the developer. Total: 43 minutes vs 4.5 hours. For unusual layouts — editorial sites, art portfolios, interactive web apps, anything with non-standard navigation — Relume is less helpful because its training data is heavily weighted toward standard B2B and SaaS patterns. You will spend so much time overriding the AI output that you might as well start from scratch. My rule of thumb: if I can describe the page in one sentence and it sounds like a standard web page, Relume wins. If I need a whiteboard session to figure out the layout, I start in Figma.

What is the biggest mistake people make with Relume?

Treating Relume output as final and skipping the human review pass. I have seen freelancers generate a wireframe, sync it to Figma, add colors, and call it done — then the client comes back with "this feels generic" or "why is the pricing section above the testimonials?" The AI puts sections in a statistically common order, which is fine for a first draft but terrible for conversion optimization. Your job as the designer is to take Relume output and ask: does this page flow tell the right story? Are the sections ordered to match the buyer journey? Does each section earn its place on the page? These are judgment calls the AI cannot make. My personal workflow: Relume generates wireframe, I reorder maybe 30% of sections, I delete 10-15% that feel like filler, I add 1-2 custom sections for whatever makes this client unique, then sync to Figma for visual design. The second biggest mistake is not showing the sitemap to the client before visual design. Spend the 20 minutes to do a sitemap walkthrough. It saves hours of redesign later.

Does Relume replace Figma, or is it just an add-on?

Relume is an add-on, not a replacement. Think of it as a wireframing accelerator that feeds into Figma. You still need Figma for visual design, prototyping, developer handoff, and component management. Relume handles the structural planning and initial wireframing — the part where you are arranging gray boxes and writing lorem ipsum for the 50th time. Once the wireframe is in Figma, Relume steps back and you work in Figma as normal. The Figma plugin lets you pull in additional components from Relume library as needed, but it is pull-on-demand, not a constant sync. If you do not already use Figma, Relume is not very useful — the wireframe tool has a basic preview, but it is not a design tool and it is not a site builder. Relume + Figma + Webflow/Framer is a solid stack for freelance web design. Relume solo is not enough to deliver a finished website.

Squarespace spent years as the design-forward website builder that AI tools kept failing to imitate. In 2026 it flipped the script: the entire plan structure now runs on AI credits, with selling features on every tier. The result is a builder that is simultaneously more AI-native and more commerce-friendly than the one most people remember.

The 2026 plans, decoded

Three tiers, verified on the official pricing page. Basic: $19/mo monthly or $12/mo annual, 2 contributors, 10 one-time AI credits, and a 2% fee on online store transactions. Core ($27/$17): unlimited contributors, 20 AI credits a month, zero transaction fees, a free year of Google Workspace email, advanced discount and lead-capture tools, automated taxes. Advanced ($36/$25): 120 AI credits a month, reserved cart, countdown timer, high-traffic checkout, and no fees on digital content or memberships. Annual billing saves up to 36% and includes the first year of a custom domain. Every plan gets a 14-day free trial.

Read the structure honestly and it is a nudge toward Core: the 2% fee on Basic means a store doing $1,000/month pays $20 in fees - more than the $5 monthly gap to Core. The AI credit ladder points the same direction; 10 one-time credits on Basic are a demo, while Advanced's 120/month can sustain weekly content generation.

What the AI actually does

Design Intelligence generates layouts, writes on-brand copy and handles image editing inside Squarespace's template system. The honest assessment: it accelerates the template path rather than replacing design judgment. You get competent, coherent pages fast - closer to 'custom agency' than Wix's interview-style builder produces, but not a substitute for a designer when the brief is genuinely custom. That is a different pitch from Durable, which generates a full site in minutes with less polish, or Webflow AI, which targets professionals who want code-level control.

Making money with it

  • Client sites for local businesses. The classic play: charge $500-$3,000 per build on a $17/month Core plan, then $50-$150/month for care plans. Squarespace's all-in-one hosting means you inherit none of the WordPress update treadmill - the maintenance you sell is content and edits, not security patches.
  • Digital products and memberships. Advanced's zero-fee policy on digital content and memberships makes it a quiet alternative to creator platforms for selling courses or gated content - compare against Shopify when physical inventory enters the picture.
  • Fast landing funnels. For pure one-page funnels, Carrd at $19/year undercuts it badly - Squarespace earns its keep when the client needs more than one page.

Honest limitations

Lock-in is the big one: no self-hosting, limited export, and designs that do not travel to another platform - a decade of client work on Squarespace is a decade of Squarespace subscriptions. Template flexibility sits below Webflow for custom layouts. Renewal prices rise off the promotional annual rate. And the AI, while genuinely useful, is metered - heavy AI users are buying Advanced whether they like it or not. Questions we get asked

Squarespace or Wix AI in 2026?

Wix's AI builder interviews you and assembles a full site in minutes; Squarespace's Design Intelligence works inside better design defaults and lands closer to custom-agency results with less cleanup. Pick Wix AI for speed and the widest feature net, Squarespace for polished brand sites and simple commerce. The full field is compared in our AI website builder breakdown.

Can I sell on the Basic plan?

Yes - selling is included on every plan now. The catch is the 2% transaction fee on online store orders. At $1,000 in monthly sales that is $20/month, which already exceeds the $5/month gap between Basic ($12) and Core ($17) - so Core pays for itself almost immediately once the store is real.

Are the AI credits enough to build a full site?

On Basic, no - 10 one-time credits cover a taste of layout or copy generation, not a complete build. Core's 20/month handles steady small tasks; Advanced's 120/month sustains weekly content generation. Treat the AI as an accelerator inside a template-driven workflow, not as an autopilot.

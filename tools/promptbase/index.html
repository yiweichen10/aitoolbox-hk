What PromptBase Actually Is (From a Seller's Side)

PromptBase launched in 2022 and is now the largest dedicated marketplace for buying and selling AI prompts - tested prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion and friends, with listings priced from $1.99 to $19.99 and most sitting between $3 and $8. The pitch is simple: buyers pay a few dollars to skip trial and error; sellers get paid for prompts they already built.

The detail that matters: every submission goes through human review before it goes live, usually 1-3 days. Reviewers test whether the prompt actually does what the listing claims. That gate is the whole reason a prompt here can charge $6 when the same text would drown on a free prompt-sharing site.

💰 The Money Angle

Five ways this turns into income, in the order I would pursue them:

  • List tested prompts in a narrow niche. Not "10 ChatGPT prompts for writing" - that market is saturated. Think "cold email prompts for solar installers" or "Midjourney prompts for product photography on white backgrounds". Marketplace traffic finds you; PromptBase takes 20%.
  • Route your own audience through your referral link. This is the killer feature almost nobody uses properly: sales from your own link carry 0% commission. Post on X, Reddit or a newsletter with your link and you keep the full price.
  • Custom prompt jobs. Buyers commission you directly at a 10% commission rate. This is where real money lives - a business needing a bespoke prompt for a repeatable workflow will pay $50-200, not $5.
  • Package prompts as apps. The built-in App Builder turns a prompt into an embeddable interactive mini-app - a demo that also markets your listings.
  • Use it as demand research, then sell bigger elsewhere. Watch what sells, then bundle refined packs at $15-29 on Gumroad or Payhip where you keep more margin and own the buyer relationship.

The honest math: 80% of a $5 sale is $4. A storefront of 10 listings in a good niche might make $50-150/month. The sellers who treat PromptBase as a storefront for a prompt *system* - packs, updates, custom work - do meaningfully better than those who upload three prompts and wait.

❓ FAQ

How much can you realistically earn selling prompts on PromptBase?

Set expectations low, then beat them with volume and custom work. At a typical $3-8 price point, 80% revenue share means $2.40-6.40 per marketplace sale. A focused storefront of 10-20 listings in a narrow niche, promoted occasionally, plausibly makes $50-300/month; top sellers with strong preview images and regular new listings report four figures, and custom jobs (10% commission) are where individual jobs pay $50-200. Three generic listings uploaded once will earn close to nothing - the sellers making real money treat listings like a product line, with good example outputs and a clear niche.

Can buyers just copy and share my prompts after purchase?

Yes, trivially - a prompt is text with no watermark or license key, and there is no realistic technical protection. The practical defense is packaging: a listing that is a curated system (ordered variations, negative prompts, parameter notes, use-case guidance) is far more work to redistribute usefully than a few clever lines, and the human-review badge plus your review history is part of what buyers pay for. Price and structure accordingly instead of relying on protection that does not exist.

What happens to my listings when the AI model they were built for gets updated?

They degrade - models are revised two or three times a year, and a prompt tuned to a specific version drifts. You have three defensible options: push an updated version to past buyers where the platform allows it, sell a version-two pack as a separate listing, or state the exact model version on the listing and make no update promises. What is not defensible is implying lifetime updates and not shipping them - buyers review, and the marketplace's credibility rests on listings doing what they claim.

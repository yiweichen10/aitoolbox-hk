What Lemon Squeezy Is (From A Maker Who's Used It)

I've shipped a small software license and a template pack through Lemon Squeezy, so this is field notes, not a sales page. Lemon Squeezy is a merchant of record: when someone buys from you, Lemon Squeezy is the legal seller on the receipt. That one detail is the whole point. Because they're the seller, they calculate, collect, and file VAT, GST, and sales tax in more than 200 jurisdictions. You price in dollars, someone in Germany buys, and you never register for a German VAT number or hire a tax service. For a solo maker selling across borders, the removal of that paperwork is worth more than the fee looks at first glance.

How People Actually Make Money On It

The tool is just the checkout. The income is what you put behind it. What works:

1. Sell A Digital File, Once, Forever

An ebook, a Figma template, a Lightroom preset, a Notion dashboard, a sprite pack. Upload it, set a price of $9 to $49, and Lemon Squeezy delivers the file and handles tax on every country. A pack that sells 300 times at $19 is about $5,700, and you made it once. The 5% + 50¢ is the cost of not running a tax department.

2. License Keys For Software And Plugins

This is where Lemon Squeezy beats a plain processor. It generates and validates license keys out of the box, so if you sell a WordPress plugin, a desktop app, or a CLI tool, buyers get a working key at checkout and you get renewals without glue code. Subscriptions add 0.5%, but for software that is expected.

3. Turn Buyers Into Resellers

The built-in affiliate program is the part most people skip. Give a customer a tracked link, set a 20 to 30% commission, and they send you buyers for a cut of the sale. You pay only after money comes in. For a maker with an audience, this is cheaper customer acquisition than ads, and it runs while you sleep.

4. Recurring Revenue Without The Tax Headache

Subscriptions for a SaaS, a preset club, or a monthly asset drop. Lemon Squeezy bills, duns failed cards, and files the cross-border tax. The catch: the subscription surcharge plus international and PayPal fees can push a European PayPal sub to about 9%, so price for it or steer buyers to cards.

Where It Bites

Read the fee stack before you celebrate. The base is 5% + 50¢. International cards add 1.5%. PayPal adds 1.5%. Subscriptions add 0.5%. On a $5 product the fixed 50¢ alone is 10%, and the surcharges push it to 15%. Lemon Squeezy is a poor fit for high-volume, low-ticket items. There the flat fee eats you. It is also not your business shell: no course host, no community, no funnel builder. It moves money. Pair it with a host for the rest.

And the 2026 reality check: the platform is live, but new signups have been reported as gated, and it stopped taking new mainland-China developer applications after Stripe bought it in 2024. Its tech now feeds Stripe Managed Payments, a costlier successor. If you can't open an account, the features don't matter. Have Paddle or Polar ready as a fallback.

Who Should Reach For It

Pick Lemon Squeezy when you sell software, license keys, or digital downloads and want tax filed for you plus an affiliate engine, without a monthly bill. Skip it if you're moving thousands of $5 items, the fixed fee hurts, or if you need a full course-and-community platform. For a maker shipping a product to the world from a laptop, it removes the single most annoying part of going global: the tax.

  • Support slowed after the Stripe acquisition, with multi-week approvals reported. Fine once you're live. Frustrating while you wait to get approved.

❓ FAQ

How do I actually make money with Lemon Squeezy?

You list a digital product, an ebook, a template pack, a software license, a Notion dashboard, a preset, set a price, and Lemon Squeezy handles checkout, tax, and delivery. The angle that compounds is the affiliate program: give buyers a tracked link and they earn a cut for sending others your way, so you pay for growth only after it sells. Because tax is filed for you, you can price in dollars and sell to someone in Germany or Japan without touching their tax system. Most makers use it as the checkout layer under their own site or a storefront.

What does Lemon Squeezy cost, really?

The headline is 5% + 50¢ per transaction and there is no monthly fee. But the rate is stackable: a 1.5% surcharge applies to international cards, another 1.5% to PayPal, and 0.5% to recurring subscriptions. On a $50 US card sale you keep about $47. On a $50 European PayPal subscription the take can drop near $45.50. If you sell many cheap items the fixed 50¢ is the painful part, a $5 sale loses 15% before your own costs. The Sweet plan at $29/month lowers the effective rate but still charges the same base fee.

Lemon Squeezy or Gumroad or Paddle?

All three are merchants of record. Gumroad is the easiest place to drop a product and start, but takes ~10% + 50¢, double Lemon Squeezy's cut. Lemon Squeezy is built for software and license keys, with the best native affiliate tooling, at 5% + 50¢. Paddle matches the 5% + 50¢ but uses a blended rate with no international or PayPal surcharge, and it is the stronger pick once you clear a few thousand a month and want enterprise-grade compliance, at the cost of a slower, stricter onboarding. If you sell templates or code and want affiliates cheap, Lemon Squeezy fits. If you're scaling a SaaS, compare it against Paddle.

Can I still sign up in 2026?

The platform is live and processes payments, with no announced shutdown. But multiple 2026 reports describe new-merchant signups as waitlist or invite-gated, and sources say it stopped taking new mainland-China developer applications after the Stripe acquisition. Its technology feeds Stripe Managed Payments, a pricier successor. If you already hold an account, keep using it. If you're new, budget for the chance you can't open one and line up Paddle or Polar as a fallback before you build your checkout around it.

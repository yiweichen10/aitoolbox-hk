Lokalise Review 2026: What the Plan-Tier Bill Really Costs and How Agencies Resell Localization

Lokalise is the continuous-localization platform product teams reach for once they already ship to several markets and a spreadsheet plus a translator stops scaling. You connect a repo (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket), push source strings, and Lokalise returns every language through its API, CLI or over-the-air (OTA) updates so UI text can change without a new app release.

I have watched agencies bill clients off Lokalise and talked to teams that switched away from cheaper tools because the bill turned unpredictable. Here is what the platform does in 2026, what the restructured pricing actually costs, and where a solo operator or small agency can turn it into recurring revenue.

What Lokalise actually does

Lokalise sits between your source content and the languages you ship. You sync strings from a repo, a CMS, or upload files, and the platform keeps each target language in its own branch. When a string changes upstream, Lokalise flags it, machine-translates or routes it to a translator, and delivers approved text back through the API, CLI, webhooks, or OTA so mobile and web UIs update without a build.

The parts that matter day to day: translation memory (every approved sentence is reused and cuts the next bill), glossaries (product terms stay consistent), and QA checks that catch missing placeholders, broken tags, and mixed-up punctuation before anything ships. The CLI and mobile SDKs are the reason companies like Notion and Mastercard picked it - over-the-air pushes mean a typo fix lands in the app without waiting for the next release train.

The 2026 pricing model

Lokalise restructured its plans in 2026. The current ladder (annual billing) runs Explorer at $144/month, Growth at $375/month, Advanced from $999/month, and Enterprise as a custom quote. There is no true free plan; every tier opens with a 14-day trial and no card.

The billing philosophy is the opposite of Phrase's. Lokalise charges by plan tier with unlimited hosted words, while Phrase caps managed words and adds per-user seats. That matters because a product that adds features (more source words) never pushes your Lokalise bill up - but adding teammates does, since Advanced seats are the lever. For a team of 3-10 engineers in 5-15 languages, that is usually the more predictable bill.

AI is split in two. Standard AI and machine translation are unlimited on every plan. The Pro AI tier - context-aware translation with custom AI profiles backed by RAG, style guides and scoring - is metered: bundled at 40K-400K words per year depending on tier, then you either pay overage or move up a plan. If your output is steady, the bundle is fine; if a launch dumps 150K words in a month, you feel it.

How people make money with Lokalise

The clean play is localization-as-a-service, and the math is the same one translators have used for years, just cleaner with a platform behind it. Take a client's 5,000-word product and push it to 10 languages. That is 50,000 words of work. You invoice $4,000-6,000 for the first pass and a $300-1,500/month retainer to keep every release localized. Your Lokalise cost on Explorer is $144/month, and machine translation covers most strings, so human post-editing is the only real variable. The margin is the point: you are not selling hours, you are selling an outcome the client cannot produce alone.

Three concrete angles:

  • App and website localization retainers for small software firms that have outgrown Google Sheets but cannot justify a full localization hire.
  • Bolt a translation-ops line onto an existing web, app, or SEO agency - you already have the client relationship, Lokalise is the engine.
  • The Lokalise partner program pays agencies to bring on accounts, so a resold seat plus your service fee stacks.

Where it falls short

Lokalise has no free plan, and the floor at $144/month is high for a solo founder. The Explorer-to-Growth jump is 2.6x, so a small team that crosses the AI-word bundle can feel the pinch fast. Pro AI overage is quiet - the dashboard does not wave a flag before you burn through 40K words. And like every TMS, AI pre-translation is strong on UI strings and shaky on marketing or legal copy, so a human still touches the high-stakes text.

Lokalise vs Crowdin

If the client is price-sensitive and the job is small, Crowdin wins on the free tier and $50/month Pro. Lokalise wins when the team is bigger, the word count grows, and unlimited hosted words removes the multiplying-by-languages surprise. FAQ

Does Lokalise have a free plan?

No. Every tier starts with a 14-day trial and no credit card, but there is no permanent free tier. If you need a working free product, Crowdin's free plan is the better starting point.

How does Lokalise bill - words or seats?

By plan tier with unlimited hosted words. You pay for the tier, not the word count, so adding languages does not raise the bill. Adding seats on Advanced and above does. That is the inverse of Phrase, which caps managed words and charges per user.

Can I make money with Lokalise if I am not a translator?

Yes, and most people who do are not translators. Sell the outcome: a localized product shipped on every release. A 5,000-word app into 10 languages invoices $4,000-6,000 with a monthly retainer on top, while your tool cost stays at $144/month on Explorer. The partner program adds reseller revenue on top.

What is the difference between Standard AI and Pro AI?

Standard AI and machine translation are unlimited on every plan and handle the bulk of UI strings. Pro AI adds context-aware translation with custom AI profiles (brand voice, terminology, scoring) and is metered annually - 40K words on Growth, scaling to 400K on higher tiers. Choose Pro AI when consistency across languages matters more than raw volume.

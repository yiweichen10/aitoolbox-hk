What Chatbase Actually Is

Chatbase is a no-code platform for building AI support agents trained on a business's own material - paste a website URL, upload the PDFs, connect Notion, and you have a bot that answers customer questions in that company's voice. Founded by Yasser Elsaid, it now serves 10,000+ businesses, runs SOC 2 Type II and GDPR compliance on every plan, and deploys the same agent across website chat, WhatsApp, email, Slack, Instagram, and phone. Under the hood it is model-agnostic: each agent can run on OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, DeepSeek, Meta, or Mistral models, so you are never locked to one provider's pricing or quality.

But the reason this page sits in the money-tools row of this site is not the widget - it is the spread between what Chatbase costs and what a working AI support agent is worth to a small business.

The Money Angle

The reseller spread is the headline. A local business - HVAC, dental, law, e-commerce - loses money every time a customer question goes unanswered at 9pm. They will pay $150-$500/month for a competent AI agent that answers from their own docs, handles order lookups, and hands off to a human with full context. Your cost to deliver that: a $40/mo Hobby subscription, an afternoon of setup, and the discipline to keep the training data fresh. Five clients on a $150 Standard plan nets roughly $850/month; ten clients put you past $2,000. The workflow that works: niche down (dental clinics, Shopify stores), build one polished template, and sell setup + monthly maintenance rather than one-off builds - Contra or Fiverr can carry the first clients, then referrals take over.

Own-product angle. If you run a store or SaaS, the same $40 buys a support bot that deflects most ticket volume. One avoided VA shift pays the subscription several times over - and the built-in Helpdesk means the tickets the bot cannot handle route to a human queue with AI summaries attached.

The portfolio trap to avoid. The free plan deletes agents after 14 days of inactivity, so never host client bots on Free - and budget the $99/month branding removal only when a client contract justifies it.

The Honest Cons

The credit model means your cost scales with your clients' success - a viral product launch for your client is your invoice. Seat counts are stingy next to competitors that sell unlimited seats, and the $99 branding removal on a $40 plan is a 250% markup for exactly the feature agencies want most. Voice, API, and the Helpdesk all live on Standard or above, so the Hobby tier is genuinely a one-bot plan. And Chatbase is now a full customer-support platform, not a hobby widget builder - if your project is a simple FAQ bot for a blog, the pricing page will feel like paying for a helpdesk you do not use.

Questions we get asked

How do people actually make money with Chatbase?

The dominant play is agency reselling: subscribe at Hobby ($40/mo), build a support agent trained on a local business's docs, and charge $150-$500/month for setup plus a retainer. Five clients at $200 covers your Standard plan ($150) and nets roughly $850/month. The second play is product-led: a support bot for your own Shopify store that deflects tickets you would otherwise pay a VA to answer. White-label reselling at scale needs Enterprise.

What happens when a client's bot exceeds its message credits?

Your subscription credits run out and service stops or degrades unless auto-recharge is on, which adds non-expiring credits at $40 per 1,000. Serious resellers put auto-recharge on and price client contracts above their worst-case credit burn - support volume is seasonal (holidays spike it), and eating a surprise $120 recharge bill is how reseller margins die.

Chatbase vs Voiceflow or Botpress - which should an agency pick?

Chatbase is the fastest to ship: paste a website, get a working agent the same afternoon. Voiceflow is stronger for complex conversation design with teams and client review flows; Botpress is more flexible for developers but slower to deliver. If your clients are SMBs that need 'a bot that answers questions from our docs,' Chatbase wins on time-to-invoice. For deeper comparisons see our chatbot builder breakdown.

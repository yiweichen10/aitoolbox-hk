eBay is the marketplace everyone forgot and nobody should have. While seller applications, waitlists and approval queues swallowed the rest of e-commerce, eBay still works the way it did in 2005: create an account, photograph the thing, list it, and 130 million+ active buyers can find it by tomorrow morning. No monthly fee unless you want one, no interview, 250 free listings every month. For anyone testing whether reselling can pay - and for anyone sitting on assets the internet wants - it remains the fastest on-ramp in commerce.

I have sold on eBay on and off since 2019, mostly vintage audio gear and discontinued electronics, with a season of thrift flipping in between. The honest picture: the fee stack is heavier than the "13%" headline (count shipping and tax in the base, plus a fixed per-order fee), buyer protection leans buyer, and the customers belong to eBay. What keeps me listing there is simple - for used and one-of-a-kind items, nowhere else has this many buyers who want the exact thing in my hands.

The Money Angle

  • Flipping sourced goods. The core side-income engine: thrift stores, estate sales, clearance and marketplace freebies become $30-150 resales. Margin math absorbs fees here - buy at $8, sell at $60, and the ~$9.10 in fees (13.6% + $0.40) still leaves a real spread. This is the category where eBay's take rate is genuinely fair.
  • Sell the specific, not the generic. eBay's search demand is model-level: part numbers, editions, years, colors. Discontinued electronics, out-of-print media, obsolete accessories, replacement parts. Nobody Googles for these; collectors and repair techs search eBay directly, and you are one of three listings worldwide.
  • Auctions for the unpriceable. Rare and one-of-one items (collectibles, graded cards, signed goods) belong in auctions, where competing bidders discover the price instead of you guessing it. Fixed-price stores leave money on the table on exactly these items.
  • Offload the inventory other platforms reject. Returns, open-box, refurbished, b-stock. Amazon restricts used conditions in many categories; eBay welcomes them. If you also make new goods - print-on-demand shirts, craft supplies - know that eBay works for it but Etsy and Printify-powered shops usually convert better for handmade and custom niches.
  • Run it as a discipline, not a lottery. The sellers who profit treat it like retail: a sourcing schedule, a pricing discipline (sold-comps, not asks), and a shipping workflow. The tools are all in Seller Hub, and Terapeak research comes with Store subscriptions when volume justifies one.

The Honest Cons

  • The fee base is everything the buyer pays. Item, shipping you charge, even sales tax. Pricing "low item + high shipping" saves nothing (eBay closed that in 2019) - the only levers are sourcing cheaper and shipping cheaper.
  • Low-ticket items get eaten alive. On a $15 sale, fees run about $2.44 - 16%+ effective. The fixed fee makes anything under $20 a volume game or a bundle play.
  • Buyer protection has teeth, and they point at you. "Not as described" claims mean return shipping and refunds from your side. Accurate listings, real photos of the actual item, and prompt replies are not optional hygiene; they are the fee-avoidance strategy.
  • New accounts start on a leash. Selling limits, category restrictions, and payout holds ease up with history. Plan your first two months around small wins instead of listing your entire inventory on day one.
  • You are renting the buyer, not owning them. No emails, no remarketing, no brand. Flippers do not care; anyone building a repeat-purchase product line should treat eBay as one channel and push repeat customers toward a store they control - or graduate whole categories to Shopify when volume supports the $39/mo.

Bottom Line

eBay is the fastest "turn stuff into money" channel on the internet: no approval, no monthly bill, immediate access to 130M+ buyers whose search behavior matches used, rare and discontinued inventory perfectly. The costs of that access are a 14-18% effective take rate on typical orders, buyer-protection risk, and zero customer ownership. Flippers, collectors and anyone liquidating assets should start here today; product brands should use it as a demand test and a channel, then build toward a store they own. Price on sold comps, photograph the real item, ship fast - the fee schedule is public and survivable, and the demand is 30 years deep.

Questions we get asked

What percentage does eBay actually take per sale?

Plan on 13.6% of the total amount (including shipping and sales tax) in most categories, plus a fixed per-order fee of $0.30 (orders up to $10) or $0.40 above. Cross-border orders add 1.65%. On a $50 order that is roughly $7.20 - and effective take rates on small orders run higher because of the fixed fee.

Does eBay take a cut of shipping?

Yes. The final value fee applies to everything the buyer pays - item price, shipping you charge, handling and sales tax. Packing shipping into the item price with free shipping does not dodge the fee; it only changes which line item it is calculated on.

Is eBay or Etsy better for selling in 2026?

Different markets. eBay owns used, vintage, electronics and collectibles demand; Etsy owns handmade and craft supplies and charges less overall (~6.5% + processing plus listing fees, roughly 10-13% effective). If your item is one-of-a-kind old, eBay's search demand is stronger; if it is handmade at scale, Etsy's audience converts better.

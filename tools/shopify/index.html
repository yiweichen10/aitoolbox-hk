Shopify Review 2026: How People Actually Make Money With It (and What You Pay)

Every "make money online" video ends with someone pointing at a Shopify dashboard. Most of those videos skip the part where the store owner is still paying rent on software they barely use. I have built stores for clients and watched agencies run their whole book of business on Shopify. Here is what it is, where the money actually shows up, and where the bill bites.

What Shopify actually is

Shopify is not a marketplace. It is the frame your store hangs on: hosting, checkout, inventory, discounts, and an admin that a non-developer can run. On top of that frame sit the parts people call "AI" now — Shopify Magic for product descriptions and email copy, Sidekick (a chat assistant that pulls store data and drafts changes), Shopify Inbox for customer chat, and semantic search that tries to match messy queries to your catalog. Sidekick can read your reports and even push edits to a theme, which is genuinely useful when you are dragging a store across a launch.

The honest read: the AI is a speed boost inside a mature product. It will not pick your niche, source your products, or fix a store that has no traffic. The store still needs a reason to exist.

Where the money is

This is the part the dashboards never show. Five ways people turn Shopify into income, ranked by how realistic they are for a beginner:

  • Build stores for other people. A small brand that sells on Instagram and finally wants a real site will pay $2,000 to $10,000 for a clean build and $300 to $2,000 a month to keep it running. You do not need to be a developer — a handful of themes and a few apps get most stores to launch. The skill that sells is listening to what the owner actually wants, not writing code.
  • Shopify Partners referrals. Joining the Partner program is free. When you send a new merchant to Shopify, you earn a 20% recurring cut of their monthly plan for the first twelve months. Send someone to Shopify Plus and the bounty can reach $2,300 for that one referral. If you run a YouTube channel, a newsletter, or a coaching group, this is the lowest-effort income here — you point, they sign up, you collect.
  • Sell your own products. Dropshipping and print-on-demand still work for people who pick a narrow niche and write copy that does not sound like everyone else. The trap is ad spend: the store is cheap, the traffic is not. Treat the $39 Basic plan as the easy part.
  • Build apps or themes. If you can ship a Shopify app, the App Store pays you roughly 80 cents on the dollar after Shopify's cut; themes pay a 70/30 split in your favor. This is the highest-ceiling path and the highest-skill one. A single useful app can out-earn a year of client work.
  • Run a micro-agency. Bundle store build, email (pair it with a tool like Klaviyo), and ad management into one retainer. The stores that pay you are the ones that already have orders and need someone to run the machine — not the ones hoping Shopify will save them.

Pricing

Shopify charges a flat plan plus a cut of every sale (unless you wire in your own gateway, which adds a fee instead). US monthly prices for 2026:

Two things to keep in mind. First, the transaction fee above is what you pay using Shopify Payments; if your country cannot use Shopify Payments and you plug in Stripe or PayPal, Shopify adds 0.5% to 2.0% on top. Second, the plan price is the floor, not the total. Most stores add apps — reviews, upsells, subscriptions, loyalty — and those line items stack until the "cheap" $39 store quietly costs $120 a month. POS Pro is another $89 per location if you sell in person.

Pros

  • The checkout is the reason stores stay. Shopify has converted so many buyers that Shop Pay alone pushes conversion for many merchants, and you get it without configuring anything.
  • Sidekick and Shopify Magic remove busywork: draft a product description, summarize a slow report, or push a small theme edit by chatting.
  • The app ecosystem is unmatched. Whatever the store needs, an app exists, which means you rarely build custom.
  • Resale value is real. A store on Shopify with clean financials sells for a multiple of profit on exchange marketplaces — the platform itself adds buyer confidence.

Cons

  • App creep is the silent tax. Every "just one more" app adds a monthly fee and sometimes a performance hit, and owners rarely cancel the ones they stop using.
  • Transaction fees punish anyone outside Shopify Payments countries; the extra 0.5% to 2.0% on Basic stings on thin margins.
  • Lock-in is genuine. Products, themes, and flows do not move cleanly to another platform, so leaving means rebuilding.
  • The AI will not save a store with no traffic or no offer. Sidekick is a helper, not a growth engine, and the marketing emails still need a human who understands the customer.

Who should use it

Pick Shopify if you are serious about running a store for more than three months. If you want to test one product idea this weekend, the $5 Starter plan is the lowest-risk way to take a real order. If you are an agency or freelancer, the Partner program turns the stores you already recommend into recurring income.

Skip it if you only need a landing page — a website builder will do that for less — or if your country has no Shopify Payments and your margins are thin, because the extra gateway fee will eat you alive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you actually make money with Shopify as a beginner?

Yes, but not the way the videos imply. The realistic beginner paths are building stores for local businesses ($2,000 to $10,000 per build plus a monthly retainer) and the free Shopify Partners affiliate (20% recurring on referred plans, up to $2,300 for a Plus referral). Running your own store works only if you solve traffic and an offer first; the $39 plan is the easy part.

How much does Shopify really cost per month?

The plan is the floor. Basic is $39, Shopify $105, Advanced $399, Plus $2,300 billed annually. Add the per-order fee (2.9% + 30 cents on Basic with Shopify Payments) and the apps most stores end up running, and a "Basic" store often lands near $120 a month. Outside Shopify Payments countries, expect an extra 0.5% to 2.0% gateway fee.

Is Shopify worth it for a small business?

For a business with real orders and a list, yes — the checkout and Shop Pay lift conversion enough to cover the bill. For a hobby or a one-product test, start on the $5 Starter plan and upgrade only when orders justify it. Do not pay for Advanced features you will never open.

Does Shopify's AI actually help, or is it a gimmick?

It helps with busywork, not strategy. Magic drafts descriptions and email copy, Sidekick reads your reports and can push small theme edits, and semantic search catches messy queries. None of it finds your customers or fixes a weak offer. Treat it as a faster pair of hands, not a replacement for knowing your store.

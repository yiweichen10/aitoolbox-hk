Klaviyo Review 2026: How People Actually Make Money With It (and What the Bill Looks Like)

Every week someone launches another 'AI agent that writes your emails', and every week a store owner tells me their welcome email has not been touched since 2023. Klaviyo is not the shiny thing in that story. It is the boring platform underneath it — the customer database, the flows, the segments and the sending that most Shopify and DTC brands already pay for. Which is exactly why it is a decent business to build on: the demand is not for a tool, it is for someone to finally run it.

I have run Klaviyo accounts for small stores and watched agencies live off it. Here is what it does, where the money is, and where the bill gets ugly.

What Klaviyo actually does

Under the marketing copy, Klaviyo is four things stacked together: a customer database (profiles, events and purchase history pulled from Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce or a custom API), a segmentation engine, an automation builder for flows, and a sending engine for email and SMS.

The AI parts are real but narrower than the ads suggest. There is an AI subject line generator and AI copy help in the campaign builder. There is Composer, an assistant that answers questions about your own data and can draft campaigns and flows. There is Customer Agent, a support layer that reads tickets with the customer's order history attached. The one that actually changes retention strategy — predictive CLV, churn risk and RFM segmentation — is not in the base plan; it sits behind the Marketing Analytics add-on.

So the honest summary: Klaviyo's AI helps you move faster inside the platform. It does not replace the judgement of knowing which flow to build and which segment is worth mailing.

Where the money is

Do not build a business on reselling Klaviyo. There is no fat margin in the subscription, and clients do not care which sending platform you use. Build on the gap between 'has a list' and 'has someone managing the list'.

Three offers that work right now:

  • One-time flow build ($1,000-$3,000). A new store usually has zero or one live flow. You build the core set — welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandonment, post-purchase, winback — clean the list and set up domain authentication. It is a two-to-three day job once you have a checklist.
  • Monthly lifecycle retainer ($1,500-$5,000/mo per brand). Flows kept tuned, two to four campaigns a month, segment work, and a monthly report with revenue attributed. This is the real business, and it renews because email keeps producing revenue.
  • Klaviyo Solutions Partner revenue share (10-20%). Join the partner program and accounts you refer pay you a percentage of their spend. Treat it as a supplement — the discount that makes your own tool cost disappear, not the plan itself.

The retainer math

A store with 10,000 active profiles pays $150/mo for the Email plan, or $165/mo with the SMS minimum. Charge $2,500/mo for management and you are at roughly $2,350/mo gross on a single client, before your hours. Four clients at that size is a small agency.

Two things to keep straight. Klaviyo bills each store as its own account, so you are not pooling client cost onto one subscription. And you are paid for the work, not the dashboard — a client who gets a chart with no recommendations cancels in month two.

If you want a number to sell with, use Klaviyo's own October 2026 benchmark report: abandoned-cart flows average $6.77 in revenue per recipient on email and $4.48 on SMS, with the top 10% of brands at $13.70 and $8.69. That is per person who receives the message, not per click. It makes the retainer conversation much shorter.

The table hides more than it shows:

  • You pay per active profile, not per email sent. Since February 2025 the bill covers every profile in the account that is not suppressed, whether you mail them or not. A list you never clean is a bill you keep paying.
  • Tiers upgrade themselves. Cross a profile threshold and Klaviyo moves you up on the next cycle. Turn on auto-downgrade in billing settings, or you quietly stay on the higher tier after a purge.
  • The 90-day suppression lock. Suppress a contact and they leave your bill — but once you unsuppress them, you cannot suppress them again for 90 days. Unsending a re-engagement campaign costs you three billing cycles.
  • SMS is an add-on with its own floor. Mobile messages start at $15/mo for 1,250 credits. A US SMS is one credit, an MMS is three, a UK SMS is five, and Klaviyo covers 24 countries — check the credit rate before you promise a client global SMS.
  • The useful analytics cost extra. Reviews start at $25/mo, Marketing Analytics at $100/mo and the Customer Data Platform at $500/mo. Predictive CLV and churn sit in Marketing Analytics, not the base plan.
  • Cross $10,000/mo in spend and Klaviyo One kicks in with a mandatory 20% surcharge. Self-serve plans have no annual discount, so there is no cheaper way to commit.

Pros

  • The data model is the reason to stay. Purchase events, profiles and segments live in one place, so revenue attribution in Klaviyo is closer to reality than in tools that only see opens.
  • Flows are the strongest part of the product. The builder handles the branching and wait logic small stores actually need, without a developer.
  • Shopify integration is genuinely deep. Products, orders and discounts sync without glue code, which is why DTC brands default to it.
  • It is a recognised platform. When you pitch a retention retainer, saying you manage Klaviyo needs no explanation, and that is worth something in a sales call.

Cons

  • The bill grows whether or not you improve. You pay for list size, not results, and the jumps at the high tiers are steep — 10,000 to 25,000 profiles is $250/mo more for the same features.
  • The AI is a helper, not the product. Subject lines, copy and a data assistant are nice. Predictive segmentation, the part that changes retention strategy, is a paid add-on.
  • No agency console. Every client is a separate account with a separate login, so managing ten brands means ten bills and ten places to check. It works, but it does not scale gracefully.
  • Deliverability still depends on you. A clean list, authenticated domain and sane sending volume are your job. Klaviyo will happily let you burn your own reputation.
  • The habit is hard to break. Templates, flows and segments do not move cleanly to another platform, and migration is a real project, so the switching cost keeps you there even when the price stings.

FAQ

Is Klaviyo worth it for a small store?

For a store under 500 active profiles the $20/mo Email plan is easy to justify if you actually build flows. If you are only sending a newsletter, a cheaper tool does the same job. The value shows up once the automated flows — welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase — are live and producing revenue you can see in the dashboard.

Can I make money selling Klaviyo services?

Yes, but you sell the service, not the software. The realistic path is a one-time flow build at $1,000-$3,000, then a $1,500-$5,000/mo retainer to keep it running, plus the 10-20% partner revenue share on accounts you refer. Nothing about that requires you to be a developer.

How much does Klaviyo cost for 10,000 contacts?

The Email plan is $150/mo for 10,000 active profiles with a 100,000-email monthly send limit. Adding SMS starts at another $15/mo for 1,250 credits. Marketing Analytics, if you want the predictive reporting, is a separate $100/mo from 13,500 profiles.

Why did my Klaviyo bill go up without sending more email?

Because billing moved to active profiles in February 2025. You now pay for every profile that is not suppressed, even ones you never mail. Suppressing unengaged contacts and turning on auto-downgrade are the two settings that bring the number back down.

Verdict

Klaviyo is not exciting, and that is fine. It is the platform most e-commerce stores already run, it handles email and SMS in one place, and its flows produce revenue you can point at. The AI features make you faster inside the tool; they do not do the thinking.

The pricing is the part to respect. Active-profile billing punishes messy lists, the add-ons stack up, and there is no annual discount to soften it. But if you sell retention as a service, a $150/mo account that produces $2,500/mo in fees is a good trade, and Klaviyo is the platform clients already trust.

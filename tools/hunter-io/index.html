Money Angle

  • Lead-gen as a service runs on credits. The freelance offer: "I'll build you a verified list of 500 decision-makers in your niche." At Starter's 2,000 monthly credits that costs under $34 in tooling and sells for $300-1,000 depending on niche and verification depth - the lead enrichment comparison maps the bigger versions of that stack.
  • The free plan is a product feature. API and MCP on the $0 tier means you can wire email-finding into a client dashboard or a Browse.ai monitoring pipeline and charge for the workflow, not the tool.
  • Sequences close the loop. Connect 1-20 email accounts (3 on Starter), write with the AI assistant, rotate inboxes, A/B test subject lines - a solo operator runs the whole cold email motion without a separate sender subscription, until volume says otherwise.
  • Recruiting sourcing, same meters. Candidate outreach runs on the identical credits - agencies bill sourcing the same way they bill lead lists.
  • Signals is a quiet retention engine. The dashboard flags when saved leads change jobs or companies post signals worth acting on - "your lead just moved to a new company" emails are the easiest touch a freelancer can sell.

The Honest Cons

  • Credits burn on mistakes. A mis-typed domain or a hard-to-verify corporate domain can eat several lookups; the credit math only works with targeting discipline.
  • No rollover, either mode. Monthly resets wipe unused credits; annual billing dumps a year at once - an idle month is a wasted month on both.
  • Sequences are capped and shallower than specialists. 500-15,000 recipients per sequence trails dedicated senders like Instantly or Smartlead on inbox rotation depth and deliverability tooling.
  • Data coverage varies by region. Small companies and some markets return sparse results - test your niche on the free 50 before buying anything.
  • AI usage is metered separately. Discover AI searches (30-900/mo) are their own quota next to credits.

Bottom Line

Hunter.io is the honest entry point into paid prospecting: the free tier is genuinely usable, the paid tiers scale by credits instead of seats, and the data has earned its reputation over a decade. It stops being enough when your volume or deliverability needs outgrow its sequences - and by then, the credit-burning habits you built here are exactly what the pricier stacks reward. Start free, upgrade to Starter when prospecting becomes weekly, and treat every credit like it has a customer's name on it.

Questions we get asked

What exactly is one credit?

One credit covers one Email Finder lookup, one Email Verifier check, or one Domain Search result set. Credits reset monthly (or arrive as a yearly lump on annual billing) and never roll over. At Starter's 2,000 monthly credits, a typical freelance workflow - verify 50 prospects, enrich 20, search 10 domains - burns maybe 150-300 credits a week.

Hunter vs Apollo vs Clay - do I need all three?

They overlap but lead differently. Hunter is strongest at finding and verifying direct professional emails and now sends sequences too. Apollo is a bigger B2B database with its own outbound stack; Clay orchestrates multiple data providers into waterfall enrichment at agency prices. Many freelancers start on Hunter's free plan, move to Apollo when database breadth matters, and graduate to Clay when running client programs.

Is the free plan actually usable?

Yes, unusually so: 50 credits a month, unlimited team members, API and MCP, and 500 recipients per sequence. It covers a light validation workflow or a single side project. The moment prospecting becomes a weekly habit, Starter at $34/mo annual is the realistic entry.

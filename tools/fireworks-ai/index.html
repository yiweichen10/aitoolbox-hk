The AI API market has two tiers: the first-party giants (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) and the open-model platforms that serve Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek and friends at a fraction of the price. Fireworks AI sits in the second tier with a clear pitch - the same open models everyone sells, but with serving modes that cut real bills by 30-50% and GPU economics that go all the way down to bare metal.

The rate card in plain terms

Everything bills per token against prepaid credits - no subscription. Highlights per 1M tokens (input/output): Kimi K3 at $3/$15, Kimi K3.1 Pro at $2/$10, Qwen 3.8 Max at $2/$6, GLM 5.3 at $1.40/$4.40, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at $0.30/$1.20, GPT OSS 120B at $0.15/$0.60, GLM 5.3 Flash at $0.15/$0.50, and NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B at $0.05/$0.20. Embeddings start at $0.008 per 1M input tokens.

The modes are where it gets interesting. Priority costs 25% more to jump the queue at peak. Batch takes 50% off both input and output for jobs that can wait. Spot takes 30% off on best-effort capacity you can retry. Prompt caching bills cached input at half price or less with no write fee, and the docs claim hit rates up to 99.6% on agentic workloads where the same context turns over and over. If you outgrow shared serverless, on-demand GPUs bill per minute - H100 and H200 at $8/hour, B200 at $13, B300 at $15, GB300 at $20 - and idle deployments scale to zero after an hour. Reserved Throughput buys an SLA by the minute; Sovereign puts Fireworks inside your own cloud.

Where the savings actually come from

A realistic agent workload - 20M input and 4M output tokens a month on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash - costs about $10.80 standard. Move the background half to Batch and cache the repeated system context, and the same workload lands meaningfully lower without changing a line of product code. On premium models the spread is bigger: GLM 5.3 at $1.40/$4.40 becomes $0.70/$2.20 under Batch. That is the whole game: Fireworks does not win on sticker price (Together's is near-identical), it wins on how little of the sticker you end up paying.

Making money with Fireworks

  • Client AI features on retainer. A customer-support summarizer burning 10M input and 4M output tokens a month on GLM 5.3 Flash costs roughly $3.50. Charge $200-$500 a month for the feature and the margin does the selling - the risk is not inference cost, it is your dev time. The same math powers the pricing tables in the LLM API price comparison.
  • Fine-tune-and-serve for verticals. Take a client's domain data, LoRA-tune a small model, serve it on serverless - the single-vendor loop keeps margins honest because training and serving share one rate card.
  • Batch products nobody prices. Lead scoring, content tagging, document pipelines: Batch mode's flat 50% cut turns barely-profitable data services into obviously-profitable ones.

Fireworks vs the field

Together AI is the closest twin - comparable prices, bigger model catalog, similar API. Fireworks counters with Batch/Spot modes and more flexible GPU reservations. OpenRouter is an aggregator, not an engine - ideal for experimentation across hundreds of models, weaker when one model needs production tuning. Groq owns ultra-low latency on a smaller model set. fal-ai covers the image/video side of the house, and Replicate remains the model-zoo play for hobby-scale deploys. The full landscape is mapped in the AI API platform comparison.

Honest limitations

No first-party frontier models: if a client's workload needs GPT-class reasoning or Claude's writing, Fireworks is not the vendor - route that traffic elsewhere. No free tier means every experiment bills something. Dedicated deployments assume you can operate an API product. And rates move often enough that you should re-check the pricing page each quarter before renewing client quotes. For teams shipping on open models at volume, though, the Batch-plus-caching combination is the best cost lever in this tier. Verify current rates at fireworks.ai/pricing.

Questions we get asked

Is there a free tier?

No standing free tier: you prepay credits and every request draws them down. The floor is NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning 30B at $0.05 per 1M input and $0.20 per 1M output tokens - roughly 2,000 full-page summaries for a dollar - so a month of testing stays in pocket-change territory even without free credits.

Fireworks vs Together AI vs OpenRouter - which one for client work?

All three serve open models at similar token prices - GLM 5.3 lands at $1.40/$4.40 per 1M on both Fireworks and Together AI. Fireworks differentiates on serving modes: Batch at -50% and Spot at -30% cut costs for pipelines that tolerate delay or retries, and Reserved Throughput adds an SLA when traffic firms up. OpenRouter is the better aggregator when you want hundreds of models behind one key; Fireworks is the better engine when one model carries your product.

Can I serve my own fine-tuned model?

Yes - that is half the pitch. Upload LoRA adapters to run on serverless, or deploy dedicated models on on-demand GPUs (H100 at $8/hr, scaling to zero when idle). The Training API bills fine-tuning by GPU-hour from the same rate card, so you can tune a niche model and serve it without changing vendors.

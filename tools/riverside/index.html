What Riverside Actually Is

Riverside is a browser-based recording studio for podcasts and video interviews. Its defining feature is local recording: every participant's audio and video is captured on their own device in up to 4K video and 48kHz audio, then uploaded progressively to the cloud. When a guest's connection stutters, the call looks bad but your files stay pristine - the reason it replaced Zoom recordings on most professional shows. Around that core: a text-based editor (cut audio by deleting words from the transcript), an AI editing agent, Magic Clips for social repurposing, AI show notes, a teleprompter, and podcast hosting with publishing to Spotify, Apple and YouTube.

It is the recording-first tool in its field: Descript is editing-first with weaker recording, Wondercraft is production-first for scripted and audio-only shows, and Opus Clip handles only the clipping stage.

The Money Angle

Podcast money comes from three places, and Riverside touches all of them:

  • Sponsorships - the classic path: consistent episodes, then ad slots. Transcripts and AI show notes make sponsor packets and attribution easier to produce. Distribution and monetization live elsewhere: Spotify for Creators for podcast monetization and YouTube for video revenue.
  • Audience growth - Magic Clips converts one interview into a week of shorts for TikTok and Shorts. Consistent clipping is the highest-impact growth habit for shows under 1,000 listeners, and it is the feature most likely to earn its keep.
  • Selling the production itself - agencies and freelancers run client podcasts through Riverside: the guest-link workflow (no accounts, no installs) is why production shops book it, and a $24/mo Pro seat against a monthly podcast-management retainer is one of the better margins in content services. Be honest about the ceiling: a recorder does not create demand. Shows that earn started with an audience or a niche worth sponsoring; Riverside's job is making you sound worth sponsoring.

The Recording Reality

Two caps decide your tier. Separate-track downloads (the files you export for serious editing in other tools) run 15/20/25 hours per month on Pro/Grow/Webinar - recording and editing inside Riverside are not capped the same way, but interview-heavy shows that polish elsewhere will feel the wall. And the host needs a desktop browser; guests join from any device. If you record weekly hour-long episodes with two guests, Pro's 15 hours covers it; daily shows do not.

The Honest Cons

The free tier is a taste: roughly 2 hours a month at 720p with watermarked exports pushes real shows to Pro almost immediately. Download caps punish high-volume exporters. AI show notes need rewriting before they are publishable. Live streaming on Grow is capable but not a produced-broadcast desk. And there is a small documentation problem: Riverside's own blog has described conflicting plan lineups within the same year, so always sanity-check the live pricing page before budgeting.

Bottom Line

If you record conversations with humans, Riverside is the default answer in 2026: local tracks are the quality floor that separates a sponsor-ready show from a Zoom recording, and the AI repurposing fixes the promotion chore that kills most new podcasts. Start on the free tier, take the 14-day Pro trial before your first real interview block, and go Grow only when you actually schedule social posts weekly. Pair it with Descript for heavy post-production and Opus Clip for a dedicated clipping engine; for the wider stack, see our podcast tools comparison and video editing comparison.

Questions we get asked

Is Riverside free?

The current official pricing page (checked 2026-11-20) lists a Free plan at $0 with limited multi-track recording - about 2 hours a month, 720p and watermarked exports - plus 14-day trials of the paid plans. Be aware that some of Riverside's own blog posts and older reviews say the free plan was discontinued; the pricing page is the source of truth.

Riverside vs Descript - which one do I need?

Riverside is recording-first: pristine local tracks and a guest-link workflow, with editing included. Descript is editing-first - stronger text-based production and studio sound tools, weaker recording. Many shows record in Riverside and do heavy post in Descript; if you record interviews with remote guests, start with Riverside and see if you ever need the second subscription.

What do you actually need to launch a podcast that earns?

Pro at $24/mo annual is the practical floor - no watermark, 4K tracks, Magic Clips and hosting. The money then comes from sponsorships, affiliates and funnels to your own products, and distribution/monetization live in Spotify for Creators and YouTube, not in the recorder. Consistency and a sponsorable niche matter more than gear; the recorder's job is making you sound worth sponsoring.

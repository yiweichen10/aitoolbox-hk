Money Angle

  • Membership revenue is the business model. 100 members at $29/mo is $2,900/mo gross; on Professional after the 2% fee and Stripe, roughly $2,780 lands. Your costs are $89 and content. Compare that stack against Patreon (10% platform fee) or Whop marketplace sales (30%) and the arithmetic is the argument.
  • Courses ride on the same subscription. A Kajabi setup runs $179-499/mo for courses alone; Circle includes them on the $89 plan with events and community attached - the bundle IS the margin.
  • AI credits make the community look staffed. Members ask the same questions forever; Circle AI (2,500 credits/mo) drafts answers and summaries, so a solo operator can run a 1,000-member room without hiring a community manager at $3-4k/mo.
  • Free community, paid inner circle. The standard funnel: open spaces convert strangers into members, paid spaces (priced separately) convert members into revenue - Circle prices spaces independently, so one workspace runs both.
  • The community is the retention. Courses get consumed and cancelled; communities renew because leaving means losing the people. That is why membership businesses outlast course businesses on the same content.

The Honest Cons

  • The fee stack is real. Professional's 2% platform fee plus Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 means ~5% of revenue leaves before taxes; $199/mo buys that back to 1% - the upgrade is a math problem, and Circle knows it.
  • API starts at Business. The $89 plan cannot connect Zapier-style automations to member data, which stings for anyone running automated workflows around their community.
  • Add-on creep. Email Hub, branded apps, even custom profile fields cost extra on lower tiers - the invoice grows with ambition.
  • Plus tier is opaque. 0.5% fees and concierge onboarding sit behind "talk to sales" with no public price.
  • Not free. There is a trial but no free tier - unlike Ghost self-hosted (MIT, $0 software) or Discord. The convenience has a list price.

Bottom Line

Circle is the serious version of "build a paid community": courses, events, payments and AI support in one subscription with unlimited members. The pricing has traps - transaction fees, add-ons, API gating - but every one of them is documented and the Business tier's math is straightforward. If your income plan involves people paying monthly to be in a room you run, this is the infrastructure that bill deserves.

Questions we get asked

How much does Circle really take from my membership revenue?

Plan price plus transaction fee plus Stripe. On Professional: $89/mo + 2% of each payment + Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 - so on a $29/month membership, roughly $1.72 in fees per member per month. At Business ($199/mo) the platform fee drops to 1%, which is why serious sellers upgrade quickly. Annual billing at checkout softens the plan price; the fee percentage is the number to watch.

Circle vs Skool vs a Discord server - which should host a paid community?

Circle fits businesses selling courses, events and community as one product, with AI handling member support. Skool is flat $99/mo with no platform fee and wins on simplicity; Discord is free but has no native payments or courses. Circle is the middle path: more structure than Discord, more features than Skool, and a transaction fee both rivals make you think about differently.

Can I run courses and charge separately for them?

Yes - courses are included on every plan and you can price spaces, courses and events independently (one-time, subscription or payment plans). Members can exist free while specific spaces or courses sit behind their own paywall, which is the standard funnel: free community, paid inner circle.

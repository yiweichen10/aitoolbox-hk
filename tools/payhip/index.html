What Payhip Is (From A Creator Who's Sold Through It)

Payhip is a storefront for people who make things once and sell them many times: templates, ebooks, courses, memberships, coaching calls, even physical prints. You upload the file or build the course, set a price, and Payhip handles checkout, delivery, and tax. There is no monthly fee to start, and the feature set is the same on every plan, which is unusual in this space.

The pitch that matters for a solo maker: Payhip is the legal seller for VAT purposes in the EU and UK, so it collects and remits that tax for you. Price in dollars, someone in Germany buys, and you never register for a German tax number. That one detail is why most creators finally go global instead of shipping only to their home country.

How People Actually Make Money On It

Payhip is the checkout and fulfillment layer. The income is what you put behind it. What works:

1. Sell A Digital File You Already Own

This is the fastest start. A Notion template, a Lightroom preset pack, a spreadsheet, a zine, a sewing pattern. Upload once, set a price, and Payhip delivers the file the moment payment clears. On the free plan you pay 5% only when it sells, so a $12 template that moves ten copies a month costs you $6 total. Most sellers underestimate how much dead material on their drive is already a product.

2. Package Knowledge Into A Course Or Membership

Payhip's course builder takes video, audio, text, and PDF lessons, with quizzes and completion certificates. Sell it once, or gate it behind a monthly membership for recurring income. A membership at $9/mo with fifty members is $450/mo before fees, and Payhip runs the dunning and access for you. This is the steadier play than one-off files because the revenue repeats.

3. Turn Buyers Into Affiliates

The affiliate program is on every plan, including free. You set a commission, say 20%, and Payhip tracks who sent the buyer and pays the affiliate out. Creators who switch this on usually see their best months come from affiliates, not ads, because real fans out-convert any cold traffic you buy.

4. Coach Or Consult Through Bundles

Coaching packages are a product type here. Sell a three-session bundle, and Payhip handles the purchase and access while you run the calls on your own calendar. Good for creators moving from free advice to paid without standing up a full booking stack.

Where It Beats The Alternatives

The free plan is the differentiator. Unlimited products, unlimited revenue, every feature, $0 upfront. Gumroad's free tier takes 10%, so once you sell with any consistency Payhip is the cheaper checkout. Memberships, courses, and affiliates on the free tier mean you can run a near-complete creator business before paying a cent of platform fee.

Against the merchant-of-record tools already in this directory (Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Paddle), Payhip is lighter and more storefront-first. Those three act as the legal seller and file tax in 100+ markets with usage billing and software license keys; Payhip is the simpler pick if you sell downloads and courses and want the lowest fee path rather than a developer billing API.

The Honest Downsides

The 5% free-tier fee is not trivial at volume. At $1,000/mo you hand Payhip $50 plus processor fees; on a $5 item you lose about 8 cents on the dollar before Stripe takes its share. Many sellers stay on free past the point where Plus would save them because the percentage feels small. Do the math at $967/mo and $3,500/mo and move plans on purpose.

You bring all the traffic. Payhip is a checkout, not a marketplace, so there is no discovery engine and no built-in buyer audience. If you don't have a list, a channel, or an ad budget, the store sits empty. This is the part new sellers get wrong most often.

Customization is shallow next to a real store builder. Themes, colors, and a custom domain are there, but you won't get deep layout control, advanced analytics, or complex checkout logic without other tools. Payouts and chargebacks run on Stripe or PayPal's clock, outside Payhip's control, and US buyers know Gumroad's name more than Payhip's, which can add a sliver of checkout friction.

Pricing

Payhip has three plans and every one includes the full feature set:

  • Free — $0/mo, 5% Payhip fee per sale. Best when you're testing or selling under roughly $967/mo.
  • Plus — $29/mo, 2% Payhip fee. Pays off past about $967/mo in sales.
  • Pro — $99/mo, 0% Payhip fee. Pays off past about $3,500/mo in sales.

Stripe or PayPal processing (about 2.9% + $0.30 per US card) applies on top of Payhip's cut on all tiers. No listing fees, no product cap, no revenue ceiling. The upgrade decision is pure math: stay free while the 5% costs less than the subscription, then step up. Full detail is in the pricing box above.

Who Should Use It

If you sell downloads, courses, or memberships and want the cheapest place to start with tax handled for you, Payhip is the most frictionless storefront you can open today. It suits solo creators and small teams who bring their own audience and want a 0%-fee path as they grow. It is the wrong tool if you need a marketplace's traffic, deep store customization, or a developer billing API for token-metered software. Treat it as your checkout layer, withdraw on schedule, and keep Gumroad or a processor as a backup so one platform's policy change can't freeze your income.

❓ FAQ

How do I actually make money with Payhip?

You upload a product, set a price, and Payhip runs checkout, delivery, and tax. The income streams that work best: sell templates, presets, or ebooks you already make; package knowledge into a paid course or membership; offer coaching bundles; or turn fans into affiliates who earn a cut for sending buyers. Because the free plan has no monthly cost and unlimited products, you can list ten things today and only pay when one sells. Pair it with an audience you already have (newsletter, YouTube, X) since Payhip brings no traffic of its own.

What does Payhip cost, really?

Free is $0/mo with a 5% Payhip fee per sale. Plus is $29/mo at 2%. Pro is $99/mo at 0% Payhip fee. Stripe or PayPal still takes about 2.9% + $0.30 per US card on top of whatever Payhip keeps. The break-even is roughly $967/mo (Free vs Plus) and $3,500/mo (Plus vs Pro) on Payhip's own fees alone. Below those numbers the cheaper plan usually wins; above them, Pro leaves you the most. There are no listing fees or product limits on any tier.

Payhip or Gumroad for a solo creator?

Both are no-code storefronts for digital goods. Payhip's free tier gives every feature at a 5% cut; Gumroad's free tier takes 10%, so Payhip is cheaper once you sell steadily. Gumroad has stronger US brand recognition, which can mean slightly smoother buyer trust. Payhip adds memberships, coaching, courses, and affiliates on the free plan, and a true 0% option on Pro. Pick Payhip if you want the lowest fee and built-in memberships; pick Gumroad if name familiarity with US buyers matters more than a few points of fee.

Does Payhip handle VAT, and is it safe in 2026?

Yes. Payhip auto-collects and remits EU and UK digital VAT, which is the main reason solo sellers pick it over a raw Stripe link. It is a live, established platform (London-based, founded 2011, with iOS and Android apps launched in 2024) processing real payments, so it is safe to sell through. The caveats: you still pay processor fees and you own the tax liability only for markets Payhip covers, so confirm any non-EU/UK region you target. As with any checkout layer, don't park large balances; withdraw on your processor's schedule and keep a second provider as backup so one policy change can't freeze your income.

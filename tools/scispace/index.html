What SciSpace Actually Is

SciSpace (formerly Typeset) is an AI research agent built on a corpus of 280M+ papers. The core loop: ask a question and get an answer with inline citations you can click; upload PDFs and chat with them; run a literature review and extract findings into comparison tables; run a systematic review (SLR) workflow; then draft text with citations inserted from the corpus. A browser extension (Copilot) explains papers as you read them anywhere on the web. It is the tool in this category that tries to cover reading, extraction and writing in one workspace - where Elicit stays focused on paper discovery and screening and Consensus stays focused on evidence-based yes/no questions.

Who Actually Makes Money With It

Be honest with yourself here: SciSpace is a work-acceleration tool, not an income source. Four groups do make money with it. Consultants and agencies produce evidence-backed reports, competitive-intelligence dossiers and literature-grounded white papers - the SLR agent and comparison-table extraction compress a two-week review into days, and clients pay for the verified output, not the speed. Content creators in technical niches (health, AI, finance) use it to keep claims citable so their articles survive editor scrutiny and rank. Graduate students and researchers run paid tutoring, thesis-support and research-assistant work faster. R&D teams use Deep Review for internal science and market scans.

The unifying rule: the money comes from selling the finished, verified work - never from reselling the AI's raw output. Every claim still needs checking against the actual paper, because a single fabricated citation can end a client relationship.

The Credit Reality

The credit pool is the pricing mechanism that matters. Light PDF-chat use barely dents the free 100 credits; agent workflows - Deep Review, SLR, multi-paper extraction - consume them quickly, and a heavy month does not borrow from next month. Practical guidance: measure one real task end-to-end on the free tier, multiply by your monthly workload, then pick the tier. Most individual researchers land on Premium ($144/yr); most teams on Advanced ($840/yr).

The Honest Cons

Output quality is strong but not trustworthy by default: AI research assistants misread methods sections and overstate findings, and SciSpace is no exception - verification against source papers is part of the job, not an optional extra. The dual Agent/Editor pricing surfaces confuse buyers, and the advanced tiers are priced for teams rather than individuals. Credits expire monthly with no banking, which punishes bursty workloads. And the free tier's 100 credits on the Lite model only is an evaluation allowance, not a working one.

Bottom Line

If your work - paid or academic - involves reading more papers than hours allow, SciSpace is this category's best all-in-one: the citation trail is real, the extraction tables save the most time, and Premium at $12/mo annual is cheap relative to the hours it returns. Buy the tier that matches measured usage, verify everything you publish, and treat the writing features as drafting support rather than a ghostwriter. When you just need quick evidence checks instead of deep review work, Consensus or Perplexity are lighter companions.

Questions we get asked

Is SciSpace free?

There is a free tier: 100 credits per month on the Lite model, with literature review search and basic PDF chat. It is a real but small allowance - enough to test one end-to-end task, not to run a review project. Premium at $12/mo billed annually (or $20 monthly) raises the pool to 1,200 credits and adds Deep Research and SLR workflows.

SciSpace or Elicit - which one?

Elicit is the focused pick for paper discovery and screening, with a Pro tier at $49/user/mo aimed at systematic review teams. SciSpace covers more of the workflow in one place - PDF chat, extraction tables, drafting with citations - at a lower entry price. Many researchers run both: Elicit for finding and screening, SciSpace for reading and writing. The full comparison is in our literature review tools guide.

Can SciSpace write my paper?

It drafts text with citations inserted from the corpus, which is drafting support rather than authorship. Every claim needs verification against the source paper - AI research tools misread methods sections and overstate findings often enough that unverified output is a real reputational risk. Journals also require AI-use disclosure under their policies. Use it to compress reading and structuring, not to bypass the scholarship.

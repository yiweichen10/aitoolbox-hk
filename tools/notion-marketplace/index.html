What Selling Notion Templates Actually Is

Notion passed 100 million users in September 2024, and every one of them eventually hits the same wall: the app is blank when they open it. They want a project tracker, a budget, a content calendar - built, linked, and working. Most will never build it themselves. The Marketplace is Notion's official store for the people who do build it, and since the checkout runs inside Notion itself, a listing there reads as "verified" in a way a Gumroad link never will.

The mechanics are simple. You build a workspace once, submit it for review, set a price, and buyers duplicate it into their own account with one click. No files, no shipping, no license keys. Notion is the merchant of record: it processes payment, collects regional sales tax and VAT, and sends you money through Stripe. The fee is 8% plus 40 cents per sale. On a $25 template that is $22.60 in your pocket - friendlier than Gumroad's 10% flat, though the flat 40 cents starts to hurt on cheap listings (a $5 template nets $4.20).

Two things everyone underestimates. First, getting in is a process: you apply, Notion's team reviews your work, and the official help page says approval "may take a few months" and can be rejected at their discretion. Second, money is slow by design - every sale is held for 14 days before it becomes payable, payouts go out biweekly on Thursdays with a $20 minimum, so cash from an early-month sale lands weeks later. Plan around it instead of discovering it.

The Money Angle

The Marketplace is not the richest payout on this site; it is the cheapest trust you can buy. Five paths, roughly in the order most sellers actually use them:

  • Official storefront as your flagship. The listing inside Notion converts better than any external link because the buyer never leaves the app. Sellers keep it as the premium, full-price version of their product.
  • The dual-track trick. Notion explicitly supports selling elsewhere: duplicate your template, set up a separate Notion Site link, and sell that copy on Gumroad or Payhip at a lower fee - or bundle it with a bonus the Marketplace copy lacks. The Marketplace version brings the traffic and the badge; the off-platform version keeps more margin on each sale.
  • The free-to-paid funnel. Easlo's catalog runs on this: dozens of free trackers feed buyers toward the $39-79 paid systems. His two cheapest products, a $20 bullet journal and a $40 finance tracker, made up close to half his revenue - not the $130 flagship everyone talks about.
  • Bundles. Thomas Frank's data is blunt: his two-template bundle outsold either individual template 3-to-1 at a higher average order value. Once you have two or three related templates, a bundle is the highest-margin page in your store.
  • Custom Agents. In March 2026 Notion launched Custom Agents, teams built over a million of them in two months, and the Marketplace's own payment webhooks now list agents as a product type alongside templates. Selling packaged automation is a shelf that did not exist a year ago; early listings face almost none of the template saturation.

If you want a number to anchor expectations, here are the honest ones. Thomas Frank reported about $1M in template sales during 2022 and later an average around $120K a month at his peak - and his own disclosure makes clear that almost none of it came from his multi-million-subscriber YouTube channel. Easlo's business averaged about $20K a month in his Indie Hackers interview. PathPages grew to five figures monthly with 120,000+ customers. Now the part the gurus skip: sellers tracking this niche put a realistic first-quarter outcome for a new seller with one decent template and no audience at $300-1,200 total, and the common six-to-twelve-month landing zone at $0-3,000 a month. The five-figure tier is real, but it sits on years of catalog-building and audience work.

The AI Reality

This section matters more here than on any other marketplace page on this site, for an uncomfortable reason: Notion itself is your new competitor. The app now ships built-in AI agents, AI meeting notes, and a Q&A agent that can sketch a working project tracker or habit database from one sentence - free, for every one of those 100M users. A bare-bones template of empty linked databases was a sellable product in 2023. In 2026 it is a 30-second AI prompt.

What still sells is what the AI cannot generate in one shot: a system refined through hundreds of real users, opinionated workflows with formulas that actually survive contact with a real job, documentation and video walkthroughs, and niche depth (a CRMs-for-freelance-photographers beats another "life OS" every time). The four categories that carry roughly two-thirds of paid template sales - productivity systems, finance trackers, content calendars, lightweight CRMs - are exactly where buyers pay for tested structure, not blank pages.

One policy note, stated plainly: unlike Adobe Stock (mandatory AI disclosure checkbox) or Canva (AI submissions banned outright), Notion's Marketplace terms contain no AI-specific disclosure or prohibition that we could find as of this review. That is not an endorsement of AI-dumped listings - human reviewers who can take months to answer your application have every incentive to reject low-effort submissions, and the terms do require you to hold the rights to what you publish. Use AI to move faster on structure and copy, and put your own hours into the parts buyers can feel. A stronger play: build and sell Custom Agents, which are AI products by definition and sell precisely because someone already did the configuration work.

Read the piracy row carefully - it is the hidden reason the fee is worth paying. Access locking only protects the copy sold through Notion. Sell the same template off-platform and anyone who buys it can share the duplicate link with the world, which is exactly why the standard playbook is: Marketplace copy at full price with access locking on, off-platform copy bundled with extras that make sharing pointless.

The terms also contain a clause template sellers should read once and then try to forget: Notion reserves the right to build products that compete with your work, with no compensation beyond paying you what it owes. Notion shipping its own templates or agents is a live business risk, not a hypothetical - the built-in AI already nibbled at the entry-level tier.

Bottom Line

Notion Marketplace is the storefront position in the template business: not the cheapest fee, but the only one where 100 million people can buy your work without leaving the app they already pay for. Three things to know before you apply. One, the gate is slow and judgmental - apply with your best, most specific template, not a portfolio of five mediocre ones, and start selling on Gumroad the same day so cash flows while you wait. Two, the money cycle (14-day hold, biweekly payout, $20 floor) means your first withdrawal lands about six weeks after your first sale; nobody warns you. Three, the ground is shifting under simple templates because Notion's own AI builds simple templates now - sell systems, sell support, sell agents, and treat the Marketplace fee as rent on trust you cannot buy anywhere else.

❓ FAQ

How much do I actually keep from each sale?

Notion takes 8% plus a flat $0.40. A $25 template pays you $22.60 before tax; a $10 template pays $8.80; a $5 template pays $4.20 - the flat fee is why $5 listings are a bad idea here. Outside the US, another 1% comes off at payout for currency conversion. Notion handles the buyer's sales tax or VAT, and your own income tax stays your problem.

How long does approval and first payout actually take?

Plan on months, not days. You apply through Settings, Notion's team reviews your submission (the help page says it may take a few months and rejections are discretionary), then you onboard with Stripe, then each sale is held 14 days before it becomes payable, and payouts run biweekly on Thursdays with a $20 minimum. From application to cash in hand, a realistic first cycle is three to five months - which is why experienced sellers list on Gumroad in parallel while they wait.

Can I still sell my template on Gumroad if it's on the Marketplace?

Yes, and Notion documents the workflow: duplicate the template, set up a separate Notion Site link for the off-platform copy, and sell that at Gumroad or Payhip. One warning from the help docs: once you onboard with Stripe, your existing paid template links start redirecting buyers to Marketplace checkout, so set up the duplicate before approval hits. The off-platform copy does not get access locking, so bundle it with extras - video walkthrough, bonus templates, a community invite - so sharing the link costs the buyer something real.

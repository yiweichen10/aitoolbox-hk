What Flippa Actually Is

Flippa has been around since 2009 - it spun out of SitePoint in Melbourne and is now the largest open marketplace anywhere for buying and selling online businesses. The inventory covers websites, ecommerce stores, SaaS products, mobile apps, newsletters, YouTube channels, domains, social accounts and - relevant to this site - AI tools as an official asset category. Flippa's own pages claim 600,000+ registered buyers, roughly 17,000 new buyers joining each month, and 10,000+ live listings.

The honest framing: it's eBay, not Sotheby's. Empire Flippers rejects about 91% of applicants; Flippa will list almost anything you can prove you own, from a $500 domain to an eight-figure brokered deal. That openness is exactly why you can exit a $3,000 starter site without meeting any minimum revenue threshold - and also why your inbox will fill with messages from people who clearly never read the listing.

Trust tooling got real after the ugly years (shill bidding and doctored earnings scandals in the 2010s). Today there's mandatory identity verification, verified revenue integrations that pull data straight from Stripe, Shopify, QuickBooks and Google Analytics, automatic due diligence on listings above $50K, and escrow by default. Those tools exist for a reason: below $50K, most listings still run on self-reported numbers, and buyers know it.

The Money Angle: Selling the Whole Asset

Every other tool in this category pays you in installments. Flippa pays you all at once, and then you're done.

Four lanes, in the order most people actually use them:

1. Exit a content site. A niche site clearing $800/month from display ads and affiliate commissions typically lists in the 30-40x monthly profit range - call it $24,000-$32,000 for our example. That's the logical end point of the build-monetize loop this site keeps describing: Google AdSense or Amazon Associates pay monthly; Flippa is where you cash out the machine itself instead of grinding another year.

2. Sell a micro-SaaS or AI wrapper app. "AI tools" is an official asset category on Flippa, and vibe-coded products sell there regularly. Buyers in this lane price working products as assets - a clean transfer, real users, a problem someone can name - not on ARR multiples.

3. Flip. Buy underpriced assets, improve them (AI makes the improvement phase faster than ever), resell. Flippa's own case studies include a buyer who took Tattooing101.com from a $10,000 purchase to a $100,000 sale. Harder than it sounds, but it's a real business model, not a story.

4. Sell the small stuff. Newsletters, domains, social accounts. Lower ceiling, fastest closings, and the $29 entry listing was made for exactly this tier.

The exit math is the part nobody runs before listing. Run it first, because fees land whether or not a buyer shows up.

The Honest Cons

  • Listing fees are non-refundable. Sixty days with no serious buyer means you paid for advertising, not an exit. Price realistically the first time - an overpriced listing that sits is the most expensive mistake here.
  • Buyer quality below $50K is rough. Tire-kickers, due-diligence tourists, lowball offers, and people who ask for revenue screenshots and vanish. Verified Stripe/Shopify integrations are your best filter - listings with verified financials convert conversations into offers at a completely different rate.
  • The BBB gives Flippa an F rating (unanswered complaints), while Trustpilot sits at 4.2/5 across 3,000+ reviews. The one-star cluster is support responsiveness and listing-fee refund disputes. Neither kills the platform, but don't expect white-glove service at the $29 tier.
  • The success fee is 10% flat up to $499.9K. Older guides still quote 7.5% for the $50-100K band - that's a stale tier structure. If your deal is six figures, confirm the current rate on the live pricing page before you budget.
  • Premium buyers ($388/yr) get early access to listings, and sellers report standard listings losing their crucial first-week exposure to paying members. If a listing goes quiet in week one, that's part of why.
  • History shapes buyer behavior. The shill-bidding and fake-earnings episodes from the 2010s are a decade old, but they're why buyers verify everything twice. Bring proof before they ask.

Flippa is the exit ramp for everything else on this site. The AI-assisted content site, the micro-SaaS, the newsletter - at some point the smartest financial move is selling the machine rather than milking it another year, and for sub-$100K digital assets Flippa is where those deals actually happen. ❓ FAQ

How much do I actually take home selling a small site?

On a $1,800 sale: $29 entry listing plus a 10% success fee ($180) = $209 in Flippa costs, about 11.6% of the deal. You keep roughly $1,591, and per Flippa's help center the buyer pays the FlippaPay or Escrow.com fee at close. The percentage matters more than the flat fee: a $9,000 site in the same band pays $29 + $900, still about 10.3%. Remember the listing fee is non-refundable - if no buyer closes, the $29 is gone.

Can I sell an AI-built website or AI tool on Flippa?

Yes - AI tools are an official asset category and vibe-coded apps sell there regularly. What buyers pay for is proof: verified revenue integrations (Stripe, Shopify, QuickBooks, Google Analytics) and traffic data that survives due diligence. An AI-generated site whose rankings collapsed at the last search update sells for near zero; one with stable verified revenue sells like any other asset. Build it with AI, but document the revenue like a business - that is what you are actually selling.

Flippa or Empire Flippers - which should I use?

Deal size decides. Empire Flippers requires roughly $2,000/month profit and 12 months of history, rejects about 91% of applicants, and charges 15% on the first $700K with no upfront fee - but it delivers a vetted buyer pool and runs the process for you. Flippa takes any asset at any size, costs $29-$1,499 upfront plus 10% (sliding to 5% on very large deals), and you handle buyer screening yourself. Under $100K it is Flippa by default; above that, compare total platform cost against the higher price a curated pool might produce.

What Base44 Actually Is

Base44 is a vibe-coding platform that treats "app" as the deliverable, not a codebase. You describe what you want in a sentence; it returns a deployed, full-stack application - UI, database, authentication, hosting - that you refine through conversation. Founder Maor Shlomo bootstrapped it into one of the fastest-growing AI products of 2025, and Wix bought it mid-year for a reported ~$80M, which turned Base44 into both a standalone builder and the custom-app engine inside the Wix App Market.

The Wix integration is the differentiator that matters now. Open Base44 from a Wix dashboard and it detects what the site actually runs - Store, Bookings, Events, Restaurants, Blog, CRM - then proposes apps and agents built on that live data: a flash-sale campaign page that sells through Wix checkout, a profit-per-product dashboard, an agent that recovers abandoned checkouts or chases no-shows. Competitors generate generic apps; Base44 generates apps wired to a business that already exists.

Under the hood it runs a dual-credit economy: message credits buy your build-time conversation (25/month free up to 1,200/month on Elite), and integration credits buy your app's runtime - the AI calls, uploads, and database queries your end users generate (100/month free up to 50,000/month). Understanding that split is the difference between a $16 hobby and a $40 client-delivery business.

The Money Angle

  • Client micro-apps for local businesses. The core freelance play: a clinic's appointment desk, a restaurant's lunch-special page, a salon's booking flow - one sentence to working software. Sell at $500-3,000 per build plus monthly care at $50-150, while your infrastructure cost sits on a $40 Builder plan. The approval-based workflow (build, demo, iterate) compresses what used to be a six-week agency job into days.
  • Wix-ecosystem services. Millions of Wix sites run stores, bookings, and CRMs that the default dashboard doesn't serve well. Selling "custom apps and AI agents for your Wix business" - recovery agents, restock planners, staff schedulers on live data - is a new service category with a distribution channel no other vibe-coding tool has.
  • MVP validation for micro-SaaS. Build the smallest working version on the free plan, put it in front of real users, and charge for it before writing production code. If it validates, either scale on higher tiers or rebuild on Replit/Lovable with an actual engineering budget - the $16 experiment cost is nothing.
  • Internal tools for your own operation. The use case hiding in plain sight: profit dashboards, client-history views, restock forecasts built on your real data. Every hour saved is margin, and nobody bills you for it.
  • Sell the build, own the support. Vibe-coded apps still need prompt maintenance as usage grows - and that maintenance is the retainer. The lock-in critics complain about is, from the service-provider side, the revenue guarantee.

The Honest Cons

  • The free tier is a demo, not a workspace. Twenty-five messages end mid-project; plan on Starter the moment a build gets real.
  • Lock-in is structural. Apps live on Base44's infrastructure; GitHub export (Builder up) gives you source, not self-hosting. Migrations are rebuilds.
  • Usage-based costs scale quietly. Integration credits rise with your app's success while your plan price stays flat - monitor the meter as part of operating the app.
  • The complexity ceiling is real. Prompt-built apps tangle at multi-role permissions and long feature lists; serious products eventually need engineering.
  • Wix direction risk. The acquisition funds the roadmap - and could bend it toward Wix-site features over standalone builders.

Bottom Line

Base44 is the cheapest honest path from "this business needs a small app" to a URL that works - one sentence to full-stack, with the Wix acquisition adding a distribution channel its rivals cannot copy. The credit system is legible once you internalize the split: message credits are your building budget, integration credits are your operating budget, and client pricing should mirror both. Start free, prove the idea in one sitting's worth of messages, then price the client work against the tiers.

Questions we get asked

What is the difference between message credits and integration credits?

Message credits pay for building - each conversational turn with the AI costs one, and plans range from 25/month (Free) to 1,200/month (Elite). Integration credits pay for running - every AI call, file upload, API request, or database query your app's end users generate, from 100/month (Free) to 50,000/month (Elite). Practical rule from the official guide: budget by message credits while you build, by integration credits once real users arrive. A client app with daily usage will burn integration credits far faster than you burn messages.

Can I export my app's code and move it off Base44?

Partially. In-app code editing arrives at Starter ($16/mo), and GitHub integration at Builder ($40/mo), which gives you versioning and source visibility. But the app still runs on Base44's hosting with its managed database and auth - lifting a live app onto your own stack is a migration project, not an export button. Treat Base44 apps like premium no-code products: build fast on-platform, and if a client needs portable infrastructure, budget a rebuild or start elsewhere.

Is the free plan actually usable?

Yes, for learning and prototypes - and no, for finished builds. The official guide itself warns that 25 monthly message credits will likely run out mid-app if you try to build something complete in one sitting. The realistic free-plan workflow: design your prompt carefully, build the smallest working version, test the idea, then move to Starter ($16/mo, 100 messages) for real iteration. Compared with Replit or Lovable free tiers, Base44's allocation is smaller but the result is a hosted, login-ready app rather than a code sandbox.

Serper is a Google Search API that returns structured JSON - organic results, answer boxes, People Also Ask, knowledge panels - in one to two seconds, at prices that undercut every SERP API we track. Where Tavily and Exa sell AI-optimized search for LLM grounding, Serper sells raw Google data, which is why it has become the default backend for AI SEO tools, rank trackers and agent workflows. If you can code a loop, you can resell what it returns.

What the plans actually cost

The official homepage (read 2026-11-28) leads with 2,500 free queries for new accounts and no monthly plans - Serper sells prepaid credit packs. Four independent pricing trackers checked within the last two weeks agree on the grid: Starter $50 for 50,000 queries ($1.00 per 1,000, 50 queries/second), Standard $375 for 500,000 ($0.75, 100 QPS), Scale $1,250 for 2.5M ($0.50, 200 QPS) and Ultimate $3,750 for 12.5M ($0.30, 300 QPS). Credits expire six months after purchase, refunds follow a 7-day window with under 20% of credits used, and prices exclude tax. One caveat from this run: the site's /pricing path returns a 404 and the pack grid lives behind the dashboard, so treat the dashboard as the final word.

Where the money angle sits

The margin math is the pitch. A daily rank tracker for 1,000 keywords burns about 30,000 queries a month - roughly $30 in Starter credits - and agencies bill that dashboard at $100-300/mo. AI SEO consultants run GEO audits: thousands of AI-answer checks per client for pennies of API cost. Product people wrap the autocomplete endpoint into keyword research tools and charge subscriptions on top. If you already resell OpenRouter tokens into chat products, Serper slots in the same way: raw inputs at cost, services on top. Our search API pricing comparison maps where it beats Tavily, Exa and Brave - and where it does not.

The honest drawbacks

The six-month expiry is the real cost of the cheap rate: a $50 pack that sits unused ages out, so sporadic projects effectively pay more than the sticker price. There is no cache and no monthly plan - re-checking the same 5,000 keywords re-bills every query, forever. Google-only coverage means Bing, DuckDuckGo or Yandex projects need a second vendor. And for AI grounding work where you want LLM-ready chunks and citations, Perplexity or Tavily remain better fits - Serper returns Google's page, not an answer.

Bottom line

Serper is the cheapest way to put live Google data behind a product or a client service, and the 2,500 free queries settle the question in an afternoon. Buy the smallest pack that fits six months of usage and re-evaluate at renewal. The current packs sit on the Serper site - note the full grid is behind the dashboard.

Questions we get asked

Is Serper free?

New accounts get 2,500 queries free with no card - that is a one-time grant, not a monthly allowance. After that you buy prepaid packs starting at $50 for 50,000 queries, and unused credits expire six months after purchase.

How does Serper compare to SerpApi?

On raw price Serper wins by a wide margin - $1.00 per 1,000 queries on the entry pack against SerpApi's $25 per 1,000 on its starter plan. SerpApi wins on engine coverage, locations, cached results and its Legal Shield. For Google-only JSON at volume, Serper is the value pick; for multi-engine compliance-sensitive work, SerpApi still earns its price.

What can I build with Serper that makes money?

Three proven shapes: a daily rank tracker (1,000 keywords costs about $30/month in credits and bills at $100-300), GEO audits that check how AI engines describe a client, and keyword research tools built on the autocomplete endpoint. Agencies resell all three as white-label services on top of pennies of API cost.

CURRENT PRICING
Pay-as-you-go from $0.35/M tokens
Verified on cerebras.ai/pricing, 2026-11-25. Self-serve is pay-as-you-go, no subscription: gpt-oss-120b ~3,000 tok/s claimed at $0.35/M input and $0.75/M output; Qwen 3.8 27B ~1,850 tok/s at $0.99/M input and $1.49/M output. One-time $5 promotional credit after adding a card, expires 30 days after activation; API and Playground access pause when credits run out until you buy PayGo credits - no permanent free tier. Enterprise: dedicated capacity, custom model weights and fine-tuning, priced by agreement. Cached tokens bill at the standard input rate (no discount) but do not count against uncached rate-limit buckets. Third-party checks: Artificial Analysis measured 1,347 tok/s on gpt-oss-120b vs 338 on Groq; blended market price for Cerebras gpt-oss-120b was $0.23/M in October 2026. Cerebras Code subscriptions ($50 and $200/mo) have been listed as sold out at times.

Cerebras builds wafer-scale chips - each one a whole silicon wafer instead of a reticle-sized GPU die - and rents them out through an OpenAI-compatible inference API. The pitch is narrow and honest: when the model's thinking speed is your product's user experience, nothing measured so far is faster. On gpt-oss-120b the company claims about 3,000 tokens per second; the strictest independent measurement still lands at 1,347 tok/s with 0.65s to first token, roughly four times what Groq serves on the identical checkpoint.

Pricing, straight up

Self-serve billing is pure pay-as-you-go with no subscription. gpt-oss-120b costs $0.35 per million input tokens and $0.75 per million output at a claimed ~3,000 tok/s. Qwen 3.8 27B runs $0.99/$1.49 at ~1,850 tok/s and accepts up to 10 images per request. Every new account gets a one-time $5 credit after adding a card; it expires 30 days after activation, and API access pauses when credits run dry until you buy PayGo credits. There is no permanent free tier. Enterprise capacity, custom weights and fine-tuning are quote-only. One detail that matters for cost planning: cached input tokens bill at full list price - Groq discounts them 50% - but they skip your uncached rate-limit bucket, so a tool-heavy agent with big repeated system prompts gets throughput headroom rather than savings.

Where the money angle actually lives

The buyers making money on Cerebras are people whose product breaks when tokens arrive slowly. A freelance AI developer building customer-facing agent tools can price the project on outcomes and eat the inference premium, because a response loop that takes 40 seconds on a GPU host takes 10 here - and users stay. Agencies shipping voice or chat copilots use the speed headroom to run tighter retry-and-verify loops, which cuts their cost per accepted answer even at a higher per-token rate. Coding-tool builders treat it as infrastructure for the /tools/openai-codex/ and /tools/cursor/ generation of products where agent latency is the churn driver. If your workload is offline summarization or data labeling, none of this applies - batch discounts on GPU hosts beat wafer-scale speed every time.

The honest trade-offs

The self-serve catalog is two models deep. No Kimi K3, no DeepSeek, no GLM 5.3 - the checkpoints that dominate open-weight chatter in late 2026 live on Together AI and Fireworks AI instead. Input pricing sits around twice Groq's on the shared tier, so the speed premium is real, not marketing fog. Speed claims deserve the same skepticism you apply everywhere: 3,000 tok/s is a docs figure, 1,347 is what a stopwatch sees including the wait before the first token. Questions we get asked

Is Cerebras actually faster than Groq?

On the same model, yes, by a wide margin. Artificial Analysis timed gpt-oss-120b at 1,347 tok/s with 0.65s to first token on Cerebras against 338 tok/s on Groq. Groq's counters are price: half-price cached input and a larger model menu. If your workload is batch generation, the speed premium rarely pays for itself; if it is a chatty agent loop, it usually does.

Is there a free tier?

No permanent one. You get a one-time $5 credit after adding a payment method, it expires 30 days after activation, and API access stops once credits are exhausted until you buy PayGo credits. For a true free lane, run the same open models on Together AI (Ternary Bonsai free) or via OpenRouter free models.

Which models can I actually use?

Self-serve: gpt-oss-120b and Qwen 3.8 27B, both Apache-licensed. The dedicated enterprise catalog spans GLM, Kimi, DeepSeek and Llama 4 variants by agreement. Through partners you can reach Cerebras-powered models from Hugging Face, AWS Marketplace or Vercel without a direct account.

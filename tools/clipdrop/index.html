What Clipdrop Actually Is (From Someone Who Sells Product Photos With It)

Clipdrop is an AI image-editing toolkit. You drag in a photo and it removes the background, upscales it up to 16x, paints out unwanted objects, relights the scene, or stretches the frame to a new aspect ratio - each as a separate one-click tool in one browser dashboard. It started at Stability AI, the Stable Diffusion company, and Jasper bought it in 2024, so it now sits inside Jasper's marketing stack. I do not care about the corporate history; I care that it replaces five single-purpose subscriptions I used to keep for product photography.

The tool that pays my bills is Relight. A seller sends me a product shot taken under bad kitchen light. I drop it in Clipdrop, move a virtual light source, and suddenly it looks like a studio grab - no reshoot, no softbox. Background removal, cleanup, and uncrop cover the rest of a marketplace listing. That is the whole service, and Clipdrop does the repetitive 80% while I make the calls.

How I Make Money With Clipdrop (The Part You Came For)

  • Done-for-you product photos. Sellers on Amazon, Shopify, and Etsy rarely shoot clean listings. I take their raw photos, return white-background, relit, upscaled images in every ratio the platform wants, and bill $50-$200 per SKU set or $300-$1,500/month per store with a rotating catalog. Ten stores at $500 is $5,000/month against a $9 Clipdrop bill.
  • Real-estate and listing cleanup. Agents send cluttered room photos; I remove the mess and fix the light for $25-$75 a photo or $200-$600/month per agent. Relight does the heavy lifting.
  • Batch catalog prep through the API. A store with 2,000 legacy photos pipes them through background removal and upscaling via the REST API; I charge $0.10-$0.30 per image or $150-$800 per project and never open the UI. This is where the Jasper API matters - the web app caps at 10 files per upload.
  • Print-on-demand upscaling. Low-res art becomes 16x print-ready files; I charge $15-$60 per design and list on Redbubble or Etsy. This pairs well with Magnific AI for creative upscales.
  • White-label inside a bigger package. Clipdrop is the photo step inside larger content retainers I sell to small brands, next to copy and Canva layouts.

The margin is the point: the software is $9, the rate is for taste. Ship raw output and you are a $9 tool; edit with judgment and you are a $500-a-month service.

Pricing (Verified August 2026)

  • Free $0 - 20 background removals, 20 upscales (2x), 20 cleanups, 20 relights, 50 text removals per day; watermark on some exports; queued at peak hours.
  • Pro $9/month - 1,000 images per 24 hours per tool, high-resolution output, queue skip, no watermark, commercial rights. (A few listings show $15/month; check clipdrop.co/pricing - the number moves.)
  • API from ~$49/month - usage-based through Jasper, for batch catalog and app integration.
  • Enterprise - custom volume terms via Jasper sales; needed above the 1,000/24h per-tool Pro wall.

Cons - The Honest Caveats

Who Should Use It

  • E-commerce sellers cleaning up listings without a photographer.
  • Freelancers and agencies selling done-for-you product or real-estate photos.
  • Developers piping image fixes into an app via the API.

Who Should Skip It

  • High-volume catalogs past 1,000 images/day per tool on Pro - you need enterprise terms.
  • Compositors who live in layers and masks - Clipdrop gives results, not control.
  • Anyone who needs pixel-perfect, hand-retouched output - that is still Photoshop's job.

Getting Started (If You Want To Do This)

  • Open the free tier, run 20 background removals on a client's worst photos to learn the edge quality.
  • Move to Pro ($9/mo) before taking paid work - the watermark and queue alone justify it.
  • Build a fixed ratio set (1:1, 9:16, banner) per product so uncrop becomes a one-click step.
  • Use Relight as your signature upsell; sellers pay for "studio look, no reshoot."
  • For catalogs over a few hundred images, script the API early instead of fighting the 10-file web upload.
  • Keep Photoshop handy for the 1 in 20 shot that needs a mask Clipdrop cannot give you.

❓ FAQ

Can a freelancer actually make money with Clipdrop?

Yes, and the path is direct. You pay $9/month for Pro and sell a product-photo cleanup package to e-commerce sellers: remove background, relight, upscale, and uncrop each shot for every marketplace ratio (1:1, 9:16, banner). Charge $50-$200 per SKU photo set or $300-$1,500 a month per store with a rotating catalog. Ten stores at $500 puts you at $5,000 a month against a $9 tool bill - your margin is north of 98%. The rate is for the eye, not the click: you decide which shot is worth relighting and where the cut is rough, and that judgment is what the seller pays for.

Is the free plan enough to start a service?

For learning the tools and your first one or two clients, yes. The free tier gives 20 background removals, 20 upscales, 20 cleanups, and 20 relights a day with no card - enough to deliver a small batch and prove the service. Two walls appear fast: free exports watermark some tools, and the 20/day cap dies the moment a client sends 40 photos. Move to Pro at $9/month before you take paying work; the watermark and queue alone justify it.

How is Clipdrop different from Photoroom, Remove.bg, and Photoshop?

Remove.bg does one thing - cutouts - and edges it slightly better on hair. Photoroom is built for high-volume commerce templates. Photoshop gives you layers and pixel control Clipdrop never will. Clipdrop sits between: it bundles background removal, 16x upscale, cleanup, relight, and uncrop in one login at $9/month, so you do the whole product-photo workflow without a design subscription. Pick Remove.bg if you only cut out, Photoroom if you want commerce templates, Photoshop if you need masks - Clipdrop if you need several fixes per image and want them in one place. See our Photoroom and Topaz Photo AI write-ups for the contrast.

What are the real downsides before I commit?

Three bite in practice. First, the 1,000-images-per-24h-per-tool cap on Pro: a 5,000-SKU catalog refresh needs the API and Jasper enterprise terms, not the $9 plan. Second, no layers - if a cleanup looks wrong you re-run it, you do not fix it locally, so fine composites still land in Photoshop. Third, Jasper owns Clipdrop now and the support reputation is poor; treat it as a utility you can swap, not infrastructure you bet the business on. For day-to-day product photos it is still the best value going.

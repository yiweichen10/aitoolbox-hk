What Ko-fi Actually Is (From Someone Who's Run a Tip Page for Years)

I've used Ko-fi since before it added memberships and a shop - back when it was just a "buy me a coffee" button you dropped on a blog. So this is written from the creator's side, not Ko-fi's sales page. Ko-fi is a monetisation hub for creators: fans send one-off tips ("coffees"), subscribe as monthly members, buy digital or physical items from your shop, or book you for commissions - all from one free page. Money lands straight in your own PayPal or Stripe account; Ko-fi never holds it.

The hook that pulled me in, and the reason it belongs in any "make money" toolkit, is the 0% tip fee. On the free plan you keep 100% of every one-off tip - Ko-fi takes nothing, you only lose the standard Stripe or PayPal processing of about 2.9% plus $0.30. A $5 coffee leaves you about $4.55. That is a lower cut than anywhere else I've tried for pure tipping.

One honest catch: new accounts now start in "Contributor" mode, which adds a 5% fee even to tips. You have to go into Payment Settings and toggle Contributor off to get the true 0% on tips. It is one click, but plenty of creators never notice and lose 5% they did not need to. I'll say it plainly so you don't.

How People Actually Make Money On It

Ko-fi is a low-friction money funnel, not a discovery engine. The income comes from the audience you already have - readers, viewers, listeners - and the small, repeated asks you make of them:

  • Tip jar on your existing channels. Drop the Ko-fi button or link on your blog, YouTube description, podcast show notes, Twitch overlay, or Linktree. Fans who got value send a $3-5 coffee. Because the fee is 0%, this is the cheapest "thank you" money you can collect. A creator with 20k followers and a 0.5% tip rate pulls $300-1,000 a month from coffee buttons alone.
  • Monthly memberships. Set tiers at $3-10/month and reward members with Discord roles, exclusive posts, or early access. Free plan takes 5% on memberships; Ko-fi Gold ($6/month) drops that to 0%. Gold breaks even around $120/month in membership income - above that it is cheaper than the 5% cut. This is the recurring-income play that sits beside Patreon, but without Patreon's 8-12% bite.
  • Digital shop for files you already make. Sell prompts, templates, ebooks, Lightroom presets, notion packs, art prints - anything you can attach as a file. On the free plan shop sales take 5%; on Gold they take 0%. This is the one-off-product play that overlaps Gumroad, but Ko-fi wins if your volume is small and you already have a tipping audience.
  • Commissions with managed slots. If you do custom work - art, editing, consult calls - list a menu with prices and available slots. Clients pay up front, you deliver, money is already in your account. No invoicing back-and-forth.
  • Crowdfunding goals. Set a target (new gear, a project) and let tips fill the bar. Good for one-off community pushes.

The real strength is the funnel: a reader who tips you $5 once, with zero friction, is far easier to upsell to a $5/month membership later than someone who has never paid you at all. Ko-fi gets people into the habit of paying you directly before you ever ask for a subscription.

Where It Falls Short (Read Before You Commit)

Ko-fi's weak spots are scale and support. Discovery is thin - its Explore page sends far less traffic than Patreon's recommendation network, so you bring your own crowd. There is no email tool, no newsletter, no drip sequences; if you want to run a mailing list you pair it with Substack or your own ESP. The shop is deliberately light: fine for a few downloads or prints, useless for dozens of SKUs or real shipping logic. And support is email-only and slow when something breaks - automated moderation has frozen accounts with little warning, which matters if your rent depends on that button.

Getting Started (What I'd Do)

  • Create the page, turn Contributor OFF. Settings > Payment. This restores the 0% tip fee most people think they already have.
  • Add the button everywhere you already have an audience. Blog, social bios, video descriptions, email signature. Tip income comes from places you already own.
  • Open one low-priced membership tier at $3-5/month once a few people have tipped. Reward with a Discord role or a monthly post.
  • List 1-2 digital products you already have (a template, a prompt pack). Keep it light - Ko-fi's shop is not a warehouse.
  • Re-evaluate at ~$120/month in fees. If the 5% on memberships or shop now costs more than $6, switch on Ko-fi Gold and zero out the cut.

Bottom Line

Ko-fi is the lowest-friction way to start collecting money from an audience you already have. The 0% tip fee is real (once you toggle off Contributor), payouts hit your own PayPal or Stripe the same day, and the free plan covers tips, a light shop, and commissions with no monthly cost. The trade is scale: discovery is weak next to Patreon, there is no email tool, and the 5% on memberships and shop only makes sense below about $120/month. For a creator who wants to turn readers and viewers into a few hundred dollars a month without building a store, Ko-fi is the first button I'd add.

❓ FAQ

How much does Ko-fi take, and what do you actually keep per tip or sale?

On the free plan Ko-fi takes 0% of one-off tips - you keep the full amount after standard Stripe or PayPal processing of about 2.9% plus $0.30. A $5 coffee leaves you about $4.55. Shop sales and memberships carry a 5% platform fee on the free plan; Ko-fi Gold ($6/month) drops that to 0% on everything. On a $10 monthly membership the free plan takes $0.50 (5%) plus ~$0.59 processing, so you keep about $8.91; on Gold you keep about $9.41 since only processing applies. One catch: new accounts start in 'Contributor' mode that quietly adds 5% to tips too - toggle it off in Payment Settings to keep the true 0% on tips.

Can you actually make money on Ko-fi, or is it just a tip jar?

It is a real income tool, not just a tip button. Creators earn from four streams: one-off tips, monthly memberships, a digital or physical shop, and paid commissions. A YouTuber or blogger with an audience can pull a few hundred dollars a month from coffee buttons alone, then add a $3-5 membership or a small product to build recurring income. The limit is discovery: Ko-fi will not find you readers the way Patreon's network or Substack's recommendations do, so the money comes from an audience you already have. It suits $100s a month well; it is not built for enterprise-scale membership businesses.

Is Ko-fi Gold worth it, and when should I switch it on?

Ko-fi Gold is $6/month (cheaper billed yearly) and removes every platform fee - 0% on memberships, shop, and commissions - plus custom domain, no Ko-fi branding, and basic analytics. It breaks even around $120/month in membership or shop revenue: below that the free plan's 5% costs less than $6, above it Gold wins. For someone doing $500/month in memberships, Gold saves about $19 a month versus the free plan. If you only take tips and the odd sale, stay on the free plan. If memberships or shop sales pass ~$120/month, switch Gold on and zero out the cut.

vidIQ is a YouTube growth toolkit with an AI layer on top of a decade of keyword data, and its most useful feature has nothing to do with AI: the Outlier Score. It flags videos that pulled 5x or more views than their channel's usual baseline - the closest thing YouTube research has to a proven-demand signal. Find the outlier format in your niche, make a better version, and that is the core loop a lot of full-time creators run every week.

What the plans actually cost

Verified on the official pricing page, 2026-11-26. Free stays genuinely usable: 150 AI credits a month, trend research, content ideas, the AI Coach, and the vidIQ MCP server that pipes your channel data into Claude or ChatGPT. Boost costs $199/year (displayed as $16.58/mo) with 2,000 AI credits monthly, AI thumbnail generation, unlimited trend research and subscriber insights. MAX is $468/year (displayed as $39/mo) with 6,000 credits, deeper Max Mode research and up to 11 hours of short-form clipping per month. Credit costs matter in practice: one AI Coach message runs 10 credits and one thumbnail 22, unused credits do not roll over, and monthly billing - which the checkout shows after the annual options - costs more than the headline figures.

Where the money angle sits

YouTube pays through AdSense, affiliate links and sponsorships, and every one of those starts with views on videos people actually search for. vidIQ's research stack feeds that loop: keyword volumes show what demand exists, Outlier data shows what is breaking out right now, and the AI Coach turns channel analytics into a next-actions list. Faceless channel operators - the audience running faceless YouTube channels - lean on it hardest, because research-driven topic picking substitutes for an on-camera personality. The clipping and thumbnail tools inside Boost and MAX also replace two separate subscriptions that would run $20-50 combined.

A sober note on the AI Coach: in a channel's first months it mostly restates best practices - post consistently, fix packaging, target underserved search terms. Its value grows once there is real channel data to analyze. Buying Boost on a channel with twelve videos is usually premature. Start free, upgrade when research volume actually bottlenecks you, and treat the Coach as an analyst rather than a strategist.

The honest drawbacks

The credit system adds friction: trend research is unlimited, but the AI features drain a meter that resets monthly with no rollover. The displayed prices are annual commitments billed upfront - $199 and $468 land as single charges. Coverage is YouTube-only, so TikTok, Instagram or X creators need other tools. And extension-dependent workflows occasionally break when YouTube redesigns its pages, which happens regularly.

How it compares

TubeBuddy competes on A/B testing and bulk processing; vidIQ's edge is Outlier discovery plus the MCP integration, which no direct competitor ships. Questions we get asked

Is vidIQ's free plan enough to grow a channel?

For the research phase, mostly yes: the free tier includes 150 AI credits a month, niche trends, content ideas, the AI Coach and the MCP server. What you do not get is the deep keyword and Outlier research volume, thumbnail generation and clipping that paid tiers carry. Start free, and upgrade only when research volume or production tooling becomes the actual bottleneck.

vidIQ vs TubeBuddy - which is better?

They optimize different loops. vidIQ's edge is Outlier discovery, the AI Coach and the MCP integration that connects channel data to Claude or ChatGPT; TubeBuddy competes on bulk processing and A/B testing of thumbnails and titles. If your bottleneck is deciding what to make next, vidIQ fits better; if you have steady output and want conversion testing, TubeBuddy fits better. Both gate most value behind paid plans, so judge on the research you actually do weekly.

Can vidIQ help me monetize a faceless YouTube channel?

Yes - faceless channels live or die on topic research because there is no on-camera personality to carry weak packaging. The Outlier data shows which formats break out in your niche without a face, and the clipping tools on MAX feed a Shorts pipeline.

The first thing to understand about User Interviews is what it is not. It is not a survey site. It is a recruiting platform that product teams at companies like Spotify, Adobe, Amazon and Pinterest use to find real humans for research sessions - and the pay reflects that. A 30-60 minute 1-on-1 video interview pays $50-150. An online focus group pays $100-250. Compare that to the $10 you get from a usability test or the $3 from a survey panel and the math gets interesting fast.

The catch, and there is always a catch, is that you do not get to just show up. You apply to studies through 2-minute screeners, researchers manually review those applications, and only 5-15% of applicants ever get an invitation. I tracked my own numbers for a month: 40-odd applications, 6 invites, 4 completed sessions. That is the real funnel.

Payment runs through Tremendous, an incentives platform offering 1,000+ reward options - virtual Visa cards, Amazon, Target, Starbucks, or donations - across 200+ countries. Most rewards land within 1-3 business days after the researcher approves your session. Researchers legally have up to 10 business days, and a few use all of it. One thing that surprised me: User Interviews itself does not pay out cash. No PayPal, no Venmo, no bank transfer. The closest cash-like option on platform-issued incentives is a virtual prepaid Visa. If a study shows a different incentive type, that researcher is paying you directly and PayPal becomes possible - it is disclosed before you apply.

The Money Angle

Here is where an experienced operator plays this differently from a casual survey-taker:

1. Your job title is the product. A college student qualifies for $50 consumer interviews. A project manager, nurse, accountant or software developer gets invited to B2B sessions paying $100-250 because researchers pay premiums for professional perspectives. Fill out your profile with your actual work tools, industry and seniority - that is what the matching engine reads.

2. The all-in hourly rate is the honest number. A hands-on 2026 review earned $81 per hour during sessions, but $42 per hour once application time was counted. Applications average 2 minutes each, but volume matters. Budget your time accordingly: 30 minutes of daily applications, a few hours of sessions per week.

3. Stack it, do not depend on it. This is a gap-filler, not a job. The realistic pattern that works: User Interviews for the high-ticket sessions, UserTesting (which now owns this platform, as of January 2026) for the steady $10 filler tests, Outlier for the AI-training hours in between. Three sources beats one.

4. AI's legitimate role here is small and specific. You can use AI to track new study postings, draft honest 2-3 sentence screener answers based on your real experience, and prep your thoughts before a session. What you cannot do is fake your way through. The platform runs AI-powered fraud detection, sessions are live video, and roughly 0.6% of sessions get flagged. Lying on a screener to grab a B2B study you do not qualify for is the fastest route to a permanent ban - and researchers talk to each other.

The Honest Cons

  • Gift cards, not cash. For most studies your reward is a Tremendous choice - excellent selection, but if you need rent money, a virtual Visa is the closest you get on platform-issued incentives.
  • The 10-day window is real. Most pay lands in 1-3 days, but the researcher controls the clock, and slowpokes use the full 10 business days. Do not count on money being there Friday.
  • Volume is weather, not climate. Some weeks bring 6 relevant studies; some bring zero. Your profile, your country and plain luck all move it. US, UK, Canada and Australia see the most; Recruit projects also cover France, Germany, South Africa and India.
  • High-ticket studies are gatekept hard. The $200 focus groups target narrow professional profiles. If you are a general consumer, your realistic average is the $20-40 unmoderated tier.
  • It changed hands. UserTesting acquired the platform in January 2026. So far nothing has gotten worse - payment options actually improved - but "acquired" has historically been the first chapter of "degraded" in this industry.

Bottom Line

If you have a professional background and honest opinions, User Interviews is the highest effective hourly rate in the get-paid-for-your-input category - $75-150 per booked session beats everything else that requires no portfolio. Just walk in knowing the funnel: most applications pay nothing, rewards are gift cards, and the money arrives on the researcher's schedule, not yours. Stack it with UserTesting and one other income source and it earns its place; treat it as a paycheck and it will disappoint you.

❓ FAQ

How much can I realistically make per month?

Track the funnel, not the sticker rates. A hands-on 2026 review earned $81 per hour during sessions but $42 per hour all-in once application time was counted, with a 16% qualification rate. Part-time participants report roughly $200-600 per month. The realistic pattern: apply to everything remotely relevant in 30 minutes a day, complete 2-6 sessions a week, and stack the platform with UserTesting or Outlier rather than treating it as standalone income.

Why do I get gift cards instead of PayPal?

The platform switched to Tremendous as its incentives provider, and the current contract excludes cash options - no PayPal, Venmo, bank transfer or physical prepaid cards on platform-issued rewards. Your best cash-like pick is a virtual Visa card, which spends anywhere online. Some researchers distribute incentives themselves and can pay via PayPal or direct transfer; the incentive type is always shown on the study page before you apply, so filter for those if cash matters to you.

Can I use AI to pass screeners or do the interviews for me?

No, and attempting it is how accounts die. Sessions happen over live video with a researcher watching, the platform runs AI-powered fraud detection on every session, and about 0.6% get flagged for fraud. Answering screener questions dishonestly to grab a high-paying B2B study is the classic ban path. The legitimate AI use is narrow: monitoring new postings fast, drafting honest screener answers from your real experience, and organizing your availability. The product being sold here is a real human's attention - AI cannot substitute for it.

Transcription tools mostly split into two camps: meeting-notes bots that transcribe your calendar, and production tools that turn uploaded audio into billable deliverables. HappyScribe does both, but the money is in the second camp - transcripts, subtitles and translations in 150+ languages, with a human-proofreading tier that ships in four hours.

Pricing without the marketing gloss

The key distinction: recordings are free and unlimited on every plan, uploads are what you pay for. The free plan gives unlimited Notetaker and mobile recordings (45 minutes each) plus a one-time 10 upload minutes - not monthly, once. Basic costs $17/mo monthly or $8.50/mo annual and buys 120 upload minutes a month (1,440 minutes a year on annual billing). Pro is $29/$19 with 600 minutes a month, unlimited AI Chat, three seats and every export format including STL and FCPXML for broadcast workflows. Business is $89/$59 with 6,000 minutes a month and five seats. Extra credits cost $0.20/min and never expire; plan minutes reset each cycle without rollover.

The human services tier is the sleeper product: professional proofreading from $2.00/min ($1.90 on Business), advertised at 99% accuracy with 4h+ delivery in 60+ languages. Traditional agencies charge several times that.

The freelance money angle

  • Captioning services. Market rates on freelance platforms run $1-$5 per video minute for captions and subtitles; your AI cost on HappyScribe is $0.20/min or less inside a plan. The workflow - upload, auto-generate, fix names and punctuation, export SRT - is about 15 minutes of work per 30-minute video. Ten small YouTube clients a month is a real side income.
  • YouTube multilingual tracks. Creators now upload multiple audio/subtitle languages; translating existing subtitles through the 80+ language engine is minutes of work per video. Pair it with Rask AI when a client wants full voice dubbing rather than text subtitles.
  • Meeting-notes as a productized service. The free unlimited Notetaker means you can run client calls through it at zero marginal cost - consultants and VAs resell structured summaries as part of retainer packages.
  • Agency math. Business at $59/mo annual with five seats, shared glossaries and style guides fits a small subtitling team; anything a client needs broadcast-grade goes to the human tier at $2/min and gets marked up.

How it compares

Rask AI owns voice dubbing and lip-sync - different deliverable. DeepL translates text but does not transcribe. Descript is an editor first, with transcription as one feature. Fireflies AI and Otter AI are meeting-notes tools without serious subtitle workflows. HappyScribe is the only one of the group where the subtitle deliverable itself is the product, with a human tier attached. For the wider field, see the AI transcription comparison and the dubbing tools breakdown - and if voice cloning is the goal, ElevenLabs is the specialist.

Honest limitations

The free upload allowance being one-time (not monthly) makes the free plan a demo, not a workstation. Accent-heavy audio still needs manual cleanup regardless of the 150+ language claim. Translation quality swings by language pair - treat the AI output as a first pass for anything client-facing. Plan minutes vanish at each renewal, so annual billing (which hands over the year's minutes upfront) is the sensible default for steady workloads. Check current rates at happyscribe.com/pricing - the plan structure was reworked recently and may shift again. For a transcription-adjacent income play, the captioning angle in this guide pairs well with it.

Questions we get asked

Is the free plan enough for real work?

For recordings, yes: unlimited Notetaker, mobile and browser recordings up to 45 minutes each, transcribed free. For uploaded files, no - you get 10 minutes once, then uploads need a paid plan (Basic from $8.50/mo annual). Freelancers testing the captioning market should spend the free minutes on one real client sample before committing.

How good is the AI translation?

80+ languages share the same credit pool as transcription, and output is usable for subtitles and internal content in major pairs. For client-facing material, either budget a proofreading pass or use the human subtitle-translation service from $2.00/min - that is what the 99%-accuracy, 4-hour-delivery tier exists for.

Can I actually make money with it?

Yes, and the math is boring but real: charge $1-$5 per video minute for captioning while your AI cost runs $0.20/min or less on a plan, polish the output, keep the spread. The same arbitrage works for podcast transcripts, course subtitles and YouTube multilingual tracks - see the transcription tools comparison for where it sits against meeting-notes tools.

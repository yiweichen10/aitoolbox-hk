Delphi clones you. Feed it your videos, books, podcasts and writings (12 million words on the paid tier), and it answers voice calls, texts and questions in your voice and judgment, around the clock. The free tier puts your clone on Delphi's network; the paid plans - and this is the notable part - start at $5,000 a year, positioning Delphi as B2B infrastructure for coaches, executives and experts who already monetize their time.

What the plans actually cost

Scaler costs $5,000/year (all plans are annual - no monthly option is listed): 10 integrations, pro voice calls, 12M training words, actions and developer platform, Slack, and product recommendations. Scaler + API runs $10,000/year and adds API access, SSO, 20M training words, unlimited integrations and 60-day onboarding support. Immortal is tailored pricing for public figures - white-glove build, WhatsApp, custom applications, unlimited training. You keep ownership of your content and data on every tier.

Where the money angle sits

The direct play is selling access to yourself at scale. A consultant charging $300/hour can sell maybe 20 hours a week; a Delphi clone answers thousands of conversations without diluting the brand - Patreon, Circle and Skool communities bolt it on as a 24/7 expert-in-residence perk. Product recommendations are built in, so the clone can sell your courses and gear the way Stan Store or Kajabi funnels do. The second, quieter business is agency work: at a $5k floor, agencies white-glove clones for executives, authors and founders and charge $10-30k per build plus retainer - the pricing effectively filters out solo creators and filters in agencies. A third angle is lead generation: a free network clone answers prospect questions and routes the serious ones to paid calls, similar to how Chatbase turns support into sales flows.

The honest drawbacks

The $5,000/year floor is the whole story: there is no $50/mo tier, so a creator needs roughly $420/month of new revenue just to break even - that math rules Delphi out for hobbyists. The free tier keeps your clone inside Delphi's network, where discovery depends on their traffic rather than yours. Voice clones need serious training material to sound like you; thin input produces an uncanny assistant that erodes trust instead of building it. And regulated advice - health, finance, legal - carries real liability when a clone answers confidently and wrong. HeyGen covers the video-avatar version of this problem; Delphi is the conversational one.

Bottom line

Delphi is a strong bet for two profiles: experts already selling their time at high rates, and agencies adding clone-building to their service menu. For a creator with a small audience, the free network tier is a lead-generation experiment, not a business. Current plans sit on the Delphi pricing page.

Questions we get asked

How is a paid Delphi different from the free one?

A free Delphi lives on Delphi's network, where people find and talk to it. Paid plans (Scaler from $5k/year, Scaler + API at $10k) take your clone to your own channels - website, product, community, Slack - with integrations, pro voice calls and far higher training limits. The official FAQ frames it exactly this way.

Can agencies build clones for clients with Delphi?

Yes, and it is one of the stronger business models on the platform. The $5k entry pushes solo creators out and gives agencies room to charge $10-30k per white-glove build plus retainers. Scaler + API adds the API access, SSO and onboarding support that client deployments need.

What training data does a Delphi clone need?

Everything that captures how you think: videos, podcasts, books, articles, course material. Scaler's ceiling is 12M training words (20M on Scaler + API). Quality beats volume - thin or off-hand content produces generic answers that stop sounding like you.

What Stan Store Actually Is (From Someone Who Sells From a Bio Link)

I have sold digital products from a Stan Store for over a year, before that from a Gumroad tab and a Linktree that sent people everywhere but the checkout. So this is written from the seller's chair, not Stan's marketing page. Stan does one job: it takes the single link you get in your Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube bio and turns it into a real store. Visitors see product tiles, tap one, and pay right there on their phone. No website, no separate course platform, no scheduling tool to wire up.

The reason it caught on with creators is the fee model. Most link-in-bio tools are free or cheap but then take a cut of sales, or they don't sell at all. Stan flips it: you pay a flat monthly fee and it takes nothing from your sales. For someone already posting to a warm audience every day, that is the cheapest on-ramp to selling your own stuff.

How People Make Money on It

Stan is not a traffic source. It is the page you send people who already trust you. The four ways I have seen creators turn it into income:

  • Digital downloads for a social-native audience. PDF guides, Lightroom presets, Canva templates, prompt packs, Notion dashboards. Upload once, set a price, and delivery is automatic. A TikTok creator with 50k followers selling a $17 preset pack at 100 sales a month clears about $1,650 with almost no work after the first upload.
  • Coaching and consultation bookings. The built-in calendar takes payment at booking, so you stop paying for Calendly. A fitness or business coach bundles a $37 PDF with a $200 one-on-one call and fills the week from Reels alone.
  • Mini-courses hosted on the page. Even the $29 plan hosts video modules. A $197 course promoted through Instagram Reels at a 2% conversion on 10k followers is 200 sales a month - roughly $39k gross, and Stan takes none of it beyond Stripe.
  • Memberships and recurring packs on Pro. The $99 plan adds subscriptions, so you can drop a fresh pack of prompts or templates each month at near-zero marginal cost. This is the same play Patreon users run, but the checkout lives in your bio instead of a separate site.

What I Like and What I Don't

The good: setup is genuinely fast. I went from sign-up to a live, purchase-ready store in under half an hour. The one-tap mobile checkout converts better than a multi-page site because the buyer never leaves the app. And the 0% cut is real - on a $97 product sold 20 times a month I keep about $1,882; Gumroad's free plan would skim roughly $194 of that.

The rough edges are just as real. There is no free plan, so you pay $29 before your first sale - fine if you already earn, rough if you are starting cold. Email automation (sequences, tagging, behavioral flows) is locked behind the $99 Pro tier, so the $29 plan is really a capture-and-broadcast tool; if you want funnels you pay double or add Kit and pay for that too. Every store looks near-identical - limited templates, no custom domain on any plan, so there is no SEO and no brand control over the URL. And Stan gives you no discovery; all traffic is on you. The people who quit and call it a scam usually never had an audience to send in the first place.

Stan Store vs the Other Tools Covered Here

This site already covers the rest of the creator-income stack, and they are not competitors so much as different jobs:

  • Gumroad - cheapest for one-off file sales (free to start, 10% on the free plan) but sends buyers to a separate checkout site.
  • Patreon - best for a simple fan club with some discovery; takes a flat ~10% plus processing.
  • Substack - the paid-newsletter path; 10% of paid plus Stripe.
  • Whop - product storefront, up to 30% on its marketplace.
  • Ko-fi - free tipping hub, 0% on tips.
  • Skool - paid community plus course, 0% on Pro at a flat $99.

Stan's lane is the social storefront: when your buyers live in Stories and Reels, it is the fastest way from follower to customer. Use Gumroad for file delivery off-platform, Patreon for the fan club, Skool for the community, and Stan for the bio-link sale.

How to Start (If You Already Have an Audience)

  • Sign up at stan.store and start the 14-day trial - no card needed to build.
  • Connect Stripe (or PayPal) so you can take payments; this is the only per-sale cost, about 2.9% plus $0.30.
  • Upload one paid product you can deliver automatically - a PDF, a preset pack, or a short video course.
  • Turn on Instagram AutoDMs with a keyword so buyers and lead-magnet grabbers get pulled into a DM.
  • Put the Stan link in your bio and mention it in every post for a week; watch which product tiles get taps and double down on that one.

If you have under ~2,000 engaged followers, hold off - pay for the audience first, then the store. If you already post to a warm crowd daily, Stan is the quickest $29 you will spend to start keeping 100% of your sales.

👍 Pros

  • Zero platform cut - you keep 100% of every sale after Stripe. On a $97 template sold 20 times a month you clear about $1,882; Gumroad's free plan would take roughly $194 of that. The flat fee pays for itself once you pass about $300 a month in sales.
  • Fast to launch - I had a live, purchase-ready page in under 30 minutes: upload a file, connect Stripe, set a price, share the link. No separate course host, no Calendly, no open Gumroad tab.
  • The bio-link format converts social traffic - when your whole audience lives in Stories and Reels, a single page with visible product tiles beats sending them to a multi-page site. The embedded one-tap checkout is built for the phone-in-hand buyer.
  • Course hosting and bookings included even on the $29 plan - you can host a video mini-course and take coaching calls on the same page, so a solo creator avoids paying for Teachable plus a scheduling tool.
  • Instagram AutoDMs pull the buyer into your world - set a keyword and Stan DMs the purchaser or lead-magnet downloader, which is a clean way to keep the relationship going off the platform.

👎 Cons

  • No free plan - you pay $29 a month before your first sale. For a total beginner with no audience yet, that is a real monthly bill with no guaranteed return, and Stan sends you no traffic.
  • Email automation is locked behind the $99 Pro tier - the $29 plan captures emails and sends broadcasts, but sequences, tagging, and behavioral automations need Pro. If you want real funnels you pay double or bolt on Kit or ConvertKit and pay for that too.
  • No custom domain on any plan - your store is always stan.store/yourname. You cannot put it on your own domain, so there is no SEO equity and no brand control over the URL. For a long-term business that stings.
  • Every store looks the same - limited templates and color options. If you sell a $500-plus course that needs a long sales page, testimonials, and a video letter, Stan's product pages will not close that sale; Kajabi or a real landing page will.
  • Stan is a checkout, not a discovery engine - all traffic must come from you. No marketplace, no SEO, weak analytics. The creators who quit blame the tool when the real problem was they never had an audience to send.

❓ FAQ

How much does Stan Store take, and what do you keep per sale?

Stan takes 0% of your sales on both plans - the vendor states it earns nothing from transactions. You pay a flat $29 a month (Creator) or $99 a month (Creator Pro), billed annually at $300 or $948. The only per-sale cost is payment processing through Stripe or PayPal, about 2.9% plus $0.30. So a $97 sale leaves you about $93.80. There is no free plan, but a 14-day trial runs on either tier. Pricing shifts, so confirm at stan.store before subscribing.

Can you actually make money on Stan Store, or is it hype?

Only if you already have a social audience or a clear offer. Stan is the store, not the crowd. The math that works: a fitness coach selling a $37 PDF workout plan to 2,000 engaged followers at a 2% conversion is 40 sales a month, about $1,450 gross, nearly all of it yours after Stripe. A TikTok creator with 50k followers selling a $17 preset pack at 100 sales a month clears roughly $1,650 passively. The people who fail treat Stan like a money button and never post the link.

Stan Store vs Gumroad or Beacons - which should I pick?

Pick Stan if your buyers come from Instagram or TikTok and you want one bio link that checks out on the spot. Gumroad is cheaper for one-off file sales (free to start, 10% on the free plan) but sends buyers to a separate site. Beacons is better if you mainly want a customizable bio link and brand-deal landing pages rather than selling your own products - it takes up to 9% per sale. If you run a paid community with a course, Skool (also covered on this site) takes 0% on Pro and adds gamification. Most creators I know use Stan for the social storefront and link out to the others for specific jobs.

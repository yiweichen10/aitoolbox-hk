What Exploding Topics Actually Is

Exploding Topics is a trend-discovery engine that reads millions of data points a day across search, social, forums, news, e-commerce, and podcasts, then scores which topics are climbing before they are obvious. Brian Dean (Backlinko) built it, Semrush bought it in 2024, and the database now holds 1.1M+ trends with roughly a thousand added each month. Every trend gets a status - Exploding, Regular, or Peaked - plus a 12-month forecast, which is the feature that separates it from free tools: Google Trends shows you the wave you already noticed, Exploding Topics hands you the wave six months before it breaks.

The platform runs three paid tiers - Entrepreneur $39/mo, Investor $99/mo, Business $249/mo, all billed annually with a 7-day trial - and every tier gates something you actually want, which is worth understanding before you pay.

The Money Angle

This is one of the few research tools where the output maps directly onto a monetization pipeline, and the people making real money with it run one of four plays:

1. Content sites that rank before competition exists. Publish on a topic while it is still "Exploding" and you own the SERP when search volume arrives. That early traffic converts through display ads (Google AdSense) or affiliate commissions (Amazon Associates). The catch flagged by every honest review: Exploding Topics gives you the topic, not the keyword difficulty - you validate volume and competition in Semrush or Ahrefs before writing.

2. Product businesses built on the Trending Products database. The Investor tier ($99/mo) opens the door to rising physical products with monthly sales estimates, price bands, and best-selling SKUs. That is a sourcing brief for POD sellers (Printify, Etsy) and Amazon Merch designers (amazon-merch-on-demand): design for the rising niche while the market is thin.

3. Selling the research itself. Trend reports for a vertical - "12 rising topics in home fitness, Q1 2026" - are digital products that sell on Gumroad or become the backbone of a paid newsletter via Substack. The Meta Trends reports give you the editorial framing for free.

4. Early-stage flipping. Trending Startups data feeds people who buy and sell digital assets - a site or tool riding an early trend is worth multiples more on Flippa before the trend is mainstream than after.

Two pricing traps: every tier bills annually (Entrepreneur is $468/year, not $39), and the add-ons are brutal - the Trends API runs $1,000-$4,000/month and TikTok Insights adds $59/month, both restricted to Business subscribers. The Honest Cons

Discovery is not validation - the single most common regret in user reviews is paying $39, flagging ten trends, and having no idea which ones they can actually rank for or sell into. The caps push you up-tier quickly: 100 tracked trends fills fast if you work across niches, and 10 analyses a month is one working day's research for an agency. False positives exist and the company publishes no accuracy rate; some flagged "trends" are social chatter that never becomes buying intent. And because Semrush owns it, expect the product and pricing to keep moving - the Pro subscription is separate from a Semrush plan, so your keyword tool and your trend tool are two bills.

Bottom Line

Exploding Topics is the closest thing to a legal crystal ball for demand, and the monetization paths are concrete: rank early, source early, or sell the research. Pay only when you have an execution plan - a site, a store, a channel - ready to move on what it flags. Buying discovery without execution is how people end up paying $468 a year to watch pretty charts. Start on the free directory for a month, confirm you will actually act on what you find, then buy Investor if products are your game.

Questions we get asked

Is Exploding Topics worth $39/month, or is Google Trends enough?

They answer different questions. Google Trends tells you how a topic you already know about is performing; Exploding Topics surfaces topics you had not thought of, scored and forecast 12 months out. If your business depends on catching demand early - content sites, POD, dropshipping, product launches - the discovery layer earns its fee. If you only ever validate known keywords, stay free.

Which plan should an e-commerce seller actually buy?

Honestly: Investor at $99/mo. The Trending Products database is the reason sellers subscribe, and it is locked to the Investor tier, not the $39 Entrepreneur plan. Content-only users can stay on Entrepreneur; anyone needing CSV exports or the API is into Business ($249/mo) plus add-ons.

How do I turn a trend flag into actual money?

Treat the flag as step one of a pipeline: validate search volume and competition in a keyword tool, check whether products or content exist for it yet, then build for it early - a niche store on Etsy, a Printify design, a YouTube channel, or a content site monetized with display ads. The tool's own methodology is discovery, not execution; the money comes from what you build in the 6-12 month window before the trend goes mainstream.

What Rask AI Actually Is

Rask AI is a browser-based dubbing studio. Upload a video (or paste a YouTube or Google Drive link), and it transcribes, translates, clones the speakers' voices, re-renders the audio in 135+ languages, and can match lip movement to the new audio. Up to 20+ speakers are separated automatically, each keeping their own cloned voice, and an editable transcript lets you approve or fix every line before a single minute is billed. SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and C2PA membership put it in the compliance tier that agencies and corporate clients ask for.

The positioning that matters for this site: Rask does not create content - it multiplies content you have already validated. That makes it one of the highest-return spend lines in a creator's budget, and also one of the easiest to misjudge, because the pricing multiplies faster than the marketing suggests.

The Money Angle

Republish what already works. The cheapest audience you will ever buy is one that wants content you already have, in a language you have not published in. The sequence that works: find your best-performing video (not your favorite - the one the numbers picked), dub it into 2-3 languages with voice cloning, and measure outbound performance before touching the rest of the library. A YouTube back catalogue dubbed into Spanish, Portuguese and Hindi reaches markets where your English channel simply does not exist.

Sell localization as a service. Businesses pay project rates for video localization that make the tool cost look small: a 10-minute corporate training video dubbed into 4 languages can bill at $500-1,500 while consuming roughly 40-120 minutes of your plan (about $30-90 of included-minute cost on annual billing). The transcript-approval workflow is what makes this sellable - you deliver a reviewed script, not a black box. Listings on Fiverr or Upwork for "AI video localization with human review" price exactly this workflow.

Course and product expansion. A course that sells in English can be reissued in 2-3 languages on Udemy or your own Kajabi site, and dubs of product demos serve export markets. The rule: only translate content that already earns - Rask multiplies winners and losers alike.

The Honest Cons

The multiplier math punishes the exact content people buy the tool for. Talking-head videos want lip-sync, lip-sync triples the meter, and a modest backlog plus three languages can blow through Creator Pro's 100 minutes in one afternoon. Monthly plans are strictly worse for uneven workloads because minutes vanish at each cycle. Voice cloning's language list (57) is much shorter than the dubbing list (135+), so your smaller target markets get a generic voice that sounds noticeably less like you. And Rask only localizes - it will not fix a video that never resonated in its original language, so testing in one language first is not optional, it is the entire cost-control strategy.

Bottom Line

Rask AI is the shortest path from one validated video to five markets, and the per-minute pricing is honest once you do the math: count your languages, decide whether lip-sync is worth tripling the meter, and buy annual minutes only when the backlog justifies them. Start with your single best performer, measure the new market, and expand the library only for videos that earned it. Creators who treat it as a multiplier for proven content get their money's worth; everyone else funds an experiment the audience never asked for.

Questions we get asked

Is Rask AI free to try?

There is no permanent free plan. You get a one-time trial with 3 minutes of dubbing, voice cloning and the editor, no credit card required. After that, every plan is a paid subscription - the cheapest is Creator at $39/month for 25 minutes.

Why did my minutes disappear so quickly?

Minutes multiply fast. One minute of video in one language is 1 minute; but lip-sync adds 3 minutes per video minute, on-screen text translation adds 3 more, and each additional language multiplies the total. A 1-minute video dubbed into 2 languages with lip-sync costs 8 minutes (2 dubbing + 6 lip-sync). Plan your language count and whether you actually need lip-sync before rendering.

Can you actually make money with Rask AI?

Three routes hold up in practice. First, republishing: a video that already works in English gets dubbed into 3-5 languages, and the new markets' ad revenue, affiliate clicks or course sales come almost free after the minute cost. Second, services: businesses pay per-finished-minute or per-project rates for localization that far exceed the $0.67-1.10 per included minute you pay - freelancers on Fiverr sell exactly this. Third, course expansion: translating a course for Udemy or Kajabi into 2-3 languages multiplies the audience on content you already recorded. The tool localizes; the market research and the pricing are still your job.

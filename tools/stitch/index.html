Free · daily credit limit
Verified via stitch.google.com and Google's Stitch FAQ (relayed through documented third-party sources), 2026-11-25. Free of charge, no paid tier as of November 2026. Usage runs on a daily credit pool per Google account that resets at midnight UTC; a Google forum representative put the pool at 400 credits in September 2026 (visible in settings, subject to change - Google has adjusted limits repeatedly since 2025). Manual canvas edits and all exports cost nothing; AI actions spend credits per an official December 2025 breakdown: rapid agent screen 1, pro or redesigned screen 3, interactive screen first generation 3, each interactive edit 1. No way to buy extra credits. Access: Google account, 18+, countries where Gemini is available. AI Ultra subscribers get priority access to the Stitch Loop waitlist.

Google Stitch turned 'vibe design' into a free utility: describe a screen in plain English, paste a sketch or a screenshot, and Gemini renders a high-fidelity UI in seconds. Every generation can leave the canvas two ways - as a Figma file with auto-layout and named layers, or as front-end code in HTML/CSS, Tailwind, React, Flutter, SwiftUI and more. The March 2026 Stitch 2.0 rebuild added an infinite canvas where each generation stays as a separate iteration (compare three directions side by side instead of overwriting), a design agent that reasons across multi-screen projects, voice input, and an MCP server that lets coding agents read your designs.

Pricing: free, with an asterisk

Stitch is free of charge per Google's own FAQ, with no paid tier as of November 2026. The real cost is the daily credit pool tied to your Google account: 400 credits as of September 2026 per a Google forum representative, resetting at midnight UTC, visible in settings. Google has changed the pool shape twice since 2025, so treat 400 as directional and check your own counter. Manual canvas edits and every export cost nothing; credits buy AI actions (rapid screen 1, pro or redesigned screen 3, interactive edit 1 per the official December 2025 breakdown). Access needs a Google account, age 18+, in countries where Gemini is available.

How people make money with it

Freelancers sell speed. A landing-page design set that took a day now takes an hour, which means 'wireframes, high-fidelity designs and a coded handoff' becomes a fixed-price Fiverr or Upwork package with real margin. Founders prototype investor demos at zero cost - a clickable, plausible app screen beats another slide. Agencies run paid 0-to-1 design sprints: prompts and sketches in a morning workshop, three directions exported to Figma by lunch, the client's designer keeps final control. Developers use the code export as billable scaffolding - hand the Flutter or React output to the client, then charge for the real build on top. For positioning against the rest of the field, see the UI/UX design tool comparison and the design tools guide for non-designers.

The honest trade-offs

This is a Labs experiment wearing a product's clothes: no SLA, credit limits that have shifted twice, and Google's demonstrated willingness to reprice or retire experiments without ceremony. The daily cap cannot be bought past - heavy sessions belong early in the UTC day. Output leans generic Material style; a distinctive brand identity still needs human hands. Reviews consistently flag accessibility gaps - contrast ratios, touch-target sizes, missing ARIA labels - so run an audit before anything ships. And multi-step UX logic (conditional flows, state) remains beyond it; that work still lives in Figma or code.

Questions we get asked

Is Google Stitch really free?

Yes - Google's FAQ states it is provided free of charge, and as of November 2026 there is no paid tier or way to buy extra credits. Usage runs on a daily credit pool (400 per account as of September 2026, per a Google forum representative) that resets at midnight UTC. Check the settings page in Stitch for your current balance, because Google has adjusted the limits before.

Can Stitch replace Figma?

No - and it is not trying to. Treat it as the upstream accelerator: generate three directions in ten minutes, export the winner to Figma via paste-to-Figma, and do brand, polish and accessibility work there. For AI inside Figma itself, see Figma AI; for code-first generation, compare v0 by Vercel.

What happens if Google changes or kills it?

Export early and often - designs leave through Figma export and code export at no credit cost, so nothing you like is trapped. The realistic posture is to treat Stitch output as disposable drafts and keep your source of truth in Figma or code, not on the Stitch canvas.

What Browse AI Actually Is

Browse AI trains little cloud robots to do the most boring job on the internet: revisit a webpage forever and copy the changing parts into a spreadsheet. You install a Chrome extension, click through the data you want - a pricing table, a directory of leads, a product listing - and the robot replays your clicks on a schedule, handling pagination, login walls, and layout changes on its own. When the site redesigns, the self-healing selectors adapt instead of silently breaking like a hand-written scraper.

The company (founded 2020 in Canada, public since 2021) claims 770,000+ users, runs SOC 2 Type II and GDPR compliance, and ships 250+ prebuilt robots for popular targets. But the interesting shift since 2024 is that Browse AI now sells monitoring as much as extraction - and monitoring is where the money is.

The Money Angle

1. Monitoring retainers, the quiet agency product. E-commerce sellers, real estate agents, and recruiters all have the same need: tell me the moment a competitor changes a price, a listing appears, or a job gets posted. A Browse AI robot does that for $19-$69/month in credits; businesses pay $100-$500/month for the alert service with a clean report. The robot does the work, you own the client relationship - the same reseller spread as any agency product, with near-zero marginal cost per added client. Client intake through Contra or Fiverr gets the first contracts going.

2. Lead lists from public directories. Robots scrape Google Maps, LinkedIn, and industry directories into Google Sheets, formula columns clean the data, and the output feeds outreach platforms - Apollo, Instantly AI, Clay. Selling verified, niche-specific lead lists (or charging per campaign) is the oldest data business on the internet; Browse AI makes the supply side a weekend project instead of an engineering one.

3. Arbitrage alerts for resellers. Robots watching eBay, Facebook Marketplace, and clearance pages flag underpriced items minutes after posting - the edge that separates professional resellers from casual ones. Pair the alerts with selling the finds on Etsy or your own store channel.

4. Your own content and SEO ops. Track SERP positions, competitor content calendars, and price drops that become article fodder - feeding sites monetized with Google AdSense or affiliate links via Amazon Associates.

The Honest Cons

The credit system punishes the exact thing the product is best at. Monitoring is the killer feature and also the treadmill: the vendor's own FAQ example - 50 detail pages checked every 3 days - consumes about 500 credits a month, and doubling frequency doubles the burn. Credits not rolling over means an unused December is money gone. Domain caps push multi-client agencies up-tier faster than the pricing page suggests, and the annual-first pricing means testing Browse AI properly costs $228, not $19. Serious volume (millions of rows) belongs on Apify or self-managed infrastructure, not a per-credit SaaS. None of this is hidden - the dashboard shows every run's cost before it executes - but budget from your real monitoring frequency, not the tier minimums.

Questions we get asked

How much does Browse AI really cost per month for a monitoring job?

Do the credit math before subscribing. One credit covers 10 rows, one standard page, or one screenshot - whichever is larger. A competitor price monitor over 20 pages checked daily is roughly 600 credits/month, which fits Personal ($19/mo annual, 12,000 credits/year). A 100-product catalog with detail pages checked daily is 3,000+ credits/month and needs Professional ($69/mo annual). The trap is frequency: halving the check interval doubles the bill.

How do people make money with it?

Three proven plays: (1) sell monitoring retainers - local businesses and e-commerce sellers pay $100-$500/month for competitor price and listing alerts that cost you $19-$69 in credits; (2) lead generation - scrape directories and marketplaces into Google Sheets, clean them, and feed outreach tools like Apollo or Instantly; (3) arbitrage alerts - robots watching eBay, Facebook Marketplace, and retail clearance pages flag underpriced items for resellers within minutes of posting.

Browse AI vs Apify - which should I pick?

Browse AI for point-and-click speed and monitoring retainers where a business user maintains the robots; Apify for developer-grade actors, huge volumes, and its marketplace.

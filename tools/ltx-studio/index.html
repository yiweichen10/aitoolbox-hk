LTX Studio, from Lightricks, is the AI video tool built for projects that have a script, not just a prompt. You feed it a logline or a full treatment, it builds a storyboard with shots, characters and locations, then generates the footage - and it now hosts rival models under one roof (Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, FLUX.2 Pro, Nano Banana 2), so one subscription covers what used to take five. That hub positioning is the pitch: one workspace from script to export.

What the plans actually cost

Lite costs $15/mo monthly or $12/mo billed yearly ($144) with 8,000 credits/mo, no watermarks and editing tools - still a personal license. Pro at $125/mo ($100 yearly, $1,200) reaches 110,000 credits, adds Veo 3.1/Fast/Lite and 3 collaborators per project. Enterprise is custom with SSO, SOC2 compliance and custom LTX model training. Where the money angle sits

The strongest commercial play is pre-production selling: agencies and freelancers generate storyboards and pitch decks from a client brief, sell the concept for $500-2,000 before anyone commits to production spend - the pitch deck generator exists for exactly this. Faceless storytelling channels are second: Standard's commercial license covers monetized uploads, and the faceless channel guide maps the workflow. Third is multi-model arbitrage - Standard's guest models mean hero shots can route through Kling AI or Seedance without separate subscriptions, and image models like FLUX and Nano Banana sit in the same credits pool. Dubbing and captions built in cut the video editing tail cost on multi-language client work.

The honest drawbacks

The free tier's personal-use license is a trap for anyone monetizing content - commercial rights start at Standard. Credit burn on multi-shot scenes adds up fast, and per-generation costs are shown in-app rather than published, so monthly budgets need trial and error. The model lineup churns: Veo 3.1 lives only on Pro, and Lightricks reserves the right to swap models at any time, which can strand a workflow built around one engine. LTX's own 2.5 model generates fast and cheap but trails Veo on realism for hero shots - comparing it with Runway in our video generator comparison is worth 20 minutes before you commit. The storyboard-to-edit workspace also has a learning curve that single-prompt tools do not.

Bottom line

For anyone selling video concepts or producing commercial content, Standard's $28-35 with 28,000 credits is the entry ticket, and Pro earns its price when Veo access and collaborators matter. Skip Lite if money touches the output - the license will not cover it. Current plans sit on the LTX Studio pricing page.

Questions we get asked

Can I use the free LTX Studio plan for client work?

No. Free and Lite carry a personal-use license that explicitly bars business, marketing or paid work. Commercial rights start at Standard ($35/mo monthly, $28/mo billed yearly). Budget for Standard if any money touches the output - that includes monetized YouTube uploads.

Do I need Pro to access Veo 3.1?

Yes - Veo 3.1, Fast and Lite sit behind the Pro tier ($125/mo, $100/mo yearly). Standard covers Kling 2.6/3.0, Seedance 2.0, Nano Banana 2, FLUX.2 Pro and ChatGPT Images 2.0. If Veo quality is the reason you are subscribing, that is a Pro decision.

How do LTX Studio credits work?

Credits refill monthly on paid plans (Free gets 800 one-time). Generation costs vary by model and settings and are shown before each render in-app. Standard adds 28,800 bonus computing seconds per month for the first 3 months, and top-ups can be purchased on Standard and above.

Retell AI Review 2026: Sell an AI Receptionist, Keep the Margin

I have watched a lot of "AI business" ideas that fall apart the moment you try to charge for them. Voice agents are different. A local business losing calls after 6pm does not need to be convinced that missed calls cost money - they already know, and they are already paying a human or losing the job. That is why the AI receptionist is one of the few AI products a plumber will actually buy.

What Retell AI Actually Is

Retell AI is a platform for building AI phone agents. You pick a voice, write the prompt, attach tools - book an appointment, transfer the call, look something up - and connect a phone number. When someone calls, the agent answers, talks back with low latency, and either resolves the call or hands it to a human.

Under the hood it bundles the pieces that used to be a two-week integration project: telephony, a speech-to-speech engine, text-to-speech voices and a language model. You can bring your own provider for each, but the default stack just works, and the visual editor means a non-developer can get a working agent on the phone the same day.

That packaging is the point. Vapi and Retell do roughly the same job; Retell's pitch is that more of it is done for you at a predictable all-in rate.

The Money Angle

Local businesses pay real money for someone to answer the phone, and the AI version is cheap enough to leave a wide margin.

1. Sell the receptionist as a service. This is the main path. Pick one niche - dentists, HVAC, law firms, med spas, real estate - and sell an agent that answers, qualifies and books. Charge $500-$2,000 a month, with a $500-$3,000 setup fee for the build. Your usage cost is about $0.11 a minute: 500 minutes of calls runs roughly $55, so a single $500 client covers the tool many times over.

2. After-hours and overflow only. Some businesses will not hand over the main line, but they will happily pay for nights, weekends and busy periods. It is an easier first yes, it uses fewer minutes, and it is a natural upsell to full coverage later.

3. Appointment setting and lead qualification. Same agent, sharper pitch: it calls back web leads within a minute, qualifies them and books a slot. Charge per booked appointment and the client stops thinking about minutes entirely.

4. Reseller and white-label. A number of platforms let you put your own brand on the agent and resell it. The margin is the same; the difference is that you look like a software company instead of a freelancer, which matters when you raise prices.

In every case the tool is a cost of goods. The business is the niche, the demo number and the follow-up.

Pricing in 2026

Retell is pay-as-you-go with no platform fee. New accounts get $10 in free credits covering voice minutes and chat messages.

  • Voice agents: $0.07-$0.31 per minute published, depending on voice engine, LLM and telephony.
  • Chat agents: $0.002+ per message.
  • Default stack: Retell voice infrastructure $0.055/min + text-to-speech $0.015/min + LLM about $0.04/min + Retell telephony $0.00/min = roughly $0.11/min all-in.

Realistic production runs $0.11-$0.15/min once you use better voices or add capacity. Included at the base rate: agent templates, call analytics and transcripts, simulation testing, webhooks, API access, 20 concurrent calls and community support.

Add-ons to watch: extra concurrency is $8 per concurrent call per month, knowledge bases are $8 each per month (first 10 free), and a verified phone number is $10 a month plus a one-time $10 fee. Enterprise is custom, with volume discounts that can push the per-minute rate toward $0.05 once you are spending $3,000+ a month.

Where It Falls Short

The advertised $0.07/minute is a floor, not your bill. The default stack lands near $0.11/min and production with premium voices and add-ons runs $0.11-$0.15/min. Quote clients from the real rate or the margin you modeled quietly disappears.

Concurrency is metered. Twenty simultaneous calls are free, then it is $8 per concurrent call per month. A client with a genuinely busy line can add $80-$160 a month at peak, and that comes out of your pocket unless your contract covers it.

It is a developer-shaped product with a no-code surface. The visual builder gets you started, but real customization means webhooks, API calls and prompt engineering - and a weak prompt produces an agent that talks over people or confidently invents an answer on a live call, which is far worse than a voicemail.

Most of all, this is the most crowded corner of AI right now. Vapi, Bland, Synthflow, 11x and a dozen white-label resellers all tell the same AI receptionist story, so the tool is not the differentiator. Undifferentiated offers compete on price and race to the bottom.

Finally, compliance sits with you. Recording, consent and AI-disclosure rules vary by state and country, and a client in medical, legal or financial services can drag you into a problem if the agent mishandles a call. Get a contract that separates you from their outcomes before you take the retainer.

The Honest Verdict

Retell AI is one of the better ways to turn voice AI into a paying service because the economics are simple and the platform does the hard integration work for you. Build one strong demo in a single niche, let prospects call it, and sell the outcome - answered calls and booked jobs - rather than the technology. The per-minute cost is small enough that the business is not about the tool at all: it is about picking a niche, being findable, and following up. Get that right and Retell is just the cheap engine underneath a service you can actually charge for.

Retell AI vs Vapi - which should you use?

They are close siblings and either will run a production voice agent. Retell leans toward a more packaged, lower-friction build with a default all-in rate around $0.11/min and platform features included at the base rate, which suits an agency that wants to ship fast and keep costs predictable. Vapi leans toward flexibility and bring-your-own-everything, which suits builders who want to swap providers and control the stack, usually landing nearer $0.10-$0.30/min all-in. On price for the same job they trade blows, so the deciding factor is usually workflow and how much of the plumbing you want handled for you. Both face the same reality: the agent is a commodity and your offer, niche and follow-up decide whether the retainer renews.

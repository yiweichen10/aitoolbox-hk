Most paid-research advice stops at survey walls that pay $3 an hour. Respondent is the other end of the market: a recruitment platform that charges researchers real money for verified participants - and passes a serious slice of that to you. The panel counts 4.3M+ identity-verified people across 150+ countries, it has been running since 2016, and the client list on its own site includes Anthropic, Microsoft, Dropbox, GoDaddy and Stripe. That last part matters, because one of those client categories is new: AI labs buying human feedback, red-teaming and expert evaluation sessions.

What the pay actually looks like

The official numbers from the participant page: most sessions pay $50-$250 depending on length and seniority, senior B2B roles regularly pay $400+, focus groups run $75-$200 online and $100-$300 in person, and online surveys or short interviews pay $50-$150. Payment goes through Tremendous 5-7 business days after the session is verified. There is no fee to join and - per the platform's own FAQ - you will never be asked to pay to participate.

Run the hourly math and the pitch is obvious: a 45-minute interview at $150 is about $200 an hour. The catch is volume. Most participants get 1-3 invitations a month, because the matching model only invites you when your background actually fits the screener. Fewer shots, bigger shots.

How the screening gate works

Sign-up takes about 90 seconds, but the real gate is verification: government ID with a selfie, a SIM-issued phone number, a work email with a verified domain, and LinkedIn for professionals. The platform claims over 50 fraud signals checked at sign-up and under 1% reported fraud across the panel. Researchers see your ratings and history; copy-pasted or AI-written screener answers get flagged. In practice, a verified work email and a complete professional profile are the two things that separate people who get the $400 interviews from people who get the $50 surveys.

Making money with it

  • Professionals earn the premium. Product managers, finance leads, nurses, developers: one senior interview a month is $400+. The recent-studies feed on the participant page reads like a B2B salary board - pricing decisions, finance software, clinical tools.
  • AI training work is growing. Respondent now lists AI training & evaluation as a first-class research method - expert feedback and red-teaming sessions. For steady hours in that same lane, dedicated AI-lab platforms like Outlier and Mercor pay weekly; Respondent sessions pay more per hour but arrive irregularly.
  • Stack it as a portfolio. The realistic setup is Respondent for the fat sessions, Prolific for academic micro-studies near its £6/$8 hourly floor, UserTesting for $10 unmoderated tests on demand, and User Interviews as a second invitation pipeline. Keep the profiles synced and answer invitations fast - a typical study fills in about a day and a half.
  • On the other side of the table, freelancers who run client discovery use the researcher platform to recruit verified users for usability tests and interviews - cost is quoted per study, and the verification layer is what you are buying.

Respondent vs the alternatives

Against Prolific: Prolific is academic, higher-frequency, lower per-session, with hard minimum pay floors. Respondent is fewer-but-fatter, skewed to industry research. Against UserTesting: UserTesting is instant, unmoderated and capped around $10 a test; Respondent is moderated, slower and worth several times more per hour. Against AI-lab gig work (Mercor, Outlier): those are weekly engagements; Respondent is one-off sessions. They overlap less than you would think, which is exactly why stacking works. Honest limitations

Invitations gate everything - if your profile does not fit a paying niche, income stays near zero no matter how good the rates are. Verification means handing over ID and a selfie, which privacy-minded people skip. Sessions are usually recorded. And payout timing (5-7 business days after verification) means this never feels like a paycheck - it is a recurring bonus. For anyone with a professional background and a work email, the effective hourly rate still makes it one of the best side-income platforms you can join free. Start at the participant sign-up on respondent.io and let the matching model decide what you are worth.

Questions we get asked

How much can I actually earn per month on Respondent?

Treat it as a top-up, not a salary. Most participants get 1-3 invitations a month and complete one or two. At the official $50-$250 range that is roughly $75-$500 a month for ordinary professionals, more if you fit senior B2B niches - finance, product management, healthcare - where sessions pay $400+. People who stack it with Prolific for volume and UserTesting for quick unmoderated tests report steadier combined income.

Is Respondent legit, and how do payouts work?

It has been recruiting research participants since 2016, rates 4.6/5 on G2 from 400+ reviews (figure from Respondent's own site), and never asks participants to pay. Once your session is verified, the incentive is sent through Tremendous within 5-7 business days and you pick the payout method there. Every researcher and study is vetted before it reaches the panel.

What kinds of studies pay the most?

Focus groups and in-person sessions ($100-$300), senior B2B interviews ($400+), and increasingly AI training and evaluation work - red-teaming models or giving expert human feedback. Generic consumer surveys and short interviews pay the least ($50-$150). Keeping your work email verified and profile current matters because matching runs on your background, not luck.

Crowdin Review 2026: What the Hosted-Word Bill Really Costs and How People Make Money Localizing Apps

Crowdin is the localization platform most small software teams move to after outgrowing a shared spreadsheet. You upload your source strings, it tracks every target language separately, and it pushes approved translations back into your app, website or help center on each release.

I have run Crowdin on app-localization jobs and watched two-person studios bill clients for it. Here is what the platform actually does, what the 2026 bill looks like once hosted words start multiplying, and the honest version of where the money is.

What Crowdin actually does

Crowdin sits between your source content and the people translating it. You connect a repository (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket), upload files, or sync a CMS, and Crowdin parses the strings into a project. From there you machine-translate everything in a click, send specific strings to human translators, and let the platform deliver approved translations back through the API, the CLI, mobile SDKs or a webhook.

The pieces that matter in practice: translation memory (every approved sentence gets reused, which cuts the bill next time), glossaries (your product terms stay consistent across languages), QA checks (catches missing placeholders, broken tags and inconsistent punctuation before they ship), screenshots, and the in-context visual editor from Team up.

File formats are the quiet gate. Free handles 3 formats, Pro 5, Team 15, Team+ and Business unlimited. If your project is a WordPress theme, a React app and a set of Figma strings, you are not doing that on Free.

What Crowdin AI actually is

Crowdin AI ships on every plan, Free included. It pre-translates strings, suggests glossary entries from your existing content, flags segments that read wrong, and can proofread machine output before a human sees it. You can also route translation through 40+ third-party engines (DeepL, Google, Amazon, Microsoft) or bring your own LLM key.

Be clear about what that buys you. AI pre-translation is good on UI strings - short, context-light and repetitive. It is weak on marketing copy, legal text and anything with a joke in it. The workflow that actually works is AI first pass, human post-edit second, and Crowdin is built around exactly that pipeline. If you only need raw machine output, a plain DeepL subscription will do the job for less.

Where the money is

Do not plan on reselling Crowdin - there is no headline affiliate payout worth building a business on. The money is in the work the platform makes repeatable.

The localization-service math

Take one 5,000-word mobile app into 10 languages. That is 50,000 words of output. A fair freelance rate for AI-assisted post-editing is $0.08-0.12 per word, so the job invoices at $4,000-6,000.

Your costs: Crowdin Pro at $50/mo (annual) covers the project; pre-translation comes included through Crowdin AI, or a few hundred dollars of machine-translation credit through the Managed Balance add-on; human post-editors for the languages that matter run $0.03-0.06 per word, so $1,500-3,000 depending on how many languages you hand to a person instead of AI.

That leaves roughly $2,000-3,500 per app, and the follow-up work is the real asset - every release adds strings, so the client keeps paying. Two people doing this full time is a business, not a side hustle.

Recurring translation ops

The steadier offer is not the translation, it is the plumbing. Charge $300-1,500 per month to own a client's Crowdin project: onboard translators, keep the glossary clean, watch the hosted-word counter, ship each release. It is boring, it is monthly, and it is the part clients hate doing themselves.

Two more angles

Register as a vendor on the Crowdin marketplace - it is included on every plan - and take jobs that arrive through the platform while you build direct clients. And use the free open-source license deliberately: localizing a popular open-source project is unpaid, but it builds a public portfolio, and those maintainers become warm leads the moment they start a company.

Who Crowdin fits, and who should walk away

Crowdin fits small software teams, agencies and solo operators running continuous localization - projects where strings change every sprint and the same phrases come back again and again. The free tier is a real product rather than a demo, which makes it easy to prove a workflow before anyone pays.

Walk away if you are translating a one-off 40-page document. A TMS is overhead there; a freelance translator and a shared doc will do. Walk away too if you need marketing-grade copy in eight languages and have no budget for human editors, because the AI pass alone will not carry a brand voice. For the wider field, see how Crowdin stacks up against Lokalise, Phrase and Smartling.

FAQ

Is Crowdin really free for open-source projects?

Yes. Create a free account, set up the project, and request an Open Source License. Granted licenses include unlimited projects, strings and members, so an OSS project never hits the hosted-word wall. Academic licenses work the same way for educational use.

How do hosted words actually work?

Hosted words are your source words multiplied by the number of target languages. A 5,000-word app translated into 10 languages counts as 50,000 hosted words. The free plan's 60K allowance is therefore roughly a 6,000-word source in 10 languages, or a 60,000-word source in one language. The counter runs across every project on the account, not per project.

Can I make money with Crowdin if I am not a translator?

Yes, and it is usually the better position. Sell localization as a service and let the platform and freelance post-editors handle the translating. A 5,000-word app into 10 languages invoices at $4,000-6,000, your tool and translation costs land around $1,500-3,000, and every future release is recurring work. The monthly translation-ops retainer of $300-1,500 per client is the steadiest version of it.

What is the difference between Free, Pro and Team?

Free is $0 with 60K hosted words, one private project, unlimited public projects and 1 integration. Pro starts at $50/mo (annual) and adds a second private project and 2 integrations. Team starts at $150/mo (annual) with 100K hosted words, unlimited private projects, 5 managers, the in-context editor, custom QA checks, pre-translation workflows and 15 file formats.

Verdict

Crowdin is the localization platform I would hand a small software team without hesitating, mainly because the free tier is a working product and the pricing unit is honest enough to plan around. The catch is that hosted words multiply by languages, so the first serious project is where the bill actually shows up.

If you are buying it for your own product, start on Free, prove the pipeline, and upgrade when the counter gets close. If you are buying it to earn, sell the outcome and let Crowdin and post-editors handle the words - the margin sits in project management and recurring release work, not in competing on the lowest per-word rate.

10Web Review 2026: The AI Website Builder That Doubles as a White-Label Hosting Business

Most "AI website builder" tools hand you a nice-looking site you cannot easily leave. 10Web is built the other way around: the AI writes the first draft, but underneath sits a real WordPress site on managed Google Cloud hosting - and on the agency plans, a white-label dashboard you can put your own logo on and resell.

I have built WordPress sites for local businesses for years, and I have used 10Web both as a personal builder and as a delivery shortcut when a client needs something live this week. Here is what it does, what the 2026 bill looks like, and the honest version of where the money is.

What 10Web actually does

Three products are stacked into one account. The first is the agentic AI builder: describe a business, or paste an existing URL, and it generates a full site - layout, copy, images, pages - inside WordPress. The second is managed WordPress hosting on Google Cloud, with automated backups, staging, SSL, plugin updates and a CDN. The third is an ecommerce dashboard (products, orders, checkout, 10Web Payments, tax setup) for small stores.

That combination is the point. With most builders you get a site and then shop separately for hosting. Here the site, the host and the speed tooling are one bill, and what you own is portable WordPress rather than a proprietary format.

What the builder and its agents actually do

The builder is not one button. Three agents matter:

  • The Redesign agent takes an existing page and rebuilds it in a modern layout. This is the one that pays for itself when a client has a 2015 site and no budget for a full redesign.
  • The Cloning agent recreates any page from a URL. Handy for replicating a landing-page structure, less useful for anything with heavy custom code.
  • The Figma agent turns a Figma file into a production page. If a designer already handed over a file, this skips the tedious rebuild.

After generation you are in normal WordPress. That is a real advantage if you already know WP, and a real wall if you do not - the AI gets you a draft, not a finished site. Expect to rewrite the copy, fix spacing and wire up forms. Anyone selling "a website in five minutes" has not delivered a site a client will pay for.

How people actually make money with 10Web

The honest split is between using it to build faster and using it to sell hosting.

Build faster, charge the same. A local business site that used to take a week of layout work can be drafted in an hour and finished in a day. You still charge for the outcome, not the hours, so the margin comes from the time you did not spend. The Redesign agent is the fastest sell here - pitch "your site is slow and dated, I will rebuild it this week" and you have a productized offer instead of a vague redesign quote.

Resell it under your own brand. On Agency Core you get a branded dashboard, a branded builder and Stripe/PayPal billing, so clients see your product, not 10Web. You pay about $4 per site and can sell website subscriptions at $50-200/mo each. That is the recurring-revenue play, and it is the reason to look past the cheaper AI tiers.

Bundle hosting and care plans. Because hosting, backups, updates and the speed tooling are included, you can sell a $50-150/mo care plan per client and keep most of it. The 90+ PageSpeed score and the CDN are the proof you point at when you invoice.

How much can you charge per site?

That depends on what you are actually selling. A generated draft that you lightly edit is worth $300-800 to a small business that just needs to exist online. A customized build with real copy, forms, SEO setup and analytics is a $1,500-5,000 project, and the AI only removed the boring half of it. The white-label subscription is the more durable number: ten clients at $99/mo is roughly $1,000/mo of recurring revenue against an $80/mo platform bill.

Can you really resell 10Web as your own business?

Yes, on Agency Core and Agency Pro. The branded dashboard, builder, invoices and email notifications are designed for exactly that, and you set your own pricing tiers and connect your own Stripe or PayPal. Two caveats: the 7% transaction fee on Agency Core eats into margin until you move up to Agency Pro, and clients are still running WordPress on 10Web infrastructure, so if you outgrow the platform you are migrating sites, not flipping a switch.

Where 10Web is weak

The AI output is a first draft. Generated copy is generic and the layouts need a human eye, so the "instant site" story collapses the moment a client has opinions.

Credits are the hidden meter. Heavy regeneration burns them fast, and top-ups and extra sites add up. The visitor caps (10K-120K) are fine for a brochure site and tight for anything with real traffic.

You are on their hosting. PageSpeed Booster and the builder are tuned for 10Web's own stack, so moving a finished site to a cheaper host means giving up some of the speed tooling.

And it is still WordPress. If you cannot handle plugins, forms and on-page SEO, the AI will hand you a site you cannot finish. Compare that with a closed builder like Durable or a code-first tool like Lovable or v0 - each hides the mess in a different place, and picking the wrong one costs more than the subscription.

If you are weighing builders generally, the 2026 website-builder comparison and the Framer page are useful before you commit a client project.

Who should use it

Use 10Web if you already build WordPress sites and want to cut the layout grunt work, or if you want a white-label website product to resell without building a hosting stack. Skip it if you want a fully hands-off site with no WP knowledge, if you need uncapped traffic, or if you are selling high-end brand design where a generated layout is a liability rather than a shortcut. For small stores, pairing it with a retention tool like Klaviyo covers the part 10Web does not - keeping the customers the site wins.

❓ FAQ

How much does 10Web cost per website?

On the AI tiers a single site is $10-22.50/mo depending on credits and visitors. The reseller math only works on the agency plans: Agency Core is $80/mo for 20 websites, which is about $4 per site, and Agency Starter is $42.50/mo for 10 sites. Extra sites cost $4-10 each depending on your plan, and AI credits are a separate meter you will end up topping up.

Can I resell 10Web sites under my own brand?

Yes on Agency Core and Agency Pro. You get a branded dashboard, a branded builder, branded invoices and email notifications, custom pricing tiers and Stripe/PayPal billing, so clients see your product rather than 10Web. Watch the transaction fee - 7% on Agency Core, as low as 0% on Agency Pro - and remember clients are still on WordPress hosted by 10Web.

Do I need WordPress experience to use 10Web?

For a personal site, no - the builder produces a usable draft and the host handles maintenance. For client work, yes. You will be editing layouts, wiring forms, adding plugins and setting up SEO inside WordPress, and the AI does not do that part for you.

Is the AI-built site good enough to sell to a client?

As a first draft, yes; as a finished product, no. A lightly edited generated site is fine for a $300-800 small-business presence. Anything above that needs rewritten copy, working forms and proper SEO, which is exactly the work you are charging for. Treat the AI as the layout step, not the whole project.

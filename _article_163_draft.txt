## Dovetail: The Enterprise Default That Stopped Publishing Prices

Dovetail remains the most complete platform in the category. Centralize interviews, support tickets, surveys, and sales calls into one searchable library, let the AI cluster themes and surface sentiment, then query everything with an Ask-AI chat that cites its sources. On pure capability, nothing here is a step down.

The problem is what you pay and how you find out. Dovetail pricing in 2026 works like this: the Free plan costs $0 with no card required, includes one project, one channel, basic AI chat and automatic summaries, and caps transcription at roughly five to ten hours a month. It is genuinely useful for evaluating the interface. It is not usable for an ongoing research practice — most teams hit the one-project ceiling within weeks.

Everything past that is Enterprise, quoted by sales. Because there is no list price, the reliable benchmark is Vendr's dataset: 65 tracked deals, median $21,600 per year, range $10,800 to $61,200. Smaller teams report negotiating 5-15% below the initial quote, and multi-year deals take 15-25% off — with caveats covered below.

Three contract terms deserve attention before any Dovetail pricing conversation: billing is invoice-only in USD, there are no refunds, and canceling gives you a 30-day read-only export window before deletion. A tool that holds your entire research history with no self-serve exit is a procurement decision, not a software subscription.

**Use Dovetail if** you are a 20+ person organization with research-ops maturity, compliance requirements like SSO and audit logs, and a budget owner who can absorb a five-figure annual contract. **Skip it if** you want predictable published pricing — the Free-to-Enterprise gap has nothing in it.

## Marvin: The Full Qualitative Suite With a Five-Seat Floor

Marvin is the closest thing to a complete research operating system in this comparison. The toolbox covers an AI note-taker that joins your Zoom, Meet, or Teams calls, an AI-moderated interviewer that can run hundreds of simultaneous sessions, thematic and emotion analysis, support-ticket and sales-call analysis, and PII redaction. Where Dovetail leans toward being a repository you feed from other tools, Marvin tries to run the entire lifecycle — recruit, interview, analyze, report — in one place.

Marvin pricing, from its own published page: Free at $0 (2 full seats, 50 viewer seats, 5 file uploads per month), Essentials at $50 per user per month, and Standard at $100 per user per month — both with a five-user minimum and billed annually. That makes the floor $3,000 a year at Essentials and $6,000 a year at Standard. Enterprise adds HIPAA compliance, SSO, Salesforce integration, and a dedicated CSM at custom pricing.

Two caveats. Some comparison sites still list older Marvin figures — $19 individual and $39 team plans — which no longer match the company's own published structure; ignore stale aggregators and budget for the five-seat minimum. And the free tier's five uploads a month is evaluation-grade: if each interview runs 45-60 minutes, storage limits become the real upgrade trigger, not features.

Compliance is a genuine strength: SOC 2 certified, ISO 27001, GDPR compliant, HIPAA certified, and aligned to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. That stack usually costs extra elsewhere.

**Use Marvin if** you want interview moderation, analysis, and repository in one product and can commit five seats. **Skip it if** you have one or two researchers — paying for five seats to use two is money with no return.

## Looppanel: The Fastest Path From Recording to Insight

Looppanel solves one problem with unusual focus: the slog after the interview ends. Record through its Zoom, Meet, or Teams integration, get transcription that independent reviewers clock at around 97% accuracy on clear audio, receive time-stamped AI notes organized by your discussion-guide questions, then compare every participant's answer to a given question side by side. That question-based analysis view is the feature competitors have started copying — it collapses a five-recording review from days to hours.

Looppanel pricing starts at $30 per month for the Solo plan: five transcription hours, five file uploads, auto-tagging, AI notes, one-click video clips, and view-only collaborators. The Pro plan runs $350 per month ($3,900 billed annually) with thirty transcription hours per month, unlimited editors and viewers, a team workspace, and repository features. Enterprise adds custom SSO, PII redaction, translation from 17 languages, and data migration at custom rates. Aggregator sites list conflicting legacy configurations, so confirm the current structure on the vendor's site before you budget.

Where it falls short: Looppanel is an interview-analysis tool wearing a light repository coat. If your practice involves survey data, longitudinal studies across dozens of projects, or a company-wide insights hub with fine-grained access control, you will outgrow it toward Dovetail or Marvin.

**Use Looppanel if** your research is mostly interviews and you want the best effort-to-insight ratio at solo or small-team scale. **Skip it if** you need a permanent institutional repository — its storage and structure are not built for that job.

## Condens: The Bootstrapped European Alternative

Condens is a fifteen-person, profitable, Munich-based company competing against venture-funded rivals — and the difference shows up in two places. First, data posture: EU or US hosting at your choice, SOC 2 Type 2, GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA options, and a DPA baked into standard terms. European enterprise procurement clears it without the friction American SaaS often triggers. Second, the evidence workflow: split-screen tagging lets you highlight a transcript passage and connect it directly to a published finding, so any stakeholder can click from a stated insight to the exact moment a participant said it. Reviewers on G2 rate it 4.8/5, several specifically calling the workflow more efficient than Dovetail's.

Condens pricing is the stark one: Lite at €15 per contributor per month (annual; €16 month-to-month) with no viewer seats and a 25-participant cap, then Business at €500 per month including five contributors with unlimited viewers, then Enterprise at roughly €36,000 per year custom. There is nothing between €15 and €500 — a team growing from two researchers to four faces a roughly thirtyfold jump at exactly the moment budget scrutiny peaks.

The trade-off for bootstrapped pace: AI features ship more slowly than at funded competitors, the public API is still listed as coming soon, and quantitative analysis is weak by design — this is a qualitative home, not a mixed-methods platform.

**Use Condens if** you are a European team that values GDPR-native hosting, transparent vendor economics, and evidence-backed findings. **Skip it if** you are a mid-sized team stuck in the €15-to-€500 gap — the jump will hurt.

## Where the ROI Actually Shows Up

Deploy AI user research tools against a real workload and the value lands in three places. First, synthesis time. A typical moderated interview costs 90 minutes of researcher time plus 60-120 minutes of tagging, quote hunting, and slide building. Looppanel claims its workflow cuts analysis time by up to 70%; even at half that, a researcher running ten interviews a month reclaims 10-15 hours monthly. At a loaded cost of $60-100 per researcher hour, that is $7,000-18,000 a year of reclaimed capacity — which is why Looppanel pricing at $360-3,900 a year pays for itself fast for interview-heavy teams.

Second, reuse. A user research repository earns its keep the day someone asks "didn't we already study this?" and the answer is a cited search result instead of a shrug. Marvin's Ask AI and Condens's cross-project search both deliver this; the economic argument is that churn studies, persona work, and pricing research stop being repeated every 18 months at $15,000 a study. This is also where Dovetail's enterprise contract starts to look rational — at 50+ employees touching research, institutional memory has real line-of-business value.

Third, the lever nobody prices in: AI-moderated interviews. Marvin can run hundreds of concurrent AI-led sessions around the clock. If your constraint is moderation bandwidth rather than synthesis time, that capability alone can substitute for contract researcher spend that dwarfs any subscription here.

## The Hidden Costs Nobody Puts on the Pricing Page

- **Storage hours, not seats, trigger upgrades.** Marvin's file limits and Looppanel's transcription-hour caps both punish teams that record video-heavy sessions. Model your actual monthly interview volume before choosing a tier.
- **Multi-year discounts with escalators.** Dovetail's 15-25% multi-year discount frequently carries 3-7% annual uplift clauses. A 20% discount with 7% escalation is nearly break-even by year three.
- **Compliance is often an add-on.** HIPAA costs extra at Dovetail and Condens; Marvin includes it. Audit logs and SSO land on enterprise tiers everywhere except Looppanel's flat-rate Pro.
- **Viewer seats shape adoption.** A repository nobody outside the research team can read delivers none of the reuse ROI. Condens Lite charges for viewers; Looppanel Pro and Marvin do not.
- **Exit friction.** Dovetail's 30-day read-only window and no-refund policy mean your data hostage risk is real. Ask about export formats and migration support in every demo.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Dovetail still worth paying for in 2026?

For enterprises, often yes — the platform's analysis depth, integrations, and compliance tooling remain best-in-class, and Vendr data shows real discounts of 5-30% are negotiable. For small and mid-sized teams, no: with self-serve pricing gone, there is no rational way to buy two or three seats anymore. Dovetail pricing now only makes sense when a five-figure annual contract is already in your budget.

### What is the best Dovetail alternative for small teams?

Looppanel for interview-heavy work at $30 a month, Condens for repository-first workflows at €15 per contributor, or Marvin if you can commit five seats and want the full suite. All three of these Dovetail alternatives publish real prices, which after July 2026 is itself the differentiator.

### Dovetail vs Marvin: which one should a five-person team pick?

Marvin, almost always. Its five-seat Essentials plan costs $3,000 a year — roughly one-seventh of Dovetail's median contract — and includes SOC 2, ISO 27001, and HIPAA certifications that Dovetail sells as enterprise add-ons. Choose Dovetail over Marvin only when you need its broader integration ecosystem or already run a mature research-ops function.

### Can AI interview analysis replace manual tagging?

It replaces the first pass, not the judgment. In testing and user reports, AI suggestions surface most themes on clean audio but overproduce tags on messy sessions with crosstalk. Treat AI interview analysis as a synthesis accelerator: let it draft the structure, then spend your editing time on interpretation and evidence quality. Rigorous studies still need a human pruning pass.

### How much does a user research repository cost for a team of ten?

From $4,200 a year (Looppanel Pro annual, though its 30 transcription hours may pinch) through $6,000-12,000 (Marvin Standard at ten seats, or Condens Business plus extra contributors) to a median $21,600 with Dovetail. The honest spread is 5x for comparable seat counts, which is why modeling your transcription and storage volume matters more than any feature matrix.

## Bottom Line

The 2026 market for AI user research tools has split into two games. Marvin, Looppanel, and Condens compete on published prices, transparent tiers, and fast time-to-value — pick by team size and interview volume, and any of the three will treat you fairly. Dovetail has left that game for enterprise procurement, where its depth is real but its $21,600 median contract demands research-ops maturity to justify. My contrarian take for most readers: start with Looppanel Solo at $30 a month to feel what AI interview analysis actually saves you, move to Marvin when your team and methods outgrow solo tooling, and only take the Dovetail conversation seriously when your organization has five-figure research budgets and the governance needs to match. The tools are no longer the bottleneck; matching the pricing model to your reality is.
